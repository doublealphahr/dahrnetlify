Tariff Conflicts Intensify Recruitment Challenges in Europe and the US

Bloomberg Reports on the Difficulties of Major HR Service Companies Under the Impact of Tariffs

 

Source: Global Times Special Correspondent Zhen Xiang | Date: 2025-05-12 05:48

The impact of tariff conflicts on many areas of the international economy continues to manifest. Bloomberg's report on May 10 focused on the plight of major European and American human resources giants under the impact of tariffs. A review found that the profits of large human resources service companies such as Adecco Group, Randstad, and ManpowerGroup have continued to decline since the second quarter of last year. As the "Trump 2.0 era" arrives, for human resources companies that welcomed the new year with dismal performance, the possibility of a business recovery in the remaining time of this year seems unlikely.

The report stated that almost all recruitment agencies have warned that US trade policy has dragged down recruitment activities. The number of open job positions in the United States is currently declining, and the number of new jobs in the UK and Germany is also declining. Bloomberg noted that because the US government has recently taken a back-and-forth attitude on tariff issues, this has further increased the cost pressure on enterprises, and many companies have cut recruitment budgets due to concerns about uncertain business prospects. Adecco CEO Denis Machuel revealed that the pace of long-term position recruitment has slowed significantly, and many of the company's clients have entered a wait-and-see mode.

In terms of short-term job recruitment, European and American companies experienced the emergency hiring boom during the pandemic labor shortage and the subsequent cooling-off period. After this roller-coaster ride, it should now be recovering and stabilizing. However, senior analyst Stuart Gordon poured cold water on this, saying that after the US government initiated trade conflicts, the recovery process of short-term job recruitment has been completely disrupted.
