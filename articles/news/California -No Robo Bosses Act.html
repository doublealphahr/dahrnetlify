Senate Bill 947 (SB 947)

 

Core Prohibition:

 

1. Relying Solely on ADS is Prohibited

Employers are prohibited from relying solely on an Automated Decision System (ADS) when making disciplinary or termination decisions. If an employer primarily relies on ADS output, a human must corroborate the proposed decision by reviewing the underlying data and other relevant supporting information (e.g., supervisory evaluations, personnel files, witness interviews). A human reviewer must do more than “rubber-stamp” the result; they must exercise independent judgment.

2. Definition of ADS

ADS refers to any computational process or software derived from machine learning, statistical modeling, data analytics, or AI that generates output (such as a score, ranking, classification, or recommendation) used to assist or replace human discretionary decision-making in employment decisions. The law excludes common tools like spam filters, firewalls, antivirus software, calculators, and databases.

3. Post-Use Notice

When an employer primarily relies on an ADS to make a termination or disciplinary decision, it must provide the employee with a separate, stand-alone written notice. This notice must be simply worded and state:

  • That the employer primarily relied on an ADS to make the decision;

  • That a human reviewed and verified the ADS output;

  • Contact information for a human employer representative;

A statement that retaliation for exercising rights under the Act is prohibited.

4. Employee's Right of Requesting the Data

The affected employee may request a meaningful, objective description of the data that the ADS used to generate the output. The employer must first anonymize personal information about customers, other employees, or other individuals before providing this description.

5. Prohibited Uses

Employers may not use an ADS to:

  • Violate any labor, employment, occupational health and safety, or civil rights laws;

  • Infer an employee’s protected characteristics under the Fair Employment and Housing Act (FEHA);

  • Predict whether or when an employee will exercise legal rights and then take adverse action on that basis

    • 6. Enforcement and Penalties

    The Labor Commissioner and public prosecutors may enforce the law. Violations carry a civil penalty of up to $500 per violation, plus potential injunctive relief, punitive damages, attorneys’ fees, and costs. Once it is shown that an ADS was used, the burden of proof shifts to the employer to prove it did not primarily rely on the ADS or that it complied with the human-review and notice requirements.

    7. Collective Bargaining Exception

    A qualifying collective bargaining agreement may waive the law’s provisions if it satisfies specified requirements, including protections from algorithmic management.
