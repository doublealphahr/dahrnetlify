Employment Contracts, Minimum Wage, ZUS Social Security, Leave, Termination and EOR

2026 POLICY UPDATE

Minimum Wage and Minimum Hourly Rate Increased: From January 1, 2026, the minimum monthly wage under an employment contract rises to PLN 4,806, and the minimum hourly rate for specified civil law contracts rises to PLN 31.40. Employers should update contracts, quotations and payroll systems.

Scope of Recognized Seniority Expanded: New rules apply from January 1, 2026 to the public sector and from May 1, 2026 to private employers. Qualifying mandate contracts and self-employment periods may count toward seniority. Employers should collect evidence and recalculate annual leave, notice periods and related entitlements.

ZUS Annual Contribution Ceiling Updated: From January 1, 2026, the annual cumulative base ceiling for pension and disability insurance is PLN 282,600. Payroll systems should stop only the insurance types subject to the ceiling and continue to assess other insurance, funds and PPK.

Hiring employees in Poland means companies must not only determine gross salary but also simultaneously handle employment contracts, Social Insurance Institution (ZUS) registration, personal income tax (PIT) withholding, Employee Capital Plans (PPK), working time, leave, occupational health and safety (HSE) and termination procedures. Contract type, employee seniority, company size, union representation and protected status can all change the employer's actual obligations and exit costs.

This guide is intended for Chinese companies planning to recruit in Poland, configure payroll, calculate employer costs, or compare a local entity, Employer of Record (EOR) and payroll outsourcing. All amounts in this document are expressed in Polish zloty (PLN). Visa, work permits and notification pathways for foreign employees are not covered in detail here, but must be verified in parallel with the employment contract, position and work location.

2. Three Rules Most in Need of Update in 2026

01 | Minimum wage and minimum hourly rate for specified civil law contracts increased. From January 1, 2026, the national minimum monthly wage for a full-time employment contract is PLN 4,806, and the minimum hourly rate for specified mandate or service civil law contracts is PLN 31.40. Companies should update employment contract and civil law contract templates separately; the two standards cannot substitute for each other.

02 | Scope of seniority recognition expanded. The new rules apply from January 1, 2026 to the public sector and from May 1, 2026 to private employers. Qualifying mandate contracts, self-employment and similar periods may count toward statutory seniority after obtaining the relevant evidence. Employers should establish evidence collection, verification and retroactive adjustment processes, because changes in seniority may affect the 20/26-day annual leave tier, notice periods and other seniority-linked entitlements.

03 | Annual base ceiling for pension and disability insurance updated. The 2026 ceiling is PLN 282,600 per employee. Once the ceiling is reached, the employer and employee generally stop pension and disability contributions, but sickness, accident, health insurance, related funds and PPK cannot automatically stop together. The payroll system must be controlled independently by insurance type, rather than setting a "one-click stop all ZUS."

3. Polish Labor Law and Regulatory Framework

The core basis of the Polish employment relationship includes the Labor Code and supporting regulations on minimum wage, social insurance, personal income tax, Employee Capital Plans, occupational safety, personal data and collective redundancies. The EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) also directly affects recruitment, employee files, monitoring and cross-border data processing.

The contracting employer is responsible for signing contracts, registering ZUS and tax, paying wages, managing working time and leave, implementing HSE and lawfully terminating employment. Clients or overseas group managers may provide business facts and management input, but cannot replace the Polish employer in signing disciplinary actions, termination notices or other statutory HR documents.

4. Recruitment, Job Offer and Onboarding

Recruitment must not involve illegal discrimination based on gender, age, disability, race, religion, nationality, union membership or other protected characteristics. Candidate data should be limited to information permitted by labor law or genuinely justified by the position and law; criminal records, health information, biometrics and background checks require special review.

The job offer should state gross PLN salary, position, work location, contract type, full-time ratio, bonus, work model, proposed start date and preconditions, and should be consistent with the formal contract. For foreign employees, it is also necessary to verify whether the contract, position, work location and permit or notification content are consistent.

A fixed-term contract generally terminates on expiry due to the end of the term; if ordinarily terminated before expiry, the notice period is generally determined by seniority with the same employer, as with an open-ended contract. Companies should track the number of contracts, cumulative months and applicable exceptions, and must not use successive short contracts to cover permanent positions long-term.

A probationary contract is generally up to 3 months. If the subsequent fixed-term contract to be signed is shorter, the probationary period should generally also be shortened accordingly; extension is possible in statutory cases. Probation is not a "period without rights"; minimum wage, ZUS, annual leave accrual, sick leave, anti-discrimination and HSE obligations all apply.

Notice periods for a probationary contract are generally: 3 working days if the probationary period does not exceed 2 weeks; 1 week if more than 2 weeks but less than 3 months; 2 weeks if the probationary period is 3 months. Notice should be delivered in writing and the end date correctly calculated.

6. Wages, Minimum Wage and Gross-to-Net Calculation

From January 1, 2026, the minimum monthly wage for a full-time employment contract is PLN 4,806. Part-time employees are generally pro-rated by full-time ratio; for example, a 0.5 FTE basic reference is 4,806 × 50% = PLN 2,403, but should be recalculated if the actual full-time ratio or working hours change.

The minimum hourly rate for specified mandate or service civil law contracts is PLN 31.40. This standard is not a uniform hourly wage for employees under employment contracts. Using the monthly employment contract floor as a simple reference, if a month has 160 ordinary working hours, then 4,806 ÷ 160 = PLN 30.04/hour; if 168 hours, approximately PLN 28.61. An employment contract still guarantees the minimum wage on a monthly basis.

Minimum wage comparison may include wage components permitted by the statutory statistical scope, but generally excludes anniversary awards, retirement or disability separation pay, overtime pay, night shift allowances, seniority allowances and special working condition allowances. Companies cannot rely on overtime pay to fill the PLN 4,806 floor.

8. Statutory Holidays, Annual Leave and Other Statutory Leave

Annual leave for full-time employees depends on total statutory seniority: generally 20 days if under 10 years, 26 days at 10 years. Education and prior employment periods may count toward annual leave seniority; from 2026, qualifying mandate contracts, self-employment and similar periods may also count after evidence is submitted. Part-time annual leave is pro-rated by FTE; fractional days are generally rounded up, and actual use is deducted based on the employee's scheduled hours that day.

First employment example: A full-time employee starts their first job in life on April 1, 2026, with an annual entitlement of 20 days; each full month accrues 20 ÷ 12 = 1.6667 days, and by the end of September after 6 months approximately 10 days have accrued. Actual rounding should be handled according to statutory rules at the annual or final settlement.

10. Local Employees and Posted/Foreign Employees

Local employees are generally handled under ordinary employment contracts, ZUS, PIT and PPK rules. Posted or foreign employees, in addition to labor compliance, require assessment of nationality, residence, work permit or notification, social security coordination, tax residency and cross-border posting requirements. An employment contract itself does not automatically grant the right to work legally.

Verification Dimension Local Employees Posted/Foreign Employees

Chinese companies should set labor, immigration, tax and social security as parallel processes. When position, work location, working hours or employer changes, it is not enough to amend only the employment contract while ignoring permit, ZUS and tax consequences.

11. Remote Work, Data Privacy and Record Keeping

Remote work agreements or internal rules should specify work location, equipment, communication tools, costs or equivalent allowances, HSE, data protection, inspection rights and return-to-office arrangements. Cross-border remote work may also change tax, social security, immigration and permanent establishment risks and cannot be concluded solely on the basis of a Polish employment contract.

GDPR and Polish labor law limit the candidate and employee data employers may collect. Health, criminal records, biometrics, union membership and monitoring data generally require a stricter legal basis and access controls. Before Chinese headquarters or global HR systems access Polish employee data, a data flow, legal basis, cross-border transfer, retention period and security measures assessment should be completed.

12. Termination of Employment, Severance Pay and Offboarding Settlement

Ordinary termination of a fixed-term or open-ended employment contract by a Polish employer generally requires a written document stating a genuine, specific and verifiable reason, and indicating the right to seek remedy before a labor court. Where the employee is represented by a union, consultation may need to be completed first. Pregnancy, maternity or parental leave, sick leave or annual leave, and pre-retirement protection periods require special verification.

Scenario Notice or Procedure Main Settlement
Termination during probationNotice of 3 working days, 1 week or 2 weeks depending on probation lengthWages, pro-rata annual leave and other earned amounts
Ordinary employer termination outside probationFixed-term and open-ended generally 2 weeks, 1 month or 3 months based on seniority; written reason and union procedureWages, notice period, annual leave and possible severance
Employee resignationGenerally the same seniority-based notice period applies, no reason requiredWages and unused leave; no redundancy severance for ordinary resignation

Employer-side redundancySpecial individual or collective rules, consultation and notification apply when employer has at least 20 employees1, 2 or 3 months' wages based on seniority, capped at 15 times minimum wage

Ordinary employment contract notice periods are generally: 2 weeks for less than 6 months with the same employer; 1 month for 6 months but less than 3 years; 3 months for 3 years or more. Notice periods calculated in weeks generally end on Saturday; notice periods calculated in months end at the end of the month. The 2026 seniority rules may count qualifying historical periods toward current employer seniority, so recalculation is mandatory before issuing notice.

Statutory severance for employer-side redundancy is generally: 1 month's wage for less than 2 years with that employer; 2 months' wages for 2–8 years; 3 months' wages for more than 8 years. Severance generally does not exceed 15 times the minimum wage on the termination date.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS (FAQ)

What is the 2026 Polish minimum wage?
From January 1, 2026, the minimum monthly wage for a full-time employment contract is PLN 4,806; the minimum hourly rate for specified mandate or service civil law contracts is PLN 31.40. The two apply to different subjects and cannot substitute for each other.

Does the Polish minimum wage vary by city or industry?
The statutory Polish minimum wage is a national standard and does not automatically vary by city such as Warsaw, region or occupation. However, collective agreements, company policies or contracts may provide more favorable wages.

Is the Polish employer cost simply salary plus 20%?
No. Employers need to separately calculate pension 9.76%, disability 6.5%, accident insurance, FP/FS, FGŚP, PPK and other items, and also consider the annual pension and disability ceiling, paid leave, sick leave, overtime and potential severance.
