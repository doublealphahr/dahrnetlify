The judgment order for US employment compliance is generally: actual work location, employee or contractor status, exempt or non-exempt classification, wage structure, company size, state payroll tax and benefits, contract and company policy. Any variable change may trigger re-registration or reclassification.

2. Three Rules to Watch Most in 2026

2.1 California minimum wage and white-collar exemption threshold raised simultaneously

From January 1, 2026, California minimum wage rises to USD 16.90/hour. Because California general administrative, managerial, and professional exemption salary requirements are generally linked to the state minimum wage, the corresponding annual salary threshold rises to USD 70,304. Employers must also independently complete the duties test and cannot judge exemption based only on job title or annual salary.

2.2 Social Security annual wage base raised to USD 184,500

In 2026, the Social Security Tax rate remains 6.2% each for employer and employee, but the annual wage base is adjusted to USD 184,500. Employers should update payroll systems to avoid continued withholding after reaching the cap, and cannot carry forward the prior year's cap to 2026.

2.3 California SDI withholding rate adjusted to 1.3%

In 2026, the California State Disability Insurance (SDI) employee withholding rate is 1.3%, and all wages are included in the SDI contribution scope, with no general wage ceiling. California Employment Training Tax (ETT) remains 0.1% borne by employer, applicable to first USD 7,000 wages per employee.

3. USA Labor Law and Regulatory Framework

The US labor system is composed of federal, state, city or county, contract, and company policy together. Federal law generally provides minimum protection, and states and localities may grant more favorable standards to employees.

Level Main Rules Practical Application
Federal Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA), anti-discrimination, payroll tax, WARN, and benefit plan rules Provides national floor, does not exclude state law from providing higher protection
State Minimum wage, daily overtime, paid sick leave, wage deductions, final wages, workers' compensation, and unemployment insurance Generally centered on employee's actual work state
City or county Local minimum wage, paid leave, scheduling, and pay transparency Remote employee relocation may change applicable rules
Contract and policy Paid leave, bonus, commission, severance, notice period, and remote work Commitments made may become enforceable obligations

Main authorities include the US Department of Labor (DOL), Internal Revenue Service (IRS), Social Security Administration (SSA), and Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC). California scenarios also involve the Department of Industrial Relations and Division of Labor Standards Enforcement (DIR/DLSE) and Employment Development Department (EDD).

Companies must not use the state of registration as the sole basis for judgment. Unapproved cross-state relocation by employees may also generate new payroll tax, unemployment insurance, workers' compensation, minimum wage, and leave obligations.

4. Recruitment, Offer, and Onboarding

Recruitment criteria should be relevant to genuine position ability and job requirements. Age, disability, race, color, religion, sex, national origin, genetic information, and other protected factors must not become unlawful screening conditions. When posting remote positions, the allowed work state should be clarified to avoid discovering after onboarding that the employer does not yet have local registration or payroll capability.

Where background checks are involved, independent disclosure, candidate authorization, pre-adverse action notice, and final adverse action notice should be completed per the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) and state law. Background check authorization should not be hidden in a comprehensive onboarding form.

Stage Employer Action Recommended Records
Pre-recruitment Lock work state, city, job duties, wage range, and exemption judgment Job description, classification memo, and approval record
Offer State employer entity, work location, wage cycle, bonus or commission, benefits, PTO, and at-will clause Signed offer and policy acknowledgment
First three days of onboarding Complete I-9, W-4, state tax form, bank, and emergency contact information I-9 stored separately from ordinary personnel files
Payroll setup Establish federal and state accounts, working hours, overtime, leave, and deduction codes Payroll master data and deduction authorization
Insurance and benefits Configure workers' compensation, unemployment insurance, and applicable medical and retirement plans Policy, plan description, and employee election or waiver record

Employment eligibility verification and visa pathways for foreign candidates should be handled separately. I-9 is an onboarding eligibility verification tool and does not equal a visa application or employer sponsorship conclusion.

US federal law has no uniform statutory probation period. The 30-day, 60-day, or 90-day "onboarding observation period" used by companies is an internal management arrangement and does not delay minimum wage, overtime, anti-discrimination, workers' compensation, or applicable state paid sick leave rights.

Dismissal during probation still needs to verify at-will or contract terms, protected status or activity, state final wage requirements, and PTO and expense settlement. Probation extension should be documented with goals, period, and evaluation results, but must not imply that the employee automatically obtains fixed-term protection after probation ends.

6. Wages, Minimum Wage, and Gross-to-Net Calculation

Minimum wage should be checked in sequence at federal, employee's actual work state, city or county, and industry-specific standards, applying the highest applicable floor. Minimum wage and overtime are two independent tests; even if employee wage is above minimum wage, it does not mean overtime pay can be omitted.

Item 2026 Standard Usage Restriction
Federal minimum wage USD 7.25/hour Must not be used when state or local standard is higher
California minimum wage USD 16.90/hour Some cities and industries have higher standards
Federal white-collar exemption threshold USD 684/week Must also pass salary basis and duties test
California general white-collar exemption threshold USD 70,304/year Only preliminary salary test, does not replace duties test

California non-exempt employee converted by 40 hours/week, minimum monthly equivalent is approximately:

USD 16.90 × 40 hours × 52 weeks ÷ 12 months = USD 2,929.33

This amount is only minimum wage conversion, does not represent market wages in San Francisco, Los Angeles, etc., and excludes overtime, payroll tax, paid sick leave, workers' compensation, and other benefits.

Employee hourly wage USD 20, actual working 45 hours in a week, considering only federal weekly overtime, wage example is:

40 × USD 20 + 5 × USD 20 × 1.5 = USD 950

If the employee works in California, daily overtime, seventh consecutive workday overtime, or double-time should also be checked. Non-discretionary bonus and commission may also be included in the overtime regular rate.

States may stipulate different minimum pay frequency, payslip fields, and permitted deduction scope. Before launching payroll, a state-level matrix should be established listing wage cycle, payday, reimbursement deadline, equipment or uniform deduction, payslip fields, and final wage deadline.

Managers sending emails or instant messages outside normal working hours may generate compensable working hours for non-exempt employees. Companies may discipline unauthorized overtime, but generally should still pay for working hours the employer knew or should have known occurred.

The employee's wage form also does not determine classification. Salaried employees who do not pass the applicable exemption test still need to record working hours and receive overtime pay.

Federal holidays mainly apply to federal agencies. Federal law does not therefore automatically require private employers to provide paid holidays, nor uniformly require holiday work to be paid at additional multiplier. Employer obligations depend on state law, collective agreement, and company policy.

Federal law does not mandate ordinary paid annual leave. Companies may establish paid time off (PTO) themselves, but once promised, accrual, carryover, use, and departure settlement may be subject to state law. California generally treats accrued vacation or comprehensive PTO as earned wages, cannot be forfeited through "year-end reset," and generally should be settled at final wage rate upon departure.

California paid sick leave is generally at least 5 days or 40 hours, whichever is more favorable to the employee. Employees generally obtain coverage after 30 days of working for the employer, and may begin using it after 90 days of onboarding; local rules may provide higher entitlements.

Eligible employees under the Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) generally receive up to 12 work weeks of unpaid, job-protected leave; military caregiver leave may reach 26 weeks. Common eligibility conditions include 12 months of employment, at least 1,250 hours worked in the past 12 months, and employer having at least 50 employees within 75 miles. Federal law has no uniform paid parental leave; FMLA, Pregnant Workers Fairness Act, and state paid family leave may apply simultaneously.

10. Local Employees and Expatriate or Foreign Employees

US citizens, local residents, and foreign employees with legal work authorization are all subject to applicable minimum wage, overtime, anti-discrimination, workers' compensation, and state labor protection. Employers must not lower statutory treatment due to nationality or immigration background, nor require specific documents beyond the permitted scope in I-9 verification.

Foreign employee onboarding should simultaneously verify: whether current status allows working for the employer, whether there are work location or position restrictions, whether employer change requires prior application, and the impact of termination on status and grace period. Employment contract, I-9, and payroll system cannot replace immigration law review.

Social Security tax and Medicare tax generally also apply to non-resident alien wages, but specific visa categories or social security agreements may provide exceptions. There is no comprehensive social security agreement between China and the US that generally eliminates dual social security contributions, so posting arrangements should be assessed case by case for US payroll tax and China social insurance responsibility.

Employer of Record (EOR) does not automatically apply to all visa pathways. Where foreign employees are involved, statutory employer, visa application entity, actual management relationship, and post-termination immigration impact should be confirmed separately.

11. Remote Work, Data Privacy, and Employee Records

Remote employees generally generate minimum wage, payroll tax, unemployment insurance, workers' compensation, paid leave, and final wage obligations in their actual work state. Employee state relocation cannot be handled only by changing address in the HR system; companies should first confirm whether new state registration, payroll system, workers' compensation, and benefit network are ready.

Remote work agreements should clarify allowed work location, working hours records, overtime approval, equipment and expense reimbursement, information security, monitoring method, travel, and relocation approval. For non-exempt employees, a policy prohibiting unauthorized overtime cannot replace paying occurred working hours.

Background checks, biometrics, health information, and employee monitoring may be simultaneously subject to federal and state privacy rules. California employee personal information should also be included in California Consumer Privacy Act and Privacy Rights Act (CCPA/CPRA) governance.

I-9, medical data, background checks, and ordinary personnel files should be stored separately with least-privilege access. Recommended records include employee classification memo, wage and hour records, deduction authorization, leave, performance, reasonable accommodation, investigation, discipline, and termination materials.

12. Termination, Compensation, and Final Settlement

At-will generally means employer or employee may end the employment relationship without a fixed contract term, but does not mean employer may terminate for unlawful reasons, nor ignore wages, PTO, commission, expense reimbursement, benefit continuation, or state final wage deadlines.

Termination Scenario Procedure Focus Settlement Focus
Employer termination during probation Still need to verify anti-discrimination, retaliation, contract, and state law Earned wages, expenses, PTO, and benefits
Ordinary at-will termination Reason must be lawful, and investigation and handling consistency maintained Wages, PTO, commission, contractual severance, and benefit notice
Employee resignation No national uniform notice period in most at-will scenarios Final hours, expenses, PTO, and equipment
Fixed-term expiry End per contract, and verify continued employment and discrimination or retaliation risk Wages, earned bonus, commission, and PTO
Fixed-term early termination Review early termination clause and state contract law May generate remaining term damages
Mass layoff or plant closing Federal WARN and state mini-WARN may require advance notice Notice, benefits, selection criteria, and severance policy
Serious misconduct First investigate, give opportunity to respond, and verify consistency Must not withhold already earned wages
Mutual agreement termination Written clarification of termination date, payment, waiver, and revocation period Wages, PTO, commission, severance, and benefit continuation

The US has no national uniform ordinary dismissal notice period and statutory severance. Federal WARN generally requires employers with 100 or more employees meeting thresholds to provide 60 days written notice before qualifying plant closing or mass layoff; state mini-WARN may be stricter.

California common rule for final wages is: employer termination generally requires immediate payment of all earned wages and vested vacation or PTO; employee resignation with at least 72 hours notice generally requires payment on last working day, and without 72 hours notice generally within 72 hours after resignation.

Assume a California employee with annual salary USD 120,000, ordinary termination, earned wages this month USD 10,000, remaining 5 days accrued annual leave, no contractual severance, no WARN and commission:

Item Calculation Example Amount
Current wage Earned USD 10,000.00
Unused annual leave 120,000 ÷ 260 × 5 USD 2,307.69
Pre-tax departure total Wage plus annual leave USD 12,307.69

This example is not a fixed severance formula. Actual settlement still needs recalculation based on termination date, bonus or commission plan, expense reimbursement, tax, benefits, and local rules.

When choosing EOR or PEO, the focus is not whether the supplier claims to "cover the US," but whether it has the necessary employer, payroll tax, unemployment insurance, workers' compensation, and state registration in the employee's actual work state, and can execute local wage, leave, benefit, and final wage rules.

Client directly deciding EOR employee scheduling, performance, discipline, and dismissal may increase co-employment risk. Client should raise management requirements to the statutory employer through agreed process, and the statutory employer completes investigation, documentation, and termination decision.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

What is the 2026 US federal minimum wage?
The 2026 federal minimum wage remains USD 7.25/hour, but when state, city, or industry standards are higher, the higher standard must be applied. Companies cannot directly use the federal standard for all US quotations.

What is the regular employer payroll tax cost in the US?
At the federal level, employers generally bear 6.2% Social Security tax, 1.45% Medicare tax, and FUTA. Companies may also bear state unemployment insurance, workers' compensation, state payroll tax, medical insurance, and other benefits, so there is no fixed employer cost ratio applicable to all US states.

Do salaried employees not need overtime pay?
No. Employees must simultaneously pass applicable salary basis, salary level, and duties tests to obtain white-collar overtime exemption. Job title or fixed monthly salary itself cannot determine exemption.

Does the US mandate paid annual leave and public holidays?
Federal law does not uniformly require private employers to provide ordinary paid annual leave or paid federal holidays, but state law, local rules, collective agreement, and company policy may create obligations. Promised PTO may also be treated as earned wages in some states.

Is there a uniform statutory probation period in the US?
No. 30-day, 60-day, or 90-day probation periods are generally only company management arrangements and do not delay minimum wage, overtime, anti-discrimination, workers' compensation, or state sick leave rights already applicable to employees.

Does at-will mean same-day, zero-cost dismissal?
No. Dismissal reason must be lawful, and employer still needs to verify contract, anti-discrimination and anti-retaliation rules, WARN, state final wage, PTO, commission, expense reimbursement, and benefit notice.

When must final wages be paid after dismissing a California employee?
When employer initiates termination, California generally requires immediate payment of all earned wages and vested vacation or PTO upon termination. Specific calculation should also include applicable commission, expenses, and other earned amounts.

Can a company hire employees without a US entity?
Companies may evaluate Employer of Record (EOR), but should confirm the service provider's registration, payroll tax, unemployment insurance, workers' compensation, benefits, and termination execution capability in the employee's actual work state. EOR also cannot automatically resolve all visa or co-employment risks.
