Denmark has no national uniform statutory minimum wage applicable to all employees; wages, pensions, overtime, public holidays, and some termination benefits depend heavily on industry collective agreements and employment contracts. Before issuing an offer, Chinese companies should first confirm whether the employee is covered by a collective agreement, whether protected by the Salaried Employees Act (Funktionærloven), which holiday payment model applies, and the applicable pension and insurance scheme.

This guide is intended for Chinese company HR, finance, legal, and overseas business teams that recruit, sign contracts, run payroll, calculate employer costs, use Employer of Record (EOR) services, or handle employee departure in Denmark. The content is organized per 2026 rules; specific collective agreements, industry codes, pensions, insurance, work rights, and high-risk termination still need to be confirmed case by case.

2. Three Rules to Watch Most in 2026

Labour market contribution exemption for under-18s

From 2026, the 8% labour market contribution (AM-bidrag) is only levied from the calendar year in which the employee turns 18. Employers should update payroll parameters by date of birth, avoiding continued withholding for young employees not yet entering the applicable year.

ATP and employer fixed contribution updated

The 2026 full-time employee ATP total is DKK 891/quarter, of which employer bears DKK 594 and employee bears DKK 297; AUB, Barsel.dk, and Finansieringsbidrag are also settled per current fixed amounts. Companies should determine actual amount based on employee working hours bracket, industry fund, and cross-border social security coverage.

No national minimum wage, collective agreement must be identified in advance

Denmark still has no national uniform statutory minimum wage in 2026. Employers must identify applicable industry collective agreement, position wage group, pension, free choice account, shift and overtime surcharge before quotation, and cannot treat market example wage as national statutory floor.

3. Denmark Labor Law and Regulatory Framework

Danish labor relations are jointly regulated by labor law, collective agreements, industry rules, the Salaried Employees Act, holiday system, tax and social security, occupational safety, equal treatment, and data protection. Collective agreements play an important role in Danish employment, and may stipulate wages, pensions, working hours, overtime, public holidays, and termination procedures.

The contract employer bears signing, registration, payroll, tax withholding, employer contributions, insurance, leave, occupational health and safety, discipline, and termination responsibility. The client manager may set business goals, but must not bypass the contract employer to directly change wages, refuse leave, discipline, or dismiss EOR employees.

The judgment order should be: confirm actual work location and contract employer, determine employee and contract type, identify collective agreement or industry rules, then configure wages, pension, insurance, tax, working hours, and leave.

4. Recruitment, Offer, and Onboarding

Job descriptions should clarify actual duties, work location, reporting line, working hours, shifts, travel, language, and objective qualifications, and must not improperly screen based on age, gender, nationality, family status, or other protected factors.

The Offer should separately state basic gross wage, fixed allowances, bonus or commission, pension, overtime arrangement, work location, remote ratio, probation, notice period, and expected onboarding date. Recruitment platform wages are not statutory minimum wage, and foreign employee work rights should be verified separately.

5. Employment Contracts, Contract Types, and Probation

Indefinite-term contracts are the common form for long-term continuous positions. Fixed-term contracts should state objective term, task, and ending condition; continuous renewal without objective reason increases the risk of conversion to indefinite-term or disadvantageous treatment disputes. Part-time contracts should state weekly working hours and change method.

The contract should at least include parties, start date, work location, duties, wage and payment cycle, working hours, holiday, probation, notice period, applicable collective agreement, pension, bonus or commission, confidentiality, IP, data processing, and equipment arrangement.

Employees covered by the Salaried Employees Act may agree in writing on probation of maximum 3 months. Employer generally needs to give 14 days notice, and the employment relationship should end within probation. Employee resignation during probation generally requires no notice, unless the contract validly provides otherwise. Other employees should be judged per collective agreement and contract.

Independent contractor status depends on actual control, organizational integration, economic dependence, substitution right, and risk bearing. When an individual has fixed scheduling, uses company systems, accepts daily direction, and mainly serves a single client, misclassification review should be conducted.

6. Wages, Minimum Wage, and Gross-to-Net Calculation

Denmark has no national uniform statutory minimum wage. The wage judgment order is: whether the employer is bound by collective agreement, which wage group the position belongs to, whether basic wage meets the agreement, whether pension and free choice account are complete, and whether shift and overtime surcharges are correct.

Using a Copenhagen software sales manager with monthly gross wage DKK 50,000 as an example, this amount is only for cost illustration and does not represent statutory or market minimum wage. Commission should clarify assessment period, acquisition time, approval, refund, payment date, and departure handling.

Payslips should separately state normal wage, overtime or night shift, fixed allowances, expense reimbursement, bonus or commission, holiday payment, employee deductions, and employer items. Part-time employee monthly salary may first be calculated based on full-time standard and working hours ratio, then reviewed by actual hours.

Employee actual net wage should be calculated based on personal electronic tax card (eSkattekort), deductions, pension, and 8% labour market contribution. Companies cannot use a uniform income tax rate to promise net wage to employees.

7. Working Hours, Overtime, and Records

The contract should clarify daily and weekly working hours. Most industry collective agreements adopt 37-hour workweek; under the EU working time framework, average weekly working hours generally must not exceed 48 hours, including overtime, and daily and weekly rest requirements should be implemented.

Whether overtime is paid in cash, arranged as compensatory rest, or partly included in fixed salary depends on applicable collective agreement and contract. "Fixed salary includes unlimited overtime" cannot cancel mandatory rest, maximum working hours, and minimum wage surcharge.

Employers should record scheduling, actual start and end time, rest, overtime approval, and compensation. Whether remote work, travel, training, on-call, and cross-time-zone meetings count as working hours should be judged based on whether the employee is controlled by the employer; manager title does not automatically generate working hours exemption.

8. Public Holidays, Annual Leave, and Other Statutory Leave

Employees generally accrue 2.08 days annual leave per month, and a full leave year is 5 weeks or 25 days. For less than a full month, generally 0.07 days per qualifying employment day. The leave year generally runs from September 1 to August 31 of the following year, with use window extended to December 31 thereafter.

Part-time employees still enjoy full holiday weeks; taking a full week generally consumes one week of leave, and statutory rest opportunity cannot be simply reduced by their weekly scheduling days. Departure is settled per FerieKonto or applicable paid leave scheme.

Constitution Day June 5, Labour Day May 1, December 24, and December 31 are not uniformly statutory paid rest days for all private employees, and are usually determined by collective agreement or company policy. Great Prayer Day is no longer a statutory holiday; public holiday work treatment is handled per collective agreement or contract.

Employees covered by the Salaried Employees Act generally enjoy contract wage continuation during sick leave. Other employees who do not enjoy full pay, when meeting 8 weeks and 74 hours conditions, employer generally bears the first 30 calendar days sick pay; 2026 ceiling is DKK 5,085/week or DKK 137.43/hour, thereafter generally handled by municipality.

Parental leave and government benefits need separate judgment; whether full pay and employer top-up depends on collective agreement, contract, or company policy. Employer should file through Virk and apply for applicable reimbursement.

10. Local Employees and Foreign Employees

Local employees, EU or EEA employees, and other foreign employees in principle enjoy the same minimum labor law protection, but work rights, tax residence status, and social security coverage may differ. Companies should verify actual work location, permit status, tax card, CPR number, and social security coverage before onboarding.

When cross-border employees hold valid A1 certificate or other coverage proof, Danish ATP or part of social security obligations may change. EOR arrangement cannot automatically grant work permit, nor eliminate cross-border tax, permanent establishment, or co-employment risk.

Foreign employee departure may affect residence or work permit status; the contract employer should coordinate labor law termination and immigration compliance, but cannot replace lawful termination procedure with permit risk.

11. Remote Work, Data Privacy, and Employee Records

Remote work agreements should state main work location, on-site frequency, equipment and expenses, working hours records, occupational health and safety, information security, and cross-border approval. Before an employee works long-term from another country, tax residence, permanent establishment, social security, A1 certificate, data transfer, and work rights should be assessed.

Employee files should follow lawful basis, necessity, access control, and retention period. Health, discipline, wage, and identity data should have restricted access; clients should not retain unnecessary EOR employee sensitive data for long. After departure, system access should be promptly closed and equipment and data handover completed.

Employers should keep contract and annex, tax and social security receipt, payslips, working hours, leave, bonus, performance, warnings, accidents, insurance, and termination materials to support payroll calculation and dispute handling.

Before termination, confirm contract type, probation status, whether employee is covered by the Salaried Employees Act or collective agreement, genuine reason, seniority, warning or performance records, protected status, notice period, unused leave, and bonus commission.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Is there a national uniform minimum wage in Denmark?
No. Wages are mainly determined by applicable collective agreement and employment contract. Companies cannot treat recruitment platform data, visa wage threshold, or a position example as national statutory minimum wage.

How many annual leave days do Danish employees have per year?
Employees generally accrue 2.08 days per month, and a full year is 5 weeks or 25 days. For less than a full month, generally 0.07 days per qualifying employment day.

What is the usual holiday allowance?
Employees under the holiday allowance model generally receive holiday allowance at 12.5% of qualifying wage; employees enjoying paid leave generally receive normal wage and at least 1% holiday supplement. The two models cannot be double-counted.

What is the 2026 labour market contribution?
Labour market contribution is generally 8% of employee wage, withheld and paid by employer. From 2026, it is only levied from the calendar year in which the employee turns 18.

Does the Danish employer only bear ATP?
No. In addition to ATP, the employer may also bear AUB, Barsel.dk, Finansieringsbidrag, AES, work accident insurance, pension, collective agreement, and holiday costs.

What is the maximum probation period for salaried employees?
Written agreement generally maximum 3 months. Employer termination generally requires 14 days notice, and the employment relationship must end before probation expiry.

Is there a uniform statutory severance in Denmark?
Employees covered by the Salaried Employees Act generally receive 1 month wage at 12 years service and 3 months wage at 17 years service; other employees should be judged per collective agreement and contract.

Can a company hire employees through EOR without a Danish entity?
Companies may evaluate a compliant Employer of Record (EOR) arrangement, but must confirm contract employer, collective agreement, payroll registration, pension, insurance, work rights, actual management, and termination responsibility case by case.
