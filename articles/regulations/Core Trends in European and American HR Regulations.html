EMPLOYMENT GUIDE

Core Trends in European and American HR Regulations 2024–2026

Forced Labor Regulation, Pay Transparency, Employment Rights Reform, AI Employment Oversight, and Platform Worker Protection

 

The core trends in European and American HR regulations include: comprehensive upgrading of forced labor regulation (such as EU FLR 2024/3015 and US UFLPA), mandatory pay transparency disclosure (EU 2023/970), the UK Employment Rights Bill 2025 strengthening employee protection, and accelerated implementation of AI employment oversight (EU AI Act). These regulations collectively push enterprises to establish stricter compliance systems in human rights due diligence, pay management, platform employment, and AI risk control.

1. Overall Trends in European and American HR Regulations (2024–2026)

European and American HR regulations show four major trends:

1.1 Comprehensive Strengthening of Supply Chain Human Rights and Forced Labor Regulation

  • US UFLPA (Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act): Prohibits Xinjiang-related products from entering the US market through a "rebuttable presumption," requiring enterprises to provide "clear and convincing evidence" that no forced labor is involved.

  • US Section 301 Investigation (2026): Makes "whether forced labor products are effectively prohibited" a national-level review subject, forming triple pressure of tariffs, border seizures, and institutional review.

  • EU FLR (Forced Labour Regulation, Regulation (EU) 2024/3015): Fully implemented in 2027, covering global product supply chains; forced labor at any link triggers a ban.

  • EU CSDDD (Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive): Requires large enterprises to conduct global supply chain human rights and environmental due diligence.

Impact: Enterprises need to establish full-chain traceability systems, risk assessment mechanisms, and evidence retention systems.

2. Key EU HR Regulations

2.1 Pay Transparency Directive (Directive (EU) 2023/970)

  • June 7, 2026 is the deadline for member state transposition; many countries' delays will face infringement procedures.

  • Enterprises with 100 or more employees must disclose gender pay gaps.

  • A gap of 5% without reasonable explanation requires mandatory remediation.

  • Salary ranges must be disclosed during recruitment, and candidates' salary history must not be asked.

2.2 AI Employment Oversight (EU AI Act)

  • Compliance deadline for HR-related high-risk AI systems is postponed to 2027–2028.

  • Germany's BMAS has published HR-AI compliance guidelines, with enforcement strengthened from 2026.

2.3 Member State Local Regulations

  • Germany LkSG (Supply Chain Due Diligence Act): Applicability threshold lowered to 1,000 employees, requiring annual risk analysis.

  • Belgium: Advancing draft legislation to cap dismissal notice periods at 52 weeks.

3. Key UK HR Regulations

Employment Rights Bill 2025 (core provisions effective April 6, 2026)

  • Sick pay waiting period abolished; available from the first day of employment.

  • Paternity leave and parental leave no longer have service length requirements.

  • Penalties for collective redundancy consultation procedure violations doubled.

  • Sexual harassment prevention obligations strengthened.

From October 2026: the sexual harassment "all reasonable steps" obligation fully takes effect.

The EHRC will strengthen enforcement, and employers that fail to fulfill their obligations will be publicly named.

4. Key US HR Regulations

4.1 Federal Level

  • White-collar exemption salary threshold: Raised to USD 1,120 per week from July 1, 2026.

  • AI employment oversight: State-level legislation accelerating (such as Illinois and Connecticut).

4.2 State-Level Regulations

  • Virginia: Comprehensive reform including paid sick leave, pay range disclosure, and restrictions on non-compete agreements.

  • Multiple states: Minimum wage increases (Los Angeles USD 18.42 per hour, etc.).

5. International Level: New ILO Standards

Decent Work in the Platform Economy Convention (Convention No. 193, 2026)

  • Clarifies criteria for determining platform worker status: algorithmic control, task allocation, etc. may constitute an employment relationship.

  • Requires member states to expand social security coverage and provide algorithmic appeal mechanisms.

6. Compliance Impact on Enterprises and Recommendations

6.1 Supply Chain Compliance

  • Establish a full-chain traceability system (raw materials → production → transportation).

  • Establish a forced labor risk database and annual due diligence mechanism.

6.2 Pay Management

  • Establish a gender pay gap monitoring system.

  • Standardize recruitment processes and disclose salary ranges.

6.3 AI Employment Compliance

  • Conduct risk classification and documentation for AI systems used in recruitment, performance, and scheduling.

6.4 Cross-Border Posting and Data Export

  • The UK, EU, and US have all strengthened regulation of cross-border personnel and data flows.

  • China's 2026 Foreign Investment Regulations also incorporate personnel posting into export control considerations.

Summary

European and American HR regulations are expanding from traditional labor relations management to broader areas such as supply chain human rights, pay transparency, AI employment, and platform worker protection. Enterprises need to build systematic, cross-regional HR compliance frameworks to respond to regulatory upgrades.
