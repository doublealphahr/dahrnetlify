Minimum Wage, Social Security, 13th Month Pay, Leave and Termination Compliance

2026 POLICY UPDATE

NCR Minimum Wage Increase: The minimum daily wage for non-agricultural employees in NCR increased from ₱695 to ₱755 effective July 25, 2026.

National Holiday Calendar Confirmed: 2026 regular holidays, special dates and two Islamic holidays have been officially confirmed.

Social Security Parameters Effective for the Full Year: SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG continue to be calculated per person according to their respective rates, contribution bases and ceilings.

As more Chinese companies establish customer service centers, shared service teams, technical positions and regional operations functions in the Philippines, local employment management is no longer just about signing contracts and paying salaries monthly. Companies also need to simultaneously handle regional minimum wages, employee status, working hours and night shifts, 13th Month Pay, statutory leave, social security, payroll tax and termination procedures.

The Philippines does not have a nationwide uniform minimum wage. Regional wage and productivity boards issue wage orders by region, industry, enterprise size and effectivity batch. Whether an employee is a regular employee, probationary employee, project employee, managerial employee or genuine independent contractor also affects working hours benefits, 13th month pay, service incentive leave and termination procedures. Companies cannot simply copy the Metro Manila wage standard to Cebu, Davao or other regions.

This guide is intended for Chinese companies planning to hire employees in the Philippines, as well as HR, legal, finance, payroll and overseas business teams. It systematically outlines the labor contracts, probationary periods, regional minimum wages, working hours and holidays, SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, 13th month pay, dismissal and redundancy, and Employer of Record (EOR) rules that need to be understood in 2026, helping companies complete a first round of compliance assessment before recruitment, quotation, payroll and employee exit.

2. Three Rules to Watch Most in 2026

2.1 Metro Manila Minimum Wage Increased Effective July 25, 2026

Metro Manila Wage Order NCR-27 was issued on June 23, 2026, published on July 9, and implemented the first tranche of adjustment on July 25.

Employee Category Before July 20, 2026 From July 25, 2026 From January 20, 2027
Non-agricultural employees₱695/day₱755/day₱780/day
Agricultural employees₱658/day₱718/day₱743/day
Employees of service or retail establishments with no more than 15 workers₱658/day₱718/day₱743/day
Employees of manufacturing establishments with fewer than 10 workers₱658/day₱718/day₱743/day

This wage order applies only to cities covered by Metro Manila and Pateros, and cannot be used for Cebu, Davao or other regions. Payroll periods crossing July 25 must be calculated separately before and after the effectivity date, and related overtime pay, holiday pay and other daily wage-based items must also be adjusted accordingly.

Metro Manila domestic workers are covered separately by NCR-DW-06, with a minimum monthly wage of ₱7,800 effective February 7, 2026.

2.2 2026 National Holiday Calendar and Two Islamic Holidays Confirmed

Proclamation No. 1006 established the 2026 national regular holidays, special non-working days and special working days. Proclamations No. 1189 and No. 1264 respectively confirmed:

  • March 20, 2026, Eid'l Fitr, as a national regular holiday;

  • May 27, 2026, Eid'l Adha, as a national regular holiday.

Different holiday types apply different pay rules:

4.3 Onboarding Closure

Before onboarding, verify the employee's identity, address, bank account, tax identification number, SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG numbers, and obtain consent for background checks.

On the onboarding date, sign the employment contract, job description, salary structure, probationary standards, employee handbook, data privacy notice and equipment list. Before the first payroll, recheck the work location wage order, social security contribution bracket, withholding tax table, attendance records and 13th month pay accrual.

The probationary period is not a period during which an employee can be dismissed at zero cost and without procedure. Even if the employee fails to meet regularization standards, the employer must still settle final pay, pro-rata 13th month pay, accrued leave and government contributions.

7. Wages, Regional Minimum Wage and Payment

The Philippines does not have a nationwide uniform minimum wage. Companies should verify in the following order:

  1. Confirm the region and city where the employee actually works;

  2. Confirm the employee's industry and enterprise size;

  3. Check the latest wage order on the NWPC or corresponding RTWPB page;

  4. Record the wage order issuance date, effectivity date and phased implementation dates;

  5. Check wages, overtime, holiday pay and other daily wage-related items;

  6. Calculate payroll periods crossing the effectivity date separately.

Wages should in principle be paid at least once every two weeks or twice a month, and the interval between two wage payments should generally not exceed 16 days.

The payslip should separately itemize basic salary, allowances, overtime pay, night shift differential, holiday or rest day premium, SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, withholding income tax and net pay.

13th Month Pay

The minimum amount of 13th month pay is:

13th Month Pay = Total basic salary actually earned by the employee during the year ÷ 12.

Covered regular employees who have served at least 1 month in the year, even if probationary, project, fixed-term, or who resigned or were terminated during the year, are generally entitled to pro-rata 13th month pay. 13th month pay should be paid no later than December 24.

8. Working Hours, Rest, Night Shift and Overtime

Standard working hours are generally 8 hours per day. Employees are generally entitled to at least 24 consecutive hours of rest after every 6 consecutive ordinary working days. The normal meal period is generally not less than 60 minutes and is generally not counted as working time.

Covered employees working from 22:00 to 06:00 the next day generally enjoy a night shift differential of at least 10%.

Managerial employees, field personnel and certain other personnel may not be covered by some working hours benefits. Whether an employee falls under an excluded category should be determined based on actual duties, not job title alone.

10.3 Local Employees and Foreign Employees

In addition to general employment contract, wage, working hours and leave rules, foreign employees also require pre-onboarding verification of visa, work permit, employment permit conditions and validity period.

Whether SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG and payroll tax apply to a specific foreign employee may be affected by residence status, registration status, international agreements and competent authority rules. Employers must not assume full exemption simply because the employee is a foreign national, nor directly copy local employee withholding parameters without verification.

11. Remote Work, Data Privacy and Occupational Safety

The remote work agreement should specify:

  • Actual work location;

  • Working hours and contactable periods;

  • Equipment, internet and electricity costs;

  • Data security and access rights;

  • Incident reporting procedures;

  • On-site attendance arrangements;

  • Approval requirements for changing work location.

Remote workers with long-term fixed hours, exclusive work, use of company equipment and direct performance management are generally closer to employees and should not be deemed freelancers merely because they work online.

Personal data processing should have privacy notices, clear purposes and access controls. Tax numbers, bank, health and disciplinary information should only be accessible to necessary personnel. Occupational safety obligations also apply to remote and client-site work.

In computing years of service, a fraction of less than 6 months is generally not counted as a full year; a fraction of at least 6 months is generally counted as 1 year.

"Half month's pay" may include 15 days' wages, one-twelfth of 13th month pay and the cash value of not more than 5 days of SIL, and cannot simply be calculated as monthly salary divided by 2.

12.3 Timing of Final Pay

Final pay should generally be paid within 30 days after the employee's separation or termination, unless company policy, agreement or collective bargaining agreement provides a more favorable period. When an employee requests a certificate of employment, it should generally be provided within 3 days.

Final pay may include:

  • Last salary;

  • Unpaid overtime, night shift and holiday pay;

  • Pro-rata 13th month pay;

  • Commutable SIL or company leave;

  • Approved reimbursements;

  • Applicable authorized cause separation pay;

  • Tax adjustments;

  • Lawful deductions.

13. Entity, Employer of Record Services and Payroll Operations Options

Employer of Record (EOR) or Professional Employer Organization (PEO) arrangements in the Philippines can be assessed, but must be confirmed case by case.

Need to verify:

  • Whether the local entity genuinely assumes the employment contract, payroll and government contributions;

  • Who is responsible for disciplinary action and termination decisions;

  • Whether the client only manages business outcomes;

  • Whether the arrangement may constitute labor-only contracting;

  • Whether the service provider has capital, tools, independent management and genuine business;

  • Whether client-site or long-term BPO positions have a clear responsibility matrix.

Adopting an EOR does not mean automatically avoiding employment relationships, work permits or termination risks. sailglobal can assess options based on position, work location, employing entity, supplier capabilities and client management approach, but cannot promise fixed costs or dispute-free termination without verification.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS (FAQ)

Does the Philippines have a nationwide uniform minimum wage?
No. Minimum wages are issued by regional wage and productivity boards based on work region, industry and enterprise size. The Metro Manila standard cannot be directly applied to Cebu, Davao or other regions.

What is the 2026 Metro Manila non-agricultural minimum wage?
Effective July 25, 2026, the minimum daily wage for non-agricultural employees is ₱755. The second tranche will increase it to ₱780 on January 20, 2027.

Can 13th month pay be omitted in the Philippines?
For covered regular employees who have served at least 1 month in the year, 13th month pay is generally a statutory obligation, not a bonus determined by company profitability.

Is an employee entitled to service incentive leave before completing one year?
The statutory 5-day service incentive leave generally accrues after 1 year of service. Companies may provide more favorable pro-rata accrual or advance policies.

How long can a probationary period generally be set?
The probationary period is generally no more than 6 months, and reasonable regularization standards must be communicated to the employee at onboarding.

Can an employer dismiss a regular employee after giving 30 days' notice?
Dismissal cannot be based on notice period alone. The employer must still have a statutory just cause or authorized cause and follow the corresponding procedure.

Can Philippine employer costs be uniformly increased by a single percentage?
No. SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG use different contribution bases and ceilings, and 13th month pay, night shift, overtime, holiday pay and potential separation pay must also be considered.

Can Chinese companies hire employees in the Philippines through an EOR?
It can be assessed, but local employer responsibility, client control, contracting rules, social security, tax and work permits need to be verified case by case.
