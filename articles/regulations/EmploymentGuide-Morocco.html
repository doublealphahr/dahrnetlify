Minimum Wage, CNSS Social Security, Working Hours, Leave and Termination Compliance

2026 POLICY UPDATE

Non-agricultural SMIG Increased to MAD 17.92/hour
Effective from January 1, 2026. Employers should update contracts, minimum wage verification, mid-month payroll and overtime parameters.

Agricultural SMAG Increased to MAD 97.44/day
Effective from April 1, 2026. Agricultural employers should update wages and cost budgets according to actual working days.

October 31 Unity Day Included as National Holiday
The 2026 holiday calendar has included Unity Day (Aid Al Wahda). Employers should update scheduling, holiday pay and compensatory rest rules.

Employing staff in Morocco in 2026 requires enterprises to distinguish between the non-agricultural minimum wage (SMIG) and the agricultural minimum wage (SMAG), and separately handle the National Social Security Fund (CNSS), compulsory health insurance (AMO), payroll income tax (IR), seniority bonus, overtime and termination compensation. In particular, the nominal employer contribution total of 21.09% cannot be directly multiplied by all high wages, because some CNSS items have a monthly base ceiling.

This guide is intended for Chinese enterprise HR, finance, legal and overseas business teams that recruit, sign contracts, run payroll, calculate employer costs, use Employer of Record (EOR) services or handle performance, redundancy and departure in Morocco. The article focuses on ordinary private sector employment; agriculture, domestic work, mining, seafarers, foreign employees and industries subject to collective agreements still need case-by-case confirmation.

Moroccan wages and employer costs cannot be expressed with only a single uniform monthly salary or uniform percentage. Enterprises should first confirm the industry, normal working hours, personnel category, wage composition, seniority, CNSS base, collective agreement and contractual benefits, and then form the offer and cost quotation.

3. Morocco Labor Law and Regulatory Framework

Moroccan labor relations are mainly governed by Labor Code No. 65-99, Minimum Wage Decree No. 2.25.983, CNSS and AMO rules, the 2026 General Tax Code, personal data protection requirements, and applicable collective agreements, employment contracts and company policies.

Rule Source Main Content Employer Impact
Labor Code No. 65-99Contracts, probation, working hours, leave, discipline and terminationConstitutes the national floor for ordinary private labor relations
Decree No. 2.25.9832026 non-agricultural and agricultural minimum wagesImplemented separately by hour and working day
CNSS and AMO RulesSocial security, medical insurance, registration and contributionsDistinguish employee, employer, capped and uncapped items
2026 General Tax CodePayroll income tax and employer withholding filingUse 2026 annual tax brackets and statutory deductions
Collective Agreements, Contracts and Policies13th month salary, supplementary retirement, additional insurance and more favorable leaveConstitutes an obligation when applicable or effectively promised

The competent department for employment and labor affairs bears labor policy and inspection responsibilities; the National Social Security Fund (CNSS) is responsible for statutory social security and medical insurance registration, filing and contributions; the tax authority is responsible for payroll income tax (IR) management; and the National Agency for Employment and Skills Promotion (ANAPEC) provides public employment services.

4. Recruitment, Offer and Onboarding

Recruitment should focus on position qualifications, experience and actual work requirements, and must not discriminate based on race, color, gender, religion, political opinion, social origin or trade union activity. Recruitment agencies must not charge placement fees to job seekers, and temporary employment enterprises should have applicable licenses.

Long-term fixed scheduling, direct manager control, personal performance of duties and fixed monthly remuneration may still be deemed an employment relationship even if named a "consulting contract." The contractor model cannot be used as the default solution to circumvent CNSS, annual leave and termination responsibilities.

After a probationary employee has worked for 1 week, if termination is not due to serious misconduct, notice is usually required according to the pay cycle: usually 2 days for daily, weekly or semi-monthly paid employees, and usually 8 days for monthly paid employees. After probation expires, "failed probation" cannot continue to be used to bypass ordinary termination procedures.

The 2026 annual taxable income brackets are: up to MAD 40,000 applies 0%; MAD 40,001-60,000 applies 10%; MAD 60,001-80,000 applies 20%; MAD 80,001-100,000 applies 30%; MAD 100,001-180,000 applies 34%; above MAD 180,000 applies 37%. Actual payroll withholding should also consider statutory deductions, family burdens and tax credits, and cannot directly multiply the highest marginal rate by all pre-tax wages.

8. Statutory Holidays, Annual Leave and Other Statutory Leave

After 6 months of continuous service, employees usually accrue 1.5 working days of annual leave per month of service; 2 days per month for employees under 18. Every 5 years of service usually adds 1.5 days, with a statutory cap of 30 working days.

Leave Type or Scenario Statutory Rules Employer Action

Split or Carry-forwardBy mutual agreement may be split or accumulated for two consecutive yearsMust not make actual continuous leave lower than statutory requirement
Ordinary Sick LeaveNotify within 48 hours; medical certificate usually required after 4 daysDistinguish employer wage responsibility and CNSS benefits
Maternity LeaveUsually 14 weeks, with 7 weeks prenatal and 7 weeks postnatalProtect position and process CNSS income replacement materials
Family Event LeaveEmployees may enjoy corresponding leave for marriage, death of close relative, etc.Whether paid should be judged by employee status and legal provisions

The 7 consecutive weeks after childbirth usually must not be worked. Eligible mothers may also apply for up to 90 days of suspension of work, or with employer consent, up to 1 year of unpaid parental leave. Employers must not terminate the employment contract due to pregnancy or protected maternity leave.

9. Employer Social Security, Mandatory Benefits and Taxation

Employees usually bear CNSS social security 4.48% and AMO basic medical 2.26%; employers usually bear CNSS social security 8.98%, AMO 4.11%, family allowances 6.40% and vocational training tax 1.60%. The 4.48% and 8.98% CNSS items usually have a monthly base ceiling of MAD 6,000, while other items are usually uncapped.

10. Local Employees and Foreign Employees

Local employees and foreign employees lawfully authorized to work are in principle protected by Morocco's minimum wage, working hours, leave, occupational safety and termination rules. Foreign employees should also have a work permit and residence status matching the contract employer, position and work location.

11. Remote Work, Data Privacy and Employee Records

Remote work arrangements should clarify in writing the usual location, equipment, expenses, normal working hours, overtime, occupational health and safety, data security and return-to-office mechanism. Before employees change cities or work cross-border, labor, tax, social security and work permit impacts should be re-assessed.

Enterprises should collect employee identity, bank, payroll, health, performance and discipline data as necessary, restrict access and cross-border transfer, and set statutory or reasonable retention periods.

It is recommended that HR confirm personnel and leave monthly, business departments confirm working hours and bonuses, the payroll team confirm statutory parameters, and finance confirm payments and receipts; any correction should be linked to the original payroll period.

Abusive dismissal without just cause or procedure may also generate damages of 1.5 months' wages per year of service, capped at 36 months, and may be combined with payment in lieu of notice, statutory severance compensation and unused annual leave.

In disciplinary dismissal, the employer should usually give the employee an opportunity to defend within 8 days of learning of the conduct, allow them to be accompanied by an employee representative or union representative and keep minutes. After the decision, it should usually be delivered in person within 48 hours or by registered mail, and a copy submitted to the labor inspection authority as required.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS (FAQ)

What is the minimum wage in Morocco in 2026?
The non-agricultural minimum wage is MAD 17.92/hour from January 1, 2026; based on 191 hours, budget reference is approximately MAD 3,422.72/month. The agricultural minimum wage is MAD 97.44/working day from April 1, 2026; the two cannot be mixed.

What is the employer social security rate in Morocco?
The nominal employer total is usually approximately 21.09%, including CNSS 8.98%, AMO 4.11%, family allowances 6.40% and vocational training tax 1.60%. However, CNSS 8.98% usually has a monthly base ceiling of MAD 6,000 and cannot be directly multiplied by 21.09% for all high wages.

How much social security do Moroccan employees bear?
Employees usually bear CNSS 4.48% and AMO 2.26%. The CNSS 4.48% usually has a monthly base ceiling of MAD 6,000, while AMO is usually uncapped.

How many days of annual leave do Moroccan employees have per year?
After 6 months of continuous service, employees usually accrue 1.5 working days per month of service; 2 days per month for employees under 18. Every 5 years of service usually adds 1.5 days, with a statutory cap of 30 working days.

How long is the probation period in Morocco?
CDI management personnel maximum 3 months, ordinary employees 1.5 months, workers 15 days, each renewable once. For CDD less than 6 months, usually 1 day per contract week, maximum 2 weeks; for more than 6 months, usually maximum 1 month.

Is 13th month salary mandatory in Morocco?
There is no national uniform mandatory 13th month salary applicable to all employers in the ordinary private sector. It constitutes an obligation only when a collective agreement, employment contract, company policy or stable practice provides otherwise.

Does ordinary dismissal only require paying notice pay?
No. The employer also needs just cause, fair procedure, written notice and complete settlement. When eligible, statutory severance compensation, unused annual leave and other amounts may also arise; unlawful or abusive dismissal may generate additional damages.

Can employees be hired through EOR without a Moroccan entity?
Enterprises can assess a compliant Employer of Record (EOR) arrangement, but must confirm case by case the local contract employer, position, client management boundary, CNSS and tax, work permit, collective agreement, data processing and termination responsibility.
