Collective Bargaining Agreements, Social Insurance, Annual Leave, and Termination Compliance

2026 POLICY UPDATE

Adjustment to the threshold for dismissal on personal grounds
From January 1, 2026, termination on personal grounds will apply a "proper reason" standard, but trivial, arbitrary, or discriminatory reasons remain prohibited. Employers must continue to complete an overall assessment, warnings, and an employee statement procedure.

Relaxation of first fixed-term contracts
From June 1, 2026, where statutory conditions are met and the parties have had no employment relationship in the past five years, a fixed-term contract of up to one year may be concluded once without a reason. Employers should verify historical relationships and avoid consecutive renewals.

Reform of temporary layoffs and re-employment
From June 1, 2026, the statutory notice period for temporary layoffs will be shortened to 7 days; the statutory re-employment obligation will in principle apply only to employers with at least 50 employees. Before restructuring, the applicable CBA must still be checked.

Finland has a comprehensive system of labor protection and social insurance, but there is no nationwide statutory minimum wage. When determining wages, overtime, holiday bonuses, sick pay, and notice periods, companies first need to determine whether a generally applicable collective bargaining agreement (CBA) applies.

This guide is intended for HR, legal, finance, and overseas business teams of Chinese companies planning to recruit or manage employees in Finland. It covers key issues such as recruitment and onboarding, contracts, wages, working hours, leave, social insurance, foreign employees, termination of employment, and Employer of Record (EOR) services.

3. Finnish Labor Law and Regulatory Framework

Finnish employment relationships are mainly governed by the Employment Contracts Act, the Working Hours Act, the Annual Holidays Act, equality and non-discrimination rules, occupational health and safety, tax, and social insurance rules.

The Ministry of Economic Affairs and Employment is responsible for labor policy; occupational safety and health authorities provide guidance on employment relationships and labor inspection; the Finnish Tax Administration (Vero), the Incomes Register, the Finnish Centre for Pensions, and employment funds respectively manage taxation, wage reporting, pensions, and unemployment insurance.

A generally applicable CBA may create mandatory minimum terms even if the employer is not a member of the signatory employer association. A CBA may determine minimum wages, wage groups, overtime, shift allowances, holiday bonuses, sick pay, and notice periods. Companies must therefore assess applicability based on their main business and actual job content.

4. Recruitment, Offer, and Onboarding

Recruitment advertisements and interviews must not discriminate on the basis of age, gender, pregnancy, family status, ethnicity, nationality, religion, disability, or other protected characteristics.

Health information should generally be processed by occupational health services only to the necessary extent. Employers should in principle retain only fitness-for-work conclusions or information permitted by law.

If a fixed-term contract without reason lasts at least 6 months, the parties may terminate it early in accordance with law. When the contract expires, the employer should explain to the employee the possibility of conversion to an open-ended contract or a fixed-term contract with reason.

If the employer plans to increase staff for the same or similar work, it may need to offer work to the former employee within a statutory period not exceeding 4 months. Unlawful use of fixed-term contracts may result in the contract being regarded as open-ended.

The probation period is usually up to 6 months; for fixed-term contracts, probation usually may not exceed half the contract term. Sick leave or family leave may extend probation within the statutory scope, but the employer should notify promptly.

During probation, the contract may be cancelled, but the reason must not be improper, discriminatory, or unrelated to the purpose of probation, and the employee should usually be given an opportunity to be heard.

6. Wages, Minimum Wage, and Gross-to-Net Calculation

Finland has no nationwide statutory minimum wage. Wage determination should follow this order:

  1. Determine the employer's main business and industry;
  2. Determine whether a generally applicable CBA applies;
  3. Determine job wage group, employee experience, work location, and normal working hours;
  4. Add night work, Sunday, shift, standby, and other CBA allowances;
  5. Confirm holiday bonus and agreement wage increase dates.

If no CBA applies, the wage must still be usual and reasonable for the work performed by the employee.

Wage changes, temporary layoffs, or working time bank arrangements must have lawful basis and written records.

8. Public Holidays, Annual Leave, and Other Statutory Leave

Finland's holiday accrual year is usually April 1 to March 31 of the following year. The main holiday season is usually May 2 to September 30.

Employees usually first apply the 14-day rule; if the contract provides for less than 14 days of work per month, the 35-hour rule applies.

9. Employer Social Insurance, Mandatory Benefits, and Tax

Finnish employers do not pay only one "total social insurance percentage." They pay or report different items separately to tax authorities, pension insurers, employment funds, and insurance companies.

Employee TyEL pension and unemployment insurance are usually withheld by the employer from wages. Employee health insurance contributions are usually included in the tax withholding rate and should not be separately deducted again.

10. Local Employees and Foreign Employees

Local employees and foreign employees in principle apply the same labor law, CBA, wage, working hours, leave, and social insurance minimum standards. However, foreign employees may also involve residence, work permits, tax residency, social insurance coverage, and cross-border work arrangements.

Employers should verify the employee's lawful right to work before issuing an unconditional offer. Employer of Record (EOR) services do not automatically have work permit or immigration sponsorship capability.

Cross-border remote work also requires assessment of:

11. Remote Work, Data Privacy, and Employee Records

Remote work agreements should clarify work location, remote work days, equipment, expenses, working time records, occupational safety, information security, and approval for work abroad.

Even if the employee works from home, the employer still bears corresponding occupational health and safety obligations. Cross-border remote work cannot be carried out only on the basis of a general remote work clause in the employment contract.

Employee data is governed by both the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and Finnish rules on privacy in working life. Health, drug testing, monitoring, background checks, and location data may only be collected and processed within necessary and lawful limits.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Does Finland have a nationwide minimum wage?
No. Wage floors in most industries are determined by generally applicable collective bargaining agreements (CBAs). If no CBA applies, the employer must still pay wages that are usual and reasonable for the specific work.

Do all employees in Finland apply the same social insurance rate?
No. The actual TyEL employer rate, occupational accident insurance, and group life insurance depend on the insurer, industry, and company. Unemployment insurance is also affected by the payroll threshold.

Can employees be dismissed without reason after the 2026 reform?
No. Termination on personal grounds still requires a proper reason and overall assessment. Trivial, arbitrary, or discriminatory reasons remain insufficient, and warning and employee statement procedures are usually required.

Can all first fixed-term contracts omit the reason?
No. The new rule from June 1, 2026 applies only to eligible first contracts, including conditions such as no employment relationship in the past five years and a maximum term of one year.

Are there no follow-up obligations after a fixed-term contract expires?
Not necessarily. Under the 2026 rule for a first fixed-term contract without reason, if the employer plans to increase staff for the same or similar work, it may need to offer work to the former employee within the statutory period.

Must fixed severance pay be paid when an employee leaves?
In principle, Finland has no unified statutory severance pay applicable to all ordinary departures. However, the employer must still pay final wages, notice period wages, unused annual leave compensation, applicable holiday bonus, bonus or commission, and other accrued amounts.

Can EOR bypass CBA or automatically obtain a work permit?
No. EOR must still identify and implement the applicable CBA, and it cannot automatically solve the employee's residence or work permit issues.
