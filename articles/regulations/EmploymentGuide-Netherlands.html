Minimum Wage, Payroll Tax, Statutory Leave and Termination Compliance

2026 Policy Update

Minimum Hourly Wage Increased Mid-Year From July 1, 2026, the statutory minimum hourly wage for those aged 21 and above increases from EUR 14.71 to EUR 14.99. Employers must update payroll tables and review CAO wage scales accordingly.

Payroll Tax and Employer Premiums Switched From January 1, 2026, new AWf, Aof and 6.10% Zvw parameters apply, with the relevant maximum contributory income usually at EUR 79,409. Employers should recalculate costs based on contract and size.

Transition Payment Cap Increased From January 1, 2026, the statutory transition payment cap increases to EUR 102,000; for higher annual salaries, the cap is one pre-tax annual salary. Termination budgets and fixed-term non-renewal calculations need to be updated accordingly.

The Netherlands has a mature labor protection, tax and social security system, but the actual employment obligations of an enterprise are not determined only by national law. Collective Labour Agreements (CAO), industry pensions, position nature, contract type, employer size and employee status all change wages, benefits and employment costs.

This guide is intended for Chinese enterprise HR, legal, finance and overseas business teams planning to recruit or manage employees in the Netherlands, covering key issues from recruitment and onboarding, payroll calculation, leave and remote work, to termination of employment and Employer of Record (EOR) model selection.

3. Netherlands Labor Law and Regulatory Framework

Dutch employment relationships are mainly governed by the Dutch Civil Code, minimum wage rules, Working Hours Act, equal treatment, occupational health and safety, tax and social insurance systems.

Tax and payroll filing is managed by the Dutch Tax and Customs Administration (Belastingdienst); employee insurance and some dismissal applications are handled by the Employee Insurance Agency (UWV); working conditions and occupational safety are supervised by the Netherlands Labour Authority.

CAO may set standards higher than the legal floor for wage scales, pensions, overtime allowances, leave, sick pay top-up and dismissal procedures. Enterprises should determine before issuing the offer whether the business activity, position and work location fall within a mandatory applicable CAO or industry pension fund.

4. Recruitment, Offer and Onboarding

Recruitment advertisements, screening and interviews must not discriminate based on age, gender, pregnancy, marriage, religion, ethnicity, nationality, disability or trade union status and other protected characteristics. Background checks, health information, criminal records, credit records and social media screening must comply with position necessity and data minimization principles.

Probation must be agreed in writing and the duration must be the same for employer and employee. During probation, termination is usually possible immediately, but must not be based on discrimination, pregnancy, illness or other protected reasons.

The contract should also clearly state work location, wages, working hours, holiday allowance, annual leave, CAO, pension, confidentiality, intellectual property, remote work, expenses and notice period.

Employee gross-to-net cannot simply multiply the marginal tax rate by total wages. The payroll system needs to calculate in conjunction with payroll tax table, payroll tax credit, resident status, age, pension and other personal circumstances; enterprises should not promise a fixed net salary.

Night work, young employees, pregnant employees and safety-sensitive positions may be subject to stricter rules. Employers should keep working hours, scheduling, overtime, rest and leave records.

Positions involving warehousing, manufacturing, logistics, weekend or public holiday work should verify CAO and allowance rules before quotation; cost cannot be estimated based only on basic monthly salary.

8. Statutory Holidays, Annual Leave and Other Statutory Leave

Statutory minimum annual leave equals 4 times the employee's weekly contractual working hours. An employee working 40 hours per week usually enjoys 160 hours, i.e., 20 working days; part-time employees and those joining or leaving mid-year are calculated pro-rata.

Statutory annual leave usually expires 6 months after the end of the accrual year, but the employer must give the employee a genuine opportunity to take leave and remind them appropriately; unused annual leave should be settled on departure.

Employee payroll tax and national insurance are withheld and paid by the employer through the payroll system. 10. Local Employees and Foreign Employees

Dutch local employees and foreign employees usually apply the same labor law minimum standards, but foreign employees also involve residence, work permit, sponsor entity, salary threshold, tax residency and cross-border social security coordination.

EU, EEA and Swiss citizens have different work rights paths from third-country nationals. Employers should verify the candidate's lawful work rights before issuing an unconditional offer.

Employer of Record (EOR) does not automatically obtain immigration sponsor qualification, nor can it replace a work permit. Employees working remotely from outside the Netherlands long-term may also trigger local payroll tax, social security, permanent establishment and work rights issues.

11. Remote Work, Data Privacy and Employee Records

Remote employees remain subject to working hours, occupational health and safety and data protection rules. The contract or policy should clarify the main work location, home office equipment and expenses, cross-border work restrictions, accident reporting, information security and arrangements for entering the office or client site.

The Netherlands applies the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). Employers must have a lawful basis for processing employee data and comply with purpose limitation, data minimization, retention periods and access control requirements.

Health data, background checks, monitoring, location tracking, cross-border transfer and AI recruitment tools are highly sensitive areas and should establish independent assessment, notice and authorization processes.

12. Termination of Employment, Compensation and Final Settlement

Scenario General Path Key Control
Performance, conduct or relationship breakdown Usually reviewed by court or mutual agreement Need improvement, warning, training and alternative position evidence
Economic redundancy UWV or mutual agreement Prove business reason, position selection and redeployment
Long-term sick leave dismissal Usually after 104 weeks through UWV Need to prove return-to-work obligation fulfilled
Fixed-term expiry Terminate on expiry and serve expiry notice Contracts of 6 months or more usually require 1 month's written notice
Probation termination Usually immediate termination possible Clause must be valid and reason must not be unlawful
Summary dismissal Only for serious urgent reasons Must act immediately and clearly state reason

The statutory notice period for employer termination of an indefinite-term contract is usually at least 1 month, increasing by 1 month for every 5 years of service, up to a maximum of 4 months; the statutory notice period for employee resignation is usually 1 month.

Notice period is not a separate termination right. Outside probation, the employer must still have reasonable grounds and adopt the correct path of UWV, court or mutual agreement depending on the specific reason.

Statutory transition payment is usually calculated from the first day of employment, at one-third of pre-tax monthly salary per year of service, calculated pro-rata for less than one year. The 2026 transition payment cap is EUR 102,000; if the employee's pre-tax annual salary is higher, the cap is one pre-tax annual salary.

When the employer actively terminates the employment relationship or decides not to renew a fixed-term contract, the transition payment should usually be calculated.

Final settlement should include:

In mutual agreement termination, the employee usually has a 2-week written cooling-off period; if the agreement does not inform of this right, the cooling-off period is usually extended to 3 weeks.

Non-EU employees, client site positions, warehousing/logistics/manufacturing positions, industries with mandatory CAO and fixed-term or flexible employment all require escalated review.

Enterprises should require service providers to explain item by item the employer entity, direction and supervision, occupational safety, insurance, payroll and tax filing, data processing and termination responsibility; compliance of the solution cannot be judged only by "ability to pay wages."

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS (FAQ)

Does the Netherlands have a national uniform statutory minimum monthly salary?
No. The Netherlands uses a statutory minimum hourly wage; the monthly amount must be converted in conjunction with employee age, contractual working hours, actual working hours and pay period; the applicable CAO may also provide higher wage standards.

Are all Dutch public holidays paid rest days?
Not necessarily. Whether public holidays are rest days, whether they are paid and how holiday work is compensated are mainly determined by the applicable CAO, employment contract or company policy.

Does a fixed-term contract expiry require no payment at all?
Not necessarily. Fixed-term contracts of 6 months or more usually require 1 month's written notice on whether to renew. When the employer decides not to renew, transition payment, unused annual leave, holiday allowance and other accrued rights should also be calculated.

Can Dutch employer social security be quoted at a fixed percentage?
It should not be. AWf rate depends on the employment contract, Aof rate depends on employer size, Whk is an individual rate, and pension and CAO costs also vary by industry, so they must be calculated item by item.

Can EOR automatically sponsor third-country employees?
No. The feasibility of the EOR solution, immigration sponsor qualification, work permit and salary threshold must be confirmed case by case based on employee nationality, position, work location and actual employer entity.
