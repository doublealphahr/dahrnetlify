2. Three Rules Most in Need of Update in 2026

2.1 2026 Regional Minimum Wage Increase

Decree No. 293/2025/ND-CP takes effect from January 1, 2026. Vietnam's minimum wage continues to be divided by Regions I–IV; a single national uniform amount cannot be written.

Region Monthly Minimum Wage Hourly Minimum Wage Common Applicable Scenario
Region IVND 5,310,000VND 25,500Most positions in Ho Chi Minh City, Hanoi core urban areas, and developed regions
Region IIVND 4,730,000VND 22,700Some provincial capitals and industrial cities
Region IIIVND 4,140,000VND 20,000Medium-developed regions
Region IVVND 3,700,000VND 17,800Other regions

Minimum wage is only the statutory floor, not the market wage. Positions requiring vocational training or skills, collective agreements, manufacturing shifts, hazardous positions, or customer position standards may require higher wages. Enterprises should also note that Region adjustment not only affects basic wage but also affects the Unemployment Insurance contribution ceiling based on regional minimum wage.

2.2 July 2026 Mandatory Social Insurance Contribution Ceiling Adjustment

The Vietnam Social Security Agency announced that from July 1, 2026, the reference level is raised from VND 2,340,000 to VND 2,530,000. The maximum mandatory Social Insurance (SI) contribution wage base is calculated at 20 times the reference level, so the ceiling is raised to VND 50,600,000/month.

Item First Half of 2026 From July 1, 2026 Payroll Impact
Reference levelVND 2,340,000VND 2,530,000Affects social insurance benefits and ceiling based on reference level
Social Insurance (SI) and Health Insurance (HI) contribution wage ceilingVND 46,800,000VND 50,600,000Employer and employee contribution amounts for high-salary employees may rise
Unemployment Insurance (UI) contribution wage ceiling20 times the regional minimum wage of the employee's work locationStill 20 times the regional minimum wageMust not be confused with the SI and HI ceiling as the same ceiling

Enterprises should complete parameter switching before July payroll and re-verify high-salary employees, foreign employees, bonuses, allowances, and contribution base. It is not enough to update only Social Insurance (SI) and omit Health Insurance (HI), nor to incorrectly apply the Unemployment Insurance (UI) ceiling as VND 50,600,000.

2.3 New Vietnam Culture Day and Updated Public Holiday Calendar

The National Assembly passed Resolution No. 28/2026/QH16, effective from July 1, 2026. The Vietnam Government Portal announcement lists November 24 "Vietnam Culture Day" as a new paid public holiday and states the total paid public holidays as 14 days, of which Tet is 7 days.

Employers should add November 24 to HR and payroll calendars and update Tet holiday parameters simultaneously. Continuous long holidays formed by public sector due to weekends, shift swaps, or compensatory rest do not automatically equal new statutory paid days for private enterprises. Private enterprises should still clarify specific holidays based on labor law, competent authority notices, and their own weekly rest arrangements, and notify employees in advance.

3. Vietnam Labor Law and Regulatory Framework

Vietnam labor relations are mainly governed by the 2019 Labor Code, regional minimum wage decrees, social insurance law, health insurance and unemployment insurance rules, and trade union law. Actual work location, working hours, management relationship, and wage payment method all affect the employment relationship and employer responsibility.

The Vietnam guide cannot be quoted only as "monthly salary × fixed employer cost ratio." Wage Region, insurance ceiling, employee nationality, bonuses and allowances, overtime and night shift, and departure scenarios may all change the final cost.

6. Wages, Minimum Wage, and Gross-to-Net Calculation

6.1 Wage Structure and Payment

Wages are generally paid in Vietnamese Dong or as lawfully agreed. Enterprises should distinguish basic wage, position or responsibility allowance, meal allowance, transport, housing, bonus, commission, overtime, night shift, and other benefits. Regular items may enter the insurance contribution base, Personal Income Tax (PIT), or departure calculation, and cannot be uniformly labeled as "allowance" and defaulted to exclusion.

13th month pay or annual bonus is not a nationally uniform statutory mandatory item; whether paid and how calculated depends on the labor contract, company bonus policy, collective agreement, and established stable practice.

6.2 Ho Chi Minh City Monthly Salary VND 50,000,000 Example

The following is an example for an ordinary Vietnamese office employee. Assume the employee works in Ho Chi Minh City Region I, with monthly contract wage VND 50,000,000. This wage is higher than the Region I minimum wage; from July 1, 2026, the SI and HI ceiling is VND 50,600,000, so this example is charged at actual VND 50,000,000. The UI base also does not exceed the Region I ceiling of VND 106,200,000.

Gross-to-Net calculation cannot be reverse-engineered using only a single fixed ratio. Employee net income is also affected by SI, HI, UI, PIT, personal and dependent deductions, bonuses, and other taxable items.

8. Public Holidays, Annual Leave, and Other Statutory Leave

8.1 2026 Vietnam Public Holidays

The continuous holiday arrangement of Vietnam's public sector and the statutory paid days of private enterprises are not the same concept. Long holidays formed by public sector due to weekends, compensatory rest, or shift swaps should not be directly listed as all new statutory paid days for private enterprises. Private employers need to determine specific dates such as Tet and National Day based on applicable law and competent authority notices, and notify employees in advance.

Less than 12 months of onboarding does not mean no annual leave. It is usually prorated as "annual leave entitlement ÷ 12 × actual working months." When the employee departs, contract terminates, or becomes unemployed, compensation should be paid for unused prorated annual leave. Enterprises should keep onboarding date, working months, entitlement days, taken days, carryover agreement, and departure balance.

8.3 Sick Leave, Maternity Leave, and Family-Related Leave

Employees are entitled to sick leave under social insurance rules, maternity leave of 6 months, and other statutory leave. Employers should handle these separately from annual leave and maintain proper records.

9. Employer Social Insurance, Mandatory Benefits, and Tax

9.1 Employer and Employee Responsibility Overview

The employer is responsible for determining the applicability, base, and ceiling of SI, HI, UI, and trade union funds, withholding the employee share from wages, and declaring and paying monthly together with the employer share.

Unemployment Insurance (UI)Generally 1%Generally 1%Base generally capped at 20 times regional minimum wage of work location; foreign employees generally not applicable

"Employer social insurance 17.5%" cannot be treated as the complete employer cost. For ordinary Vietnamese employees, employer mandatory insurance generally also includes HI 3% and UI 1%, totaling 21.5%; trade union funds generally require an additional 2%.

11. Remote Work, Data Privacy, and Record Keeping

Remote work does not change the nature of the labor contract. The employer still needs to manage working hours, overtime, occupational safety, equipment, expenses, data access, and employee personal information. When employees work remotely across provinces, the actual work location may affect minimum wage Region and Unemployment Insurance (UI) ceiling; when employees work long-term outside Vietnam, local labor law, tax residency, social insurance attribution, permanent establishment, and work permit risks should also be assessed.

12.3 Severance Allowance, Job-loss Allowance, and Unemployment Insurance Benefit

Item Who Pays Common Trigger Scenario Calculation Logic Key Reminder
Severance AllowanceEmployerContract expiry, mutual agreement, lawful employee resignation, lawful employer termination, and other qualifying scenarios0.5 × countable years × average contract wage of 6 months before terminationPeriods already covered by UI are generally deducted from countable years
Job-loss AllowanceEmployerStructural adjustment, technological or economic change, split, merger, sale, etc. causing employee to lose job1 × countable years × average contract wage of 6 months before termination, generally not less than 2 months' wageBelongs to redundancy or restructuring cost, not ordinary resignation cost
Unemployment BenefitUnemployment Insurance FundEmployee meets UI contribution, registration, and job-seeking conditionsGenerally 60% of the average UI contribution wage of the 6 consecutive months before unemploymentNot directly paid by employer, but UI participation period affects employer allowance calculation

The countable years for allowance calculation are generally: total period the employee actually worked for the employer, minus the period already covered by unemployment insurance, minus the period for which the employer has already paid severance allowance or job-loss allowance. A full 12 months counts as 1 year; a fraction of less than 1 year is generally 0.5 year for 6 months or less, and 1 year for more than 6 months.

Example: the employee's average contract wage for the first 6 months is VND 50,000,000, with 8 months of countable period not covered by UI. 8 months is generally converted to 1 year, and severance allowance is 0.5 × 1 × VND 50,000,000, i.e., VND 25,000,000. If the countable period for economic redundancy is 1 year and 4 months, it is generally converted to 1.5 years, and the formula amount is 1.5 × VND 50,000,000, i.e., VND 75,000,000; but job-loss allowance is generally not less than 2 months' wage, so it should be assessed at least at VND 100,000,000.
