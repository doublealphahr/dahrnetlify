Minimum Wage, Payroll Withholding, Statutory Leave, and Termination Compliance

2026 POLICY UPDATE

New CRA withholding tables effective from July
From July 1, 2026, Payroll should use the CRA new T4127 formulas and T4032 withholding tables, and review tax, CPP, and EI parameters for affected wage periods.

Federal minimum wage adjusted to CAD 18.15
From April 1, 2026, the federal minimum wage for federally regulated employees is CAD 18.15/hour, and must be compared with the employee's usual work province standard, applying the higher.

Ontario minimum wage raised in October
From October 1, 2026, Ontario general minimum wage rises from CAD 17.60 to CAD 17.95/hour; employers should update contracts, hourly wages, and Payroll settings simultaneously.

Canada is not a unified market applying a single labor standard. Most employees are subject to the employment standards of their actual work province or territory, while specific industries such as banking, telecommunications, aviation, railways, and interprovincial or international transport may apply the Canada Labour Code. Before quotation or recruitment, companies must first lock the actual work location, industry jurisdiction, employee status, salary structure, and remote work arrangement.

This guide is intended for Chinese company HR, finance, legal, and business teams planning to recruit, sign contracts, run payroll, calculate employer costs, use Employer of Record (EOR) services, or handle employee departure in Canada. The main text uses Ontario as the provincial calculation and leave example; Quebec must separately apply the Quebec Pension Plan (QPP), Quebec Parental Insurance Plan (QPIP), Revenu Québec, CNESST, and language rules, and cannot directly apply Ontario positions.

2. Three Rules to Watch Most in 2026

Payroll withholding parameters updated from July 1

The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) has issued T4127 payroll deduction formulas and T4032 withholding tables effective from July 1, 2026. Companies cannot continue using the beginning-of-year parameters for the whole year; they should confirm that the payroll system has switched to the July version, and re-verify federal tax, provincial tax, CPP, and EI withholding for affected wage periods.

Federal minimum wage raised to CAD 18.15

From April 1, 2026, the federal regulated private sector minimum wage is CAD 18.15/hour. If the employee's usual work province minimum wage is higher, the higher standard should be paid. This amount cannot be used as a uniform minimum wage for all Canadian employees.

Ontario minimum wage adjusted again in October

Ontario general minimum wage is CAD 17.60/hour from October 1, 2025 to September 30, 2026, and rises to CAD 17.95/hour from October 1, 2026. Employers should update Offer templates, hourly wages, holiday pay, overtime base, and Payroll rules simultaneously.

Canadian employment contracts cannot exclude mandatory employment standards. Employees covered by collective agreements should be judged by combining the agreement and mandatory regulations.

4. Recruitment, Offer, and Onboarding

Offer and employment contract should clarify statutory employer, position, actual work province, salary, bonus, commission, working hours, overtime, leave, benefits, probation, termination clause, confidentiality, and IP. Remote positions cannot only state "Canada"; they should state the province where the employee is approved to work and the approval rule for relocation or cross-province work.

Canada has no uniform statutory probation period. Probation should be clearly agreed in the contract and is subject to the work province employment standards, human rights rules, and common law. In Ontario, for example, employees generally begin to enjoy Employment Standards Act (ESA) termination notice or termination pay after 3 months of continuous service, but this does not mean termination before 3 months can be based on discrimination, retaliation, or other unlawful reasons.

Termination during probation still requires settlement of wages, accrued holiday pay, reimbursement, and other due entitlements. After probation ends, employers should continue to keep performance feedback and position fit records, avoiding handling subsequent dismissal only based on the contract's "probation qualified" statement.

6. Wages, Minimum Wage, and Gross-to-Net Calculation

Canadian minimum wage should be judged by jurisdiction and work province. Federal minimum wage only covers federally regulated employees; most employees apply provincial or territorial standards, and special students, home workers, tour guides, farm employees, commission positions, managers, or professionals may have separate rules.

Using a Toronto customer success manager with annual salary CAD 100,000 as an example, monthly gross wage is approximately CAD 8,333.33. This salary is above Ontario minimum wage, but overtime exemption, bonus and commission, work permit salary conditions, and market salary still need to be verified.

Employee gross-to-net is not simply deducting a fixed "social security ratio." Using annual CAD 100,000 and non-Quebec employee as an example, 2026 CPP employee maximum contribution is CAD 4,230.45, CPP2 maximum CAD 416, EI maximum CAD 1,123.07; income tax also depends on federal and provincial tax tables, TD1 information, and pay cycle.

8. Public Holidays, Annual Leave, and Other Statutory Leave

Using Ontario as an example, employees with less than 5 years service generally enjoy at least 2 weeks annual leave and at least 4% holiday pay per 12 months; after 5 years service generally 3 weeks annual leave and at least 6% holiday pay. Even if an employee departs before one year, holiday pay accrued by wages is still generated, and statutory minimum entitlement cannot be eliminated by a "year-end reset" policy.

11. Remote Work, Data Privacy, and Employee Records

Cross-province remote employees should lock the actual work province. After an employee moves from Ontario to British Columbia, Alberta, or Quebec, minimum wage, working hours, leave, workers' compensation, Payroll province, and employer tax may change, and the original province template cannot continue to be fully used.

Remote work policy should specify approved location, working hours, equipment and expenses, occupational safety, data security, cross-border work restrictions, and relocation notice. Before employee data is transferred to China headquarters, EOR, Payroll service provider, or Human Resources Information System (HRIS), the purpose, recipient, access rights, retention period, and security measures should be stated.

Employers should keep contract, wage, working hours, overtime, leave, withholding, performance, discipline, termination, and Record of Employment (ROE). Social Insurance Number, health, and background check data should have restricted access and cannot be retained indefinitely for convenience.

Ontario termination notice or termination pay and severance pay are two different items. Severance pay generally requires employee service of at least 5 years, and meeting statutory conditions such as employer global payroll total or permanent business closure. Benefits generally continue during statutory notice period, and ROE must be issued per Service Canada rules at departure or income interruption.

Example: Toronto employee with annual salary CAD 100,000, service 2 years 7 months, employer without-cause dismissal, assuming contract termination clause valid and only ESA minimum applies, no severance pay, bonus, or common law additional notice. Weekly wage approximately CAD 1,923.08, two weeks termination pay approximately CAD 3,846.15, 4% holiday pay on it approximately CAD 153.85; if there is also CAD 1,200 unpaid holiday pay, indicative cash subtotal approximately CAD 5,200. Actual settlement still needs to review contract validity, benefits, bonus, and common law liability.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Is there a uniform national minimum wage in Canada?
No. Most employees apply the minimum wage of their actual work province or territory; federal minimum wage only applies to federally regulated industries, and must be compared with the employee's usual work province standard, applying the higher.

What is the 2026 federal minimum wage?
From April 1, 2026, it is CAD 18.15/hour, applicable only to federally regulated employees such as banking, telecommunications, aviation, railways, and some interprovincial or international transport.

What is the 2026 Ontario minimum wage?
General minimum wage is CAD 17.60/hour before September 30, 2026, and rises to CAD 17.95/hour from October 1, 2026. Student and home worker categories apply different standards.

Is there a uniform probation period in Canada?
No. Probation depends on contract, work province, employment standards, and common law. Ontario employees generally begin to enjoy ESA termination notice or termination pay after 3 months continuous service, but termination before 3 months also cannot be for discrimination or retaliation.

What are the employer CPP and EI costs in Canada?
For non-Quebec employees in 2026, CPP is 5.95% each by employee and employer, CPP2 is 4% each; EI employee rate is 1.63%, employer generally 1.4 times employee premium. Annual ceiling and wage base must be applied simultaneously.

How many annual leave days do Ontario employees have per year?
Less than 5 years service generally at least 2 weeks, after 5 years at least 3 weeks; corresponding holiday pay generally at least 4% and 6% of wages respectively. Departing before one year also requires settlement of accrued holiday pay.

Does ordinary dismissal in Ontario require proving employee fault?
Ordinary without-cause dismissal generally does not require proving employee fault, but must meet ESA, contract, and common law notice or payment obligations, and cannot be based on discrimination, retaliation, or other unlawful reasons.

Can a company hire employees without a Canadian entity?
Companies may evaluate compliant Employer of Record (EOR), but must confirm employee work province, Payroll, workers' compensation, work permit, actual management boundary, and termination responsibility case by case.
