Employment Contracts, Wages, GOSI, Leave and Termination Compliance

2026 POLICY UPDATE

New System GOSI Pension Rate Rises to 10%: From July 1, 2026, the pension employer and employee rates for Saudi employees under the new system both rise from 9.5% to 10%; existing system participants should not directly apply this rate.

Summer Outdoor Work Ban Continues: From June 15 to September 15, 2026, the private sector is generally prohibited from arranging employees to perform outdoor work under direct sunlight from 12:00 to 15:00 daily.

2026 Founding Day Covers Private Sector: HRSD confirmed February 22, 2026 as an official holiday for the public, private and non-profit sectors, and scheduling and payroll processing should follow statutory holiday rules.

Saudi Arabia does not have a nationwide uniform statutory minimum wage applicable to all private sector employees. When setting quotations, companies need to simultaneously confirm employee nationality, GOSI system category, wage composition, Nitaqat Saudization counting, medical insurance and end-of-service benefits; SAR 4,000 is the wage threshold at which Saudi employees generally receive full Nitaqat counting, and does not equal a nationwide minimum wage.

This guide is intended for HR, finance, legal and business teams of Chinese companies recruiting, signing contracts, running payroll, calculating employer costs, using Employer of Record (EOR) services or handling separations in Saudi Arabia. The content is organized according to verifiable rules as of July 29, 2026; religious holidays, industry Saudization rates, occupational restrictions, medical insurance prices and employee right to work still need to be confirmed case by case at implementation.

2. Three Rules to Watch Most in 2026

New System GOSI Pension Rate Increased in July

For Saudi employees under the new social insurance system, the pension employer and employee rates were both 9.5% from July 1, 2025 to June 30, 2026, and both adjusted to 10% from July 1, 2026. Existing system participants generally continue at 9% each; SANED and work injury insurance are calculated separately. Companies cannot determine the system category based only on onboarding date or age, and should rely on GOSI records.

Summer Outdoor Work Ban Covers Three Months

From June 15 to September 15, 2026, the private sector is generally prohibited from arranging employees to perform outdoor work under direct sunlight from 12:00 to 15:00 daily. Construction, logistics, installation and on-site service teams should adjust shifts in advance and retain shade, hydration, heat stress training, risk assessment and exception basis.

Saudization Wage Threshold Is Not the Statutory Minimum Wage

When a Saudi employee's monthly wage reaches SAR 4,000, they generally count as 1 in Nitaqat; SAR 3,000 to below 4,000 generally counts as 0.5, and below SAR 3,000 generally does not count. This is a Saudization counting rule, not the statutory minimum wage for all private sector employees. Quotations still need to verify industry localization decisions, occupational restrictions, market wages and contract wage composition.

3. Saudi Arabian Labor Law and Regulatory Framework

Saudi private sector employment is mainly governed by the Labor Law, implementing regulations and amendments effective in 2025. The Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (HRSD) is responsible for labor supervision, Qiwa carries enterprise, employment contract and some labor affairs; the General Organization for Social Insurance (GOSI) is responsible for social insurance registration and contributions; the Council of Health Insurance is responsible for the applicable medical insurance framework, and the Wage Protection System (WPS) monitors whether private sector wages are paid on time as agreed.

The contracting employer bears responsibility for contracts, wages, GOSI, medical insurance, working time, leave, occupational health and safety and offboarding settlement. Clients or overseas headquarters may provide business management, but must not bypass the contracting employer to directly implement discipline or dismissal. Arabic contracts and Qiwa registration are particularly important for enforcement, and bilingual supplementary texts must not conflict with statutory or system records.

4. Recruitment, Job Offer and Onboarding

Before recruitment, confirm the genuine industry, job duties, work city, nationality requirements, occupational code, Saudization impact, contract type, shift and on-site risk. Recruitment advertisements must not screen by gender, disability, religion or other factors without lawful basis; where statutory Saudization or occupational restrictions apply, the basis should be clear, and false job titles cannot be used to satisfy statistics or permit requirements.

The offer should list basic wage, housing and transport allowances, fixed and variable bonuses, work location, working hours, probationary period, contract term, annual leave, notice period and employment conditions. The offer, Qiwa contract, GOSI contributory wage and Payroll master data must use consistent wage composition.

5. Employment Contracts, Contract Types and Probationary Period

The contract should include the parties' identities, work location, position, wages and allowances, start date, term, working hours, leave and termination clauses. Saudi long-term positions may use open-ended contracts; fixed-term, specific work, part-time or flexible work arrangements should use the applicable official system. The contract term for foreign employees also needs to be coordinated with work authorization and actual arrangements.

The probationary period must be clearly stated in the contract and generally must not exceed 180 days in total. Companies should record start and end dates in the HR system and correctly handle holidays or sick leave that are statutorily excluded from probation. Probation termination should also be delivered in writing, verify contract rights, prohibition of discrimination or retaliation and other restrictions, and settle earned wages and expenses; probation generally does not generate EOSB, but probation cannot be repeatedly reset by changing contract names.

6. Wages, Minimum Wage and Gross-to-Net Calculation

Saudi private sector has no uniform nationwide statutory minimum wage applicable to all employees. SAR 4,000 is generally only the full counting threshold for Saudi employees in Nitaqat. Monthly-paid employees are generally paid monthly through compliant bank and WPS channels, and payslips should be consistent with contracts, Qiwa and bank records. Deductions must have legal, judicial order or valid authorization basis, and comply with applicable limits.

Overtime pay is generally normal hourly wage plus 50% of the basic wage hourly amount. Fixed "all-inclusive overtime" clauses cannot exempt working time records or statutory differentials. EOSB is based on the last wage, and whether fixed and regularly paid allowances are included should be determined based on the wage definition, contract and actual payment facts.

8. Statutory Holidays, Annual Leave and Other Statutory Leave

Full-time employees are entitled to not less than 21 days of annual leave per year; not less than 30 days after 5 consecutive years with the same employer. Employees with less than one year should accrue by service period, and cannot be uniformly stipulated as "no annual leave before one year." Annual leave is in principle for actual rest, and accrued but unused balance should be cashed out upon separation.

11. Remote Work, Data Privacy and Employee Records

Remote or hybrid work should be clearly defined in a written policy covering work location, working time records, equipment, costs, information security, health and safety, cross-border access, inspection method and return-to-office rules. Long-term remote work from abroad may also change labor law, tax, social insurance, cross-border data and permanent establishment determinations, and should be assessed jointly by legal and tax before approval.

Employers should collect employee data on the principles of necessity and minimization, and restrict access by HR, Payroll, client managers and suppliers. Identity documents, bank accounts, medical certificates, GOSI materials, performance records and disciplinary investigation materials should be stored by category, with retention periods and deletion processes. Monitoring tools must not be infinitely expanded due to remote work; the purpose, scope and viewers should be explained, and employee notice or consent records retained.

12. Termination of Employment, Compensation and Offboarding Settlement

Before termination, confirm in sequence the contract type, termination facts, statutory reason, employee protected status, investigation and defense procedure, notice period, compensation, EOSB, unused annual leave and right to work consequences. When an employer terminates an open-ended monthly-paid employee, generally at least 60 days' notice is required; employee resignation generally requires at least 30 days. Unlawful early termination of a fixed-term contract may involve wages for the remaining term, and the applicable minimum compensation and contract agreement still need to be verified case by case.

Article 80 immediate termination only applies to strict statutory situations. The employer should investigate, retain evidence and give the employee an opportunity to respond, and cannot directly apply it because of client request, poor performance or project end. After resignation is submitted, the employer's failure to respond within 30 days is generally deemed acceptance; the employer may in writing postpone acceptance for business reasons, generally up to 60 days; the employee may generally withdraw within 7 days if acceptance has not been received.

EOSB is generally calculated on the last wage: half a month's wage per year for the first 5 years, one month's wage per year for the portion exceeding 5 years, pro-rated for less than one year. On employee resignation, generally 1/3 is obtained for 2 to 5 consecutive years of service, 2/3 for more than 5 but less than 10 years, and the full amount at 10 years; specific exceptions and wage base must be verified.

Example: Employee's last monthly wage SAR 12,500, 7 consecutive years of service, lawfully terminated by employer, with 10 days of unused annual leave and one month of unpaid wages. EOSB is SAR 31,250 for the first 5 years, plus SAR 25,000 for the next 2 years; unused annual leave on a simple 30-day monthly basis is SAR 4,166.70; final wage SAR 12,500, totaling approximately SAR 72,916.70. Actual settlement should also include bonuses, expenses, notice compensation, legality of deductions and more favorable contract terms.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS (FAQ)

What is the 2026 Saudi Arabia private sector minimum wage?
Saudi Arabia has no uniform nationwide statutory minimum wage applicable to all private sector employees. SAR 4,000 is generally the wage threshold at which Saudi employees receive full Nitaqat counting and should not be called the nationwide minimum wage; industry, occupational and localization decisions may have other requirements.

What is the employer GOSI rate for Saudi employees?
It cannot be answered with one rate. Existing system pension is generally 9% each for employer and employee; the new system is 10% each from July 1, 2026, plus 0.75% each for SANED and generally 2% work injury insurance borne by the employer. The employee's GOSI system category and contributory wage should be confirmed first.

Do foreign employees need to contribute to GOSI?
Foreign employees generally do not participate in pension and SANED, so there is no corresponding employee deduction; the employer generally still bears work injury insurance and must configure applicable medical insurance. Actual registration is based on the GOSI system and employee status.

How many days of annual leave do Saudi Arabian employees have per year?
Generally not less than 21 days per year; not less than 30 days after 5 consecutive years with the same employer. Less than one year should accrue by service period, and accrued but unused balance should be settled upon separation.

What is the maximum probationary period?
Probation must be clearly written into the contract and generally must not exceed 180 days in total. Companies should also correctly handle holidays or sick leave statutorily excluded from probation, and record probation evaluation and termination in writing.

Does Saudi Arabia levy personal income tax on wages?
Saudi Arabia currently does not levy personal income tax on individual wages, so payslips generally have no personal income tax withholding; this does not exclude other employee or corporate tax, cross-border tax and permanent establishment risks.

Can employees still receive end-of-service benefits upon resignation?
Possibly. Generally 1/3 of EOSB is obtained for 2 to 5 years of service, 2/3 for more than 5 but less than 10 years, and the full amount at 10 years; the specific amount also depends on the last wage, service period, termination facts and statutory exceptions.

Can employees be hired without a Saudi entity?
Companies may assess a compliant Employer of Record (EOR), but must confirm the service provider structure, Qiwa contract, GOSI, WPS, medical insurance, Saudization impact, right to work, client management boundary and termination responsibility case by case. EOR does not equal exemption from labor law and immigration requirements.
