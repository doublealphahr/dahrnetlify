Employment Contracts, Wage Taxes, Leave and Termination Compliance

2026 POLICY UPDATE

Minimum Wage Increased: From July 1, 2026, the national minimum gross basic wage rises to RON 4,325/month. Wage changes must also be reported within the deadlines set by REGES-ONLINE.

RON 200 Tax Exemption Measure: From July to December 2026, full-time employees meeting conditions such as the minimum wage and income ceiling may have up to RON 200 per month excluded from income tax and mandatory social security bases.

Sick Leave Benefit Reduced by One Day: For relevant sick leave certificates issued between February 1, 2026 and December 31, 2027, a temporary rule reducing the benefit by one working day applies.

Romania has a nationally uniform statutory minimum gross basic wage, and some industries may apply a higher wage floor. Employee wages, working time, overtime, leave, social insurance and termination of employment generally need to be determined based on the Labor Code, applicable collective agreements and industry rules. Before quoting or issuing a job offer, Chinese companies should first confirm the employee's start date, actual position, work location, industry of the enterprise, contract type and collective agreement applicability, and must not treat old minimum wages, market salaries or position examples as a uniform statutory standard.

This guide is intended for HR, finance, legal and overseas business teams of Chinese companies recruiting, signing contracts, running payroll, calculating employer costs, using Employer of Record (EOR) services or handling performance and separation in Romania. The Chinese data is organized according to 2026 rules; specific industry wage floors, collective agreements, individual employee tax, cross-border social security, foreign employee right to work and high-risk terminations still need to be confirmed case by case.

2. Three Rules to Watch Most in 2026

Minimum Wage Increased in July

From July 1, 2026, the national minimum gross basic wage increased from RON 4,050 to RON 4,325/month, which translates to RON 25.949/hour based on an average of 166.667 hours. This amount excludes allowances, overtime pay and other additions; special industries such as construction should also verify a higher floor.

RON 200 Conditional Tax Exemption

From July to December 2026, full-time minimum wage employees meeting the conditions of OUG No. 89/2025 may have up to RON 200 per month excluded from income tax and mandatory social security bases. This generally requires the contract basic wage to equal the statutory minimum wage and the monthly gross income not to exceed RON 4,600; it cannot be treated as a wage subsidy deductible by the employer.

Sick Leave Benefit Temporarily Reduced by One Day

For relevant sick leave certificates issued between February 1, 2026 and December 31, 2027, the temporary rule of "reducing the benefit by one working day" applies to sick leave allowance. Payroll teams need to calculate separately by sick leave code, course of illness and paying entity, and should not treat it as ordinary full-pay leave.

3. Romanian Labor Law and Regulatory Framework

Labor relations are mainly governed by the Labor Code (Law No. 53/2003), the Fiscal Code, social dialogue rules and occupational health and safety regulations. The Labor Inspectorate is responsible for labor compliance and REGES-ONLINE supervision, ANAF is responsible for payroll tax reporting, CNAS is responsible for health insurance and the sick leave system, and ANOFM is responsible for public employment services.

Applicable collective bargaining agreements may provide rights higher than the statutory minimum in wages, allowances, working time, leave or termination conditions. Companies should first confirm the contracting employer, work location, position classification and applicable CBA, then determine the uniform template and payroll scheme.

4. Recruitment, Job Offer and Onboarding

Recruitment criteria should be relevant to the genuine requirements of the position, and must not improperly screen based on gender, age, ethnicity, religion, disability or family status or other protected characteristics. The offer should clarify gross wage and basic wage, fixed allowances, bonuses or commissions, work location, remote ratio, working hours, probationary period, contract term and start date.

Before the employee starts work, the written contract, occupational health examination, HSE training and necessary data privacy notice should be completed. The prescribed data of the employment contract should generally be entered into REGES-ONLINE no later than the day before work starts, and the system receipt should be retained. Work permits and residence for non-EU candidates should be verified separately before onboarding.

5. Employment Contracts, Contract Types and Probationary Period

Open-ended contracts are the default form. Fixed-term contracts may only be used in temporary or objective situations permitted by law, with a single contract generally up to 36 months; the same parties may sign at most 3 consecutive fixed-term contracts, and renewal within 3 months after the end of the previous contract is generally deemed continuous, with subsequent contracts generally up to 12 months. Part-time contracts must state working hours and scheduling in writing, and in principle overtime must not be arranged.

The probationary period is up to 90 calendar days for executive positions and up to 120 calendar days for management positions; shorter or tiered caps apply to employees with disabilities and fixed-term employees. The probationary period must be written into the formal contract. During probation, either party may generally terminate with written notice without ordinary notice period, but must still settle wages, unused annual leave, earned bonuses and reimbursements, and exclude discrimination or retaliation factors.

6. Wages, Minimum Wage and Gross-to-Net Calculation

Wages must be paid at least once a month. From January to June 2026, the national minimum gross basic wage is RON 4,050/month, and from July 1 it is RON 4,325/month. The current special floor for construction is RON 4,582/month; before applying, the enterprise activity code, position and latest official interpretation must be verified.

Under the same contract, the national minimum wage may in principle be paid for at most 24 consecutive months, after which the basic wage must be higher than the then-current minimum wage.

13th month pay, year-end bonuses, meal vouchers and private medical care are not statutory benefits uniformly applicable to all employers; they should be included in the fixed budget only when a CBA, contract, company policy or established practice creates an obligation.

7. Working Time, Overtime and Records

Standard working time is generally 8 hours/day, 40 hours/week; average working time including overtime generally must not exceed 48 hours/week, usually calculated over a 4-month reference period. Employees are generally entitled to at least 12 consecutive hours of daily rest and 48 consecutive hours of weekly rest. Employers must record each employee's daily start and end times.

Overtime should preferably be compensated with equivalent paid rest within 90 calendar days; when compensatory rest is not possible, at least a 75% basic wage premium must be paid.

The night period is 22:00 to 06:00, and qualifying night workers may enjoy reduced working hours or at least a 25% premium. Work on statutory holidays should be compensated with rest within 30 days, and when compensatory rest is not possible, at least a 100% basic wage premium must be paid.

8. Statutory Holidays, Annual Leave and Other Statutory Leave

Employees are entitled to at least 20 working days of annual leave per year, accrued according to the actual employment period. Annual leave pay is generally calculated based on the daily average of basic wages and permanent additions over the most recent 3 months, and paid no later than 5 working days before the leave begins.

Annual leave not taken for justified reasons should be scheduled within 18 months from the following year; it may not be cashed out during employment, and the unused balance may only be settled upon termination of the employment relationship.

Holidays falling on weekends generally do not automatically carry over; although June 1 corresponds to two holidays, it does not create two working days of leave.

Maternity leave is generally 126 calendar days; paternity leave is generally 10 working days, increased by 5 days upon completion of a parenting course; parental leave is generally available until the child is 2 years old, and may be extended for children with disabilities. Sick leave, carer's leave and urgent family absence must be handled item by item according to eligibility, certificates, payment ratios and REGES registration requirements.

Employers generally complete monthly payroll tax reporting through D112. When part-time wages are below the full-time minimum wage, a minimum social security base top-up may be triggered, and whether exempted should be determined by the payroll team for the month.

Employers with 50 or more employees generally also need to check the 4% disability employment quota or alternative contribution mechanism.

10. Local Employees and Foreign Employees

Local employees, EU/EEA/Swiss citizens and non-EU employees in principle all enjoy the minimum protection of Romanian labor law, but the right to work documents differ. Non-EU employees generally need the corresponding work permit and residence qualification; the permit is often tied to the employer, position and work location, and change or separation may affect status.

Companies should verify nationality, existing residence purpose, permit validity, whether the employer can be changed and expected start date before the offer. Cross-border posted employees should also determine A1 or other social security coverage certificates, tax residency and permanent establishment risks.

Foreign status must not be used to lower minimum wage, working time, leave or occupational safety standards.

11. Remote Work, Data Privacy and Employee Records

Remote work should be agreed in writing in the contract or an additional agreement pursuant to Telework Law No. 81/2018, specifying the remote location, days, working time inspection method, equipment, costs, occupational safety and data security. Working from home does not exempt the employer from daily working time records and HSE responsibilities.

Employee data is protected by GDPR and Law No. 190/2018. Health, disciplinary, performance and family data should follow the principles of purpose limitation and minimization, with access permissions and retention periods.

Before transferring data to China or other non-EEA regions, an appropriate cross-border transfer mechanism and security measures must be established.

12. Termination of Employment, Compensation and Offboarding Settlement

Employers cannot dismiss without reason simply by paying notice period wages. Redundancy must be based on a genuine, effective and serious position elimination; unsuitability, medical or disciplinary dismissal each have independent substantive grounds and procedures.

Redundancy, unsuitability or medical reason termination generally requires at least 20 working days' notice; disciplinary dismissal generally has no ordinary notice period, but must complete investigation, defense and written decision procedures.

Employee resignation requires no reason, with notice period of up to 20 working days for executive positions and up to 45 working days for management positions. Fixed-term contracts may lawfully terminate on expiry, and early termination must use a statutory path.

Romania has no uniform statutory severance pay applicable to all ordinary employees; severance compensation depends on law, CBA, contract or social plan.

Collective redundancy is generally determined by headcount within 30 days:

  • Enterprises with 21–99 employees: at least 10 dismissals;

  • Enterprises with 100–299 employees: at least 10%;

  • Enterprises with 300 or more employees: at least 30 dismissals.

Once collective redundancy rules are triggered, procedures involving employee representatives or unions, the labor inspectorate and employment agencies must be followed. Offboarding settlement should include final wages, unused annual leave, earned commissions or bonuses, expense reimbursements and agreed severance.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS (FAQ)

What is the 2026 Romanian minimum wage?
From January to June 2026 it is RON 4,050/month; from July 1 it is RON 4,325/month. This amount is the gross basic wage and excludes allowances and other additions; special industries such as construction must check a higher floor separately.

Is 13th month pay mandatory under Romanian law?
There is no uniform statutory 13th month pay applicable to all private sector employers. The employer must pay only when an applicable CBA, employment contract, bonus policy or binding practice provides otherwise.

How many days of annual leave do Romanian employees have per year?
The statutory minimum is 20 working days, accrued according to the actual service period. Contracts or CBAs may provide more, but cannot be below the statutory minimum.

Can an employer dismiss an employee on the same day during probation?
Either party may generally terminate during a valid probationary period with written notice and no ordinary notice period, but it cannot be verbal, evidence of delivery must be retained, discrimination or retaliation excluded, and offboarding settlement completed.

Does Romania have a uniform statutory severance pay?
There is no nationally uniform general statutory severance pay for ordinary employees. Whether and how much to pay depends on applicable law, CBA, employment contract, social plan or mutual agreement; lawful termination grounds, notice and procedure must still be satisfied.

Can employees be hired without a Romanian entity?
Companies may assess hiring through a compliant EOR arrangement, but must confirm the local contracting employer, temporary agency regulation, position management, work permit and tax risks case by case. A commercial EOR label cannot replace local legal analysis.

Do remote employees still need working time records?
Yes. Remote work does not exempt the employer from working time records, occupational safety and data protection obligations, and the remote location, equipment, costs and supervision method should also be agreed in writing.
