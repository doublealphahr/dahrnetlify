Minimum Wage, Social Security, Leave, Termination and EOR Compliance

2026 POLICY UPDATE

Minimum Wage Increased: From January 1, 2026, Turkey's net minimum wage is adjusted to TRY 22,104/month. Employers should update payroll, contracts, and cost budgets accordingly, and verify the gross minimum wage and applicable social security base.

Social Security Contribution Rates: The 2026 employer social security contribution rate remains generally 20.5% (including 2% short-term insurance), and the employee contribution rate remains 14% (including 1% short-term insurance). Employers should verify applicable rates and ceilings.

Severance Pay Ceiling Updated: The 2026 statutory severance pay ceiling is adjusted. Employers should verify the current ceiling for calculating severance pay for eligible employees.

Hiring employees in Turkey means companies cannot rely on a single minimum wage or a fixed employer cost percentage. Turkey applies a national minimum wage, but social security, unemployment insurance, income tax, severance pay, and termination procedures all require separate calculation. Companies should first confirm the employee's work location, contract type, wage structure, and employee nationality, then configure payroll and compliance accordingly.

This guide is intended for HR, finance, legal and overseas business teams of Chinese companies recruiting, signing contracts, running payroll, calculating employer costs, using Employer of Record (EOR) services or handling employee separations in Turkey. Amounts in this document are expressed in Turkish lira (TRY); specific social security rates, severance pay ceilings, work permits, and foreign employee right to work still need to be confirmed case by case.

3. Turkish Labor Law and Regulatory Framework

Turkish labor relations are mainly governed by the Labor Code No. 4857, the Social Insurance and Universal Health Insurance Law No. 5510, the Unemployment Insurance Law No. 4447, the Income Tax Law No. 193, and collective agreements. The Ministry of Labour and Social Security is responsible for labor protection, the Social Security Institution administers social security, and the Revenue Administration administers tax.

Collective agreements and company work rules may set higher standards for wages, working hours, overtime, leave, and termination. Companies should confirm the applicable rules based on industry, company size, and work location.

The contracting employer bears responsibility for employment contracts, payroll, social security, tax, working environment, leave, and termination. Clients or group managers may provide business input but cannot bypass the Turkish employer to directly discipline or dismiss employees.

6. Wages, Minimum Wage and Gross-to-Net Calculation

Turkey applies a national minimum wage. Companies should verify the current gross minimum wage and calculate net minimum wage accordingly. The 2026 net minimum wage is TRY 22,104/month.

10. Local Employees and Foreign Employees

Local and foreign employees in principle enjoy the same labor law protections. Foreign employees must have a work permit consistent with the employer, position, and work location. EOR arrangements do not automatically resolve work permit issues.

Cross-border remote work may trigger tax, social security, permanent establishment, and data protection issues. Employers should assess case by case.

11. Remote Work, Data Privacy and Employee Records

Remote work should be agreed in writing, specifying work location, equipment, expenses, working hours, and data security. The Personal Data Protection Law (KVKK) applies to employee data processing.

Employee records should be retained per statutory requirements and include contracts, payslips, working time, leave, and termination documents.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

What is the minimum wage in Turkey in 2026?
The net minimum wage is TRY 22,104/month from January 1, 2026. The gross minimum wage is higher and varies by calculation; companies should verify the current gross minimum wage.

What is the employer social security rate in Turkey?
The employer social security contribution rate is generally 20.5% of gross wages (including 2% short-term insurance), and the employer unemployment insurance contribution is 2%.

How many days of annual leave do Turkish employees have?
At least 14 working days for 1–5 years of service; 20 days for 5–15 years; 26 days for 15+ years.

How is severance pay calculated in Turkey?
Severance pay is generally 30 days' gross wage per year of service, subject to the statutory ceiling. Employers should pay within 30 days of termination.

Can I use an EOR to hire in Turkey?
Yes, but you must verify the contracting employer, work permit, social security registration, and actual management boundaries. EOR does not automatically resolve all compliance issues.
