LQS, CPF, Leave, Working Hours and Termination Compliance

2026 POLICY UPDATE

CPF Rates and Wage Ceiling Adjusted: From January 1, new CPF rates apply by age band, and the Ordinary Wage (OW) monthly ceiling rises to SGD 8,000.

Shared Parental Leave Increased to 10 Weeks: From April 1, eligible parents may share 10 weeks of Shared Parental Leave; companies should update leave and reimbursement processes.

Local Qualifying Salary Increased: From July 1, the Local Qualifying Salary (LQS) threshold for full-time local employees rises to SGD 1,800 per month, affecting local employee counting and foreign employee quotas.

Singapore has a mature labor law, Central Provident Fund (CPF) and payroll reporting system, making it suitable for Chinese companies to build regional teams or conduct local recruitment. However, when setting salaries and headcount budgets, companies need to separately assess the Local Qualifying Salary (LQS), Progressive Wage Model (PWM), CPF, foreign employee quotas, working hours and offboarding settlement, and cannot use one fixed percentage to cover all scenarios.

This guide is intended for companies planning to recruit, onboard, pay salaries, manage leave or terminate employment in Singapore. Amounts in this document are expressed in Singapore dollars (SGD); Employment Pass (EP), S Pass and Work Permit matters for foreign employees are only mentioned as employment compliance reminders, and specific application eligibility still needs to be confirmed case by case under permit rules.

The key to Singapore employment compliance is not to find a single "minimum wage" or "social security rate," but to first identify employee status, age, position, industry, wage composition and work permit, then separately calculate wages, CPF, Skills Development Levy (SDL), insurance, leave and termination costs.

2. Three Rules Most in Need of Update in 2026

2.1 CPF Rates and Ordinary Wage Ceiling

From January 1, 2026, Singapore citizens and Singapore permanent residents on full rates contribute CPF according to the new age bands. Employees aged 55 and below remain at employer 17%, employee 20%; age bands above 55 apply the corresponding new rates.

The 2026 Ordinary Wage (OW) monthly ceiling is SGD 8,000. Ordinary wages above the ceiling no longer increase CPF monthly, but Additional Wages (AW) such as bonuses still need to be calculated separately with the additional wage ceiling and the annual CPF ceiling.

2.2 Shared Parental Leave Increased to 10 Weeks

Where the child's date of birth, estimated delivery date or formal adoption intent date is on or after April 1, 2026, eligible working parents may share 10 weeks of Shared Parental Leave.

It generally requires the child to be a Singapore citizen, and the employee to have served the employer continuously for at least 3 months before the relevant date. Employers should update leave policies, application and scheduling mechanisms, and distinguish the employer's advance payment of wages from the government reimbursement process.

2.3 Local Qualifying Salary (LQS) Increased to SGD 1,800

From July 1, 2026, a full-time Singapore citizen or permanent resident earning SGD 1,800/month can count as 1 local employee for foreign employee quota; those earning SGD 900 but below SGD 1,800 generally count as only 0.5.

LQS is a local employee counting threshold, not a nationwide minimum wage applicable to all positions. Companies also need to separately determine whether employees are covered by the Progressive Wage Model (PWM) or Occupational Progressive Wages (OPW).

Singapore labor rules are generally uniform nationwide, but PWM, OPW and foreign employee quotas vary by industry and occupation.

Common white-collar benefits also include supplementary medical, outpatient, dental, flexible benefits, performance bonuses or Annual Wage Supplement (AWS, often called "13th month pay"), but AWS and bonuses are not statutory mandatory items for all employees and should be based on contract, company policy or established practice.

4. Recruitment, Job Offer and Onboarding

Companies can recruit through platforms such as MyCareersFuture, JobStreet Singapore, JobsDB Singapore, LinkedIn Jobs, eFinancialCareers or Glints.

Recruitment advertisements should accurately state job duties, work location, salary range, working hours and lawful right to work requirements, and avoid improper restrictions based on age, gender, marital or parental status, race, religion or nationality. If language or professional qualifications are necessary conditions, a reasonable business justification should be retained.

Background checks should match the position risk, and the purpose should be explained when collecting candidate documents and personal data. After onboarding, companies should also establish itemized payslips, employee files, CPF and tax materials, and set up equipment, system access and information security processes.

Singapore law allows a probationary period, but there is no uniform statutory maximum, and market practice is commonly 3 to 6 months. Probation, evaluation criteria, notice period and confirmation mechanism should be written into the contract and Key Employment Terms.

Probation does not mean the employer can dismiss at will. Termination decisions involving discrimination, retaliation, pregnancy, statutory leave or exercise of statutory rights may still constitute wrongful dismissal.

6. Wages, Minimum Wage and Gross-to-Net Calculation

Singapore has no nationwide ordinary minimum wage. When setting salaries, companies should determine in sequence whether the employee is covered by PWM or OPW, whether they need to be counted for foreign employee quota, whether they are protected by Part IV working time rules, and the treatment of each pay component in CPF, overtime and offboarding settlement.

Wages must be paid at least once a month, and ordinary wages are generally paid within 7 days after the end of the wage period; applicable overtime is generally paid within 14 days after the end of the wage period.

Wage deductions must have legal basis or valid employee consent, and wages should not be reduced through opaque fines. All employees covered by the Employment Act should receive itemized payslips, and wage records should be retained.

Assume a 35-year-old Singapore citizen working as an operations specialist, monthly salary SGD 8,000, all of which is Ordinary Wage (OW), with no bonus or other additional wages:

Employee CPF—SGD 1,600.0020% × SGD 8,000

Singapore generally does not have the employer withhold personal income tax monthly; employees generally pay based on IRAS tax assessment. Therefore, SGD 6,400 in the table is only the amount after deducting employee CPF, not the full after-tax take-home wage.
