Minimum Wage, IMSS, Working Hours, Leave and Termination

2026 POLICY UPDATE

Minimum Wage Increased from January 1
The general region minimum wage is adjusted to MXN 315.04/day, and the Northern Border Free Zone to MXN 440.87/day. Enterprises should simultaneously update offers, contracts, payroll and occupational minimum wage verification processes.

UMA Updated from February 1
In 2026, UMA is adjusted to MXN 117.31/day, MXN 3,566.22/month and MXN 42,794.64/year, affecting social security ceilings, fines and multiple statutory calculations.

Working Hours Reform Launches Phased Transition
In 2026, the weekly working hours cap remains 48 hours, and is expected to gradually decrease from 2027 to 46, 44, 42 hours and reach 40 hours in 2030. Enterprises should complete scheduling and personnel cost simulations in advance.

Mexico's labor compliance is jointly composed of the Federal Labor Law, social security, housing fund, income tax, local payroll tax and industry rules. For Chinese enterprises, the real cost is not just monthly salary, but also IMSS, INFONAVIT, year-end bonus, paid annual leave, vacation premium, profit sharing and potential departure compensation.

The most important annual parameters for 2026 include the two-tier minimum wage, the new UMA effective in February, the updated social security filing tool, and the reform that has taken effect but phases in a reduction of the weekly working hours cap. Enterprises should implement these changes simultaneously in offers, contracts, attendance, payroll calculation and cost budgets.

2. Three Rules Most in Need of Attention in 2026

Two-tier Minimum Wage Increased Simultaneously
In 2026, the general region minimum wage is MXN 315.04/day, and the Northern Border Free Zone is MXN 440.87/day. Using 30.4 days as a budget reference, this is approximately MXN 9,582.47/month and MXN 13,409.80/month, but wage compliance should still be based on the applicable daily wage, work location and occupational minimum wage table. Mexico also has 61 occupational minimum wages, and the national base standard cannot be the only check.

UMA and Social Security Parameters Updated
From February 1, 2026, UMA is MXN 117.31/day. It is used for multiple social security ceilings, fines and statutory calculations, but cannot replace minimum wage payment to employees. The IMSS contribution wage ceiling is usually 25 UMA, i.e., MXN 2,932.75/day. SUA 3.7.1 has been enabled since March 2, 2026, and employers should upgrade the system and verify historical cumulative data.

Weekly Working Hours Reform Enters Transition Period
The reform has taken effect in 2026, but the statutory weekly cap in 2026 remains 48 hours. The transition arrangement is expected to be 46 hours in 2027, 44 in 2028, 42 in 2029 and 40 in 2030. Enterprises should not mistakenly write 2026 as 40 hours in advance, and should immediately assess future scheduling, headcount, overtime and production costs.

3. Mexico Labor Law and Regulatory Framework

Mexican private sector employment relationships are mainly governed by the Federal Labor Law (LFT). The Mexican Social Security Institute (IMSS) is responsible for social insurance registration and contributions, the National Workers' Housing Fund Institute (INFONAVIT) manages the housing fund, and the Tax Administration Service (SAT) is responsible for payroll income tax, electronic payroll vouchers and other tax matters.

Labor rights are highly mandatory. When contract terms, company policies or foreign parent company rules are below statutory standards, they usually cannot exclude employee rights. Collective agreements, occupational minimum wages, local payroll taxes and the employee's actual work location may also change the result.

Employment disputes usually must first go through labor conciliation and then enter the labor court. Enterprises should keep contracts, payroll vouchers, attendance, leave, performance, disciplinary procedures, social security filings and termination delivery records; only headquarters emails or English internal documents are often insufficient to support local dispute handling.

4. Recruitment, Offer and Onboarding

The offer should clearly state the position, work location, wage currency and cycle, benefits, expected start date, contract term and necessary background or work permit conditions. Recruitment rhetoric cannot be used to circumvent statutory benefits, nor should a net wage promise be written as a fixed amount that cannot be stably calculated.

At onboarding, identity and tax information are usually collected, RFC, CURP, bank and IMSS related processes are completed, and a Spanish or bilingual employment contract, job description, privacy notice and company policy are signed. Foreign employees also need to obtain appropriate immigration and work authorization before starting work.

Employers should complete IMSS registration in a timely manner. Late reporting, underreporting or mistakenly excluding wage remuneration from contribution wages may lead to supplementary contributions, updates, fines and employee benefit disputes.

5. Employment Contracts, Contract Types and Probation Period

Indefinite-term contracts are the common form. Fixed-term or specific project contracts should have a genuine, provable temporary reason; repeatedly renewing fixed-term contracts merely for convenience of dismissal may be deemed an indefinite-term relationship.

The written contract should usually include both parties' identities, service content, work location, working hours, wages, payment method, rest days, leave, training and other agreements. If a written contract is missing, the employment relationship and statutory rights do not disappear, and the burden of proof risk shifts more toward the employer.

The probation period for general positions is maximum 30 days, and may be extended to 180 days for management, administrative leadership, professional or specialized technical positions. The initial training period is usually maximum 3 months, and up to 6 months for certain senior or professional positions. Probation and initial training should not be stacked, repeated or signed afterward; arrangements and evaluation criteria should be documented.

6. Wages, Minimum Wage and Pre-tax Take-home Pay Calculation

Wages may be paid weekly, biweekly or monthly, but the cycle must comply with the position and local rules. Payslips are usually recorded through CFDI electronic payroll vouchers, and employers should ensure consistency between contracts, bank payments, CFDI, IMSS contribution wages and general ledger data.

Pre-tax wages do not equal employee take-home pay. The general calculation framework is:

Take-home pay = Taxable wages and wage benefits − Employee IMSS contributions − ISR withholding − Lawfully deductible items

The social security contribution wage (SBC) may include the integrated value of fixed wages and accrued benefits. When using the statutory minimum 15 days Aguinaldo, 12 days annual leave and 25% vacation premium, the common minimum integration factor in the first year is approximately 1.049315, but the actual factor will change with benefits, seniority and variable remuneration.

Minimum wage and UMA serve different functions. Minimum wage is used for the wage floor and some labor law calculations, while UMA is mainly used for administrative, social security and other statutory benchmarks. Payroll must not mistake UMA for the employee wage floor.

7. Working Hours, Overtime and Records

In 2026, the day shift is usually maximum 8 hours, night shift 7 hours and mixed shift 7.5 hours; the weekly cap remains 48 hours. Employees should receive statutory rest during continuous work, and scheduling must also meet the weekly rest day requirement.

The first 9 hours of statutory overtime per week are usually paid at 2 times the normal hourly wage; the portion exceeding the statutory limit is usually paid at 3 times, and may constitute a scheduling violation. Sunday work usually has an additional Sunday premium of at least 25%. Work on statutory rest days or mandatory holidays also generates different additional payment rules.

Enterprises should record actual start, end, rest, overtime approval and leave through a reliable system. "Salary already includes all overtime" in the contract or a manager title cannot automatically eliminate the employee's overtime rights.

The presidential inauguration day is not a 2026 holiday; election days officially announced by law may apply separately. When employees work on mandatory rest days, in addition to normal rest day wages, they usually should receive double wages.

Employees are entitled to at least 12 days of annual leave after completing the first service year, then increasing to 14, 16, 18 and 20 days; after the sixth year, it usually increases by 2 days for every additional 5 years of service. In addition to annual leave wages, a vacation premium of at least 25% must also be paid.

Maternity leave usually covers the statutory prenatal and postnatal period and may be adjusted when eligible; fathers usually enjoy 5 working days of paid paternity leave. Adoption, nursing, occupational injury and illness and other leave should be handled according to specific laws and IMSS rules.

Mexico does not have a single employer social security percentage applicable to all employees. Employers should also budget at least 15 days' wages of Aguinaldo, annual leave, 25% vacation premium and possibly applicable PTU. PTU usually establishes an employee distribution pool at 10% of distributable profits, with an individual cap generally at the higher of 3 months' wages or the average PTU of the past 3 years; it is not a fixed 10% wage increase for each employee.

Local payroll tax varies by state. For example, Mexico City's regular payroll tax rate in 2026 is 4%. Therefore, total employment cost must be calculated in conjunction with the employee's work location, entity location and specific tax base.

This example excludes PTU, additional benefits, service fees and one-time costs.

10. Local Employees and Expatriate or Foreign Employees

Foreign employees working in Mexico usually need immigration status and work authorization matching the actual activity. A clear distinction should be made between visits, business meetings and actually providing labor services; Mexican work authorization cannot be ignored merely because the overseas contract or wages are paid from overseas.

Local labor law may apply to employees actually working in Mexico. Enterprises also need to analyze tax residency, permanent establishment, shadow payroll, social security coverage and home country benefit coordination. Travel allowances, housing, tuition, tax equalization and exchange rate protection should be handled consistently in the assignment policy and payroll system.

Labor law may set restrictions on the proportion of Mexican employees and certain technical positions. Before introducing foreign professionals, the industry, position, knowledge transfer and localization requirements should be verified.

11. Remote Work, Data Privacy and Employee Records

When an employee works at their residence or away from the employer's premises for a statutory threshold of time, it may constitute formal remote work rather than occasional home work. Employers may need to provide and maintain necessary equipment, bear reasonable electricity and communication costs, and implement occupational health and safety and the right to disconnect.

Remote arrangements should be written into the contract or a supplementary agreement, clarifying work location, equipment, expenses, information security, attendance, occupational risk reporting and return-to-office mechanisms. Employee relocation across states or borders may change payroll tax, social security, immigration and corporate tax risks, and cannot be approved only verbally by the direct manager.

Employee personal data should be collected and processed in accordance with Mexican data protection rules. Enterprises need to provide a privacy notice, restrict access rights, and set appropriate retention and deletion rules for recruitment materials, payroll, health information, investigations and departure files.

12. Termination of Employment, Compensation and Final Settlement

Any departure usually requires payment of finiquito, i.e., wages up to the termination date, unused annual leave, vacation premium, proportional Aguinaldo and other accrued entitlements. Voluntary resignation does not mean no settlement is required.

When terminating for serious employee misconduct, the employer must fall within statutory grounds, investigate promptly and explain the facts and dates in a compliant written notice. Insufficient procedure or evidence may result in being deemed dismissal without just cause, even if the internal finding is that a problem exists.

Dismissal without just cause usually involves 3 months' wages. 20 days' wages per year of service is not automatically paid for every dismissal; its application depends on the claim, termination path, judgment or settlement. Seniority compensation is usually calculated at 12 days per year, and is more commonly applied when the employer terminates; voluntary resignation usually requires 15 years of seniority to apply, and there is a daily wage cap measured in multiples of minimum wage.

Back pay compensation in disputes is usually limited to 12 months, after which interest may accrue at 2% per month on a 15-month wage base. Before termination, local lawyers or labor experts should review the facts, protected status, notice, conciliation strategy and complete cost.

Mexico has strict restrictions on personnel outsourcing. Enterprises cannot simply hand core employees to ordinary suppliers for "payroll agency" to circumvent employer obligations. When professional services or shared services are involved, the supplier registration, service nature and whether it belongs to the client's core business should be verified.

When choosing EOR, confirm who signs the contract, performs IMSS registration, issues CFDI, pays PTU, manages disciplinary procedures and bears termination costs. Clients can conduct daily business collaboration but should avoid direct employer management behavior that conflicts with the contract structure.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS (FAQ)

What is the minimum wage in Mexico in 2026?
The general region is MXN 315.04/day, and the Northern Border Free Zone is MXN 440.87/day, effective from January 1, 2026. Specific positions also need to check the occupational minimum wage.

Has Mexico's statutory weekly working hours already been reduced to 40 hours in 2026?
No. Although the reform took effect in 2026, the weekly cap in 2026 remains 48 hours; it is expected to be reduced in phases from 2027 and reach 40 hours in 2030.

What is the Mexican employer social security rate?
There is no single percentage applicable to all employees. Employer cost depends on SBC, UMA, wage tier, work risk class and IMSS insurance types, and the employer usually also bears 5% INFONAVIT and local payroll tax.

Is year-end bonus mandatory in Mexico?
Yes. Aguinaldo is at least 15 days' wages and usually must be paid before December 20; employees with less than one year of service receive it pro-rata.

How many days of annual leave do Mexican employees have?
At least 12 days after completing the first service year, then increasing by statutory ladder according to seniority; in addition to annual leave wages, a vacation premium of at least 25% must also be paid.

Does PTU equal 10% of each employee's wage?
No. Eligible enterprises usually establish an employee distribution pool at 10% of distributable profits, then distribute according to legal rules, with individual caps and statutory exemptions applying.

How long is the probation period in Mexico?
General positions maximum 30 days; management, professional or specialized technical positions up to 180 days. Probation must be agreed in writing and cannot be repeatedly set or stacked with the initial training period.

Can employees be hired without a Mexican entity?
Enterprises can assess compliant Employer of Record (EOR) services, but must confirm case by case the lawful employer, personnel outsourcing restrictions, IMSS and CFDI responsibilities, actual management boundaries, PTU and termination costs.
