Minimum Wage, 14 Salaries, Social Insurance and Termination Compliance

2026 POLICY UPDATE

Minimum wage rises to EUR 920 — From April 1, 2026, the minimum monthly salary for white-collar workers rises to EUR 920 and the minimum daily wage for blue-collar workers to EUR 41.09. Employers should update contracts and payroll parameters.

Statutory special payment costs rise accordingly — The minimum wage increase also affects Christmas, Easter and holiday allowances and applicable seniority increments. Companies should recalculate the full annual 14-salary cost.

Digital work time card continues to expand — In 2026, the digital work time card continues to expand to more sectors. Covered companies should ensure consistency between clock-in/out records, ERGANI declarations, payslips and social insurance data.

Employment costs in the Greek private sector cannot be calculated based only on 12 months of basic salary. In addition to normal monthly salary, eligible employees are entitled to Christmas allowance, Easter allowance and holiday allowance, which in a full year typically form the so-called "14 salaries" structure. Before issuing an offer letter, Chinese companies should confirm whether the employee is white-collar or blue-collar, the applicable minimum wage, seniority increments, collective agreement, working time system and social insurance category.

This guide is intended for HR, finance, legal and overseas business teams of Chinese companies recruiting, contracting, running payroll, calculating employer costs, using Employer of Record (EOR) services or handling employee departures in Greece. The content is organized according to 2026 rules; specific collective agreements, digital work time cards, insurance codes, right to work, protected status and high-risk terminations still need to be confirmed case by case.

2. Three Rules to Watch Most in 2026

Minimum wage increases from April 1

From April 1, 2026, the full-time minimum monthly salary for white-collar workers rises from EUR 880 to EUR 920, and the minimum daily wage for blue-collar workers rises from EUR 39.30 to EUR 41.09. Pay periods crossing the effective date should be handled in segments according to the official payroll basis, and employers should also update part-time wages, seniority increments, statutory special payments and social insurance costs.

The statutory "14 salaries" must be included in the annual budget

The Christmas allowance, Easter allowance and holiday allowance for private-sector salaried employees in Greece are statutory obligations, not market practice or voluntary bonuses. A full year typically amounts to 2 months of pay in addition to 12 months of salary; when onboarding, departure or unpaid absence results in an incomplete qualifying period, the pro-rata amount should be calculated separately for each statutory period.

Digital work time card and declarations must be consistent

In 2026, the digital work time card continues to expand to more sectors and employees. Covered companies should ensure that actual clock-in/out, rest, schedule changes and overtime records are consistent with ERGANI II declarations, payslips and social insurance declarations, and cannot circumvent clock-in by renaming ordinary employees as "managers" or "field staff."

3. Greek Labor Law and Regulatory Framework

Employment relationships in Greece are mainly governed by labor law, statutory minimum wage decisions, collective agreements, the ERGANI II employment information system, the Electronic National Social Security Institution (e-EFKA), the Independent Authority for Public Revenue (AADE), occupational health and safety and the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). The Ministry of Labor and Social Security, the Labor Inspectorate, e-EFKA, AADE and the Public Employment Service (DYPA) respectively handle regulation, declaration and benefit management.

The national minimum wage is a mandatory floor, and sectoral or company collective agreements may provide higher wages, allowances, working hours or termination benefits. The contracting employer is responsible for onboarding and change declarations, employment contracts, wages and special allowances, tax and social insurance, work time cards, leave, safety, discipline and termination.

In an EOR arrangement, the client manager may set business goals and provide performance facts, but must not bypass the contracting employer to directly adjust pay, suspend pay, refuse leave, discipline or dismiss employees.

4. Recruitment, Offer Letters and Onboarding

Job descriptions should clearly state actual duties, work location, reporting line, five-day or six-day workweek, on-site or remote arrangements, shifts, travel, language and objective qualifications. Before issuing an offer, the company should first determine whether the employee is white-collar or blue-collar, the applicable collective agreement, seniority increments, minimum wage and social insurance category.

The offer letter should separately list basic salary, fixed allowances, bonuses or commissions, overtime arrangements, Christmas allowance, Easter allowance and holiday allowance. It must not merely state that "annual salary divided by 12 already includes the 14 salaries" without separately listing items according to statutory payment periods and payslip items.

Background checks should follow necessity and GDPR requirements, limiting processing of health, criminal and family data. A valid employment contract does not necessarily mean a foreign employee will obtain residence or a work permit; the right to work must be independently verified before starting work.

5. Employment Contracts, Contract Types and Probation Periods

Open-ended contracts apply to ongoing roles. Fixed-term contracts should have an objective term, project or replacement need; if repeatedly renewed without objective reason and covering a permanent role long term, they may be deemed open-ended contracts. Early termination of a fixed-term contract without serious reason may require payment of remaining wages through the original expiry date.

The contract should state the parties, role, work location, start date, term, salary composition, payment frequency, working hours, overtime, leave, probation, collective agreement, benefits, confidentiality, data processing and termination conditions. When salary, role, location or working hours change materially, it should be documented in writing and ERGANI and the payroll system should be updated accordingly.

Open-ended contracts may provide for a probation period in writing, usually up to 6 months. The probation period for a fixed-term contract should be proportionate to the contract term and role, usually not exceeding one quarter of the contract term and not exceeding 6 months. Without an explicit written agreement, the employer should not later claim the employee is in probation.

Probation termination should still be handled in writing, declared through ERGANI and settled for wages, annual leave and pro-rata amounts of the three special payments, while excluding discrimination, retaliation and protected status risks. Performance issues should be managed through objective goals, feedback, coaching, a reasonable improvement period and review records.

6. Wages, Minimum Wage and Gross-to-Net Salary Calculation

From January 1 to March 31, 2026, the full-time minimum monthly salary for white-collar workers is EUR 880 and the minimum daily wage for blue-collar workers is EUR 39.30; from April 1, they rise to EUR 920 and EUR 41.09 respectively. Part-time wages are calculated in proportion to the normal full-time standard and agreed working hours, and where a sectoral or company collective agreement provides a higher standard, the more favorable treatment applies.

Employees paid at the statutory minimum wage may receive three-year increments based on recognized seniority. White-collar workers receive 10% for each completed 3 years, up to 30%; blue-collar workers receive 5% for each completed 3 years, up to 30%. Currently recognized periods mainly include subordinate employment service before February 14, 2012 and after January 1, 2024. Employers should collect valid service evidence.

7. Working Hours, Overtime and Recordkeeping

Standard full-time working hours are usually 40 hours/week. A five-day workweek is generally 8 hours/day, and a six-day workweek is generally 6 hours 40 minutes/day. Additional work, lawful overtime, night work, Sunday and public holiday work apply different declaration and wage premiums, and cannot all be merged into a generic "overtime pay."

Covered companies should record actual start, end, rest, schedule changes and overtime through ERGANI II and the digital work time card, and complete declarations within statutory time limits. Arrangements where a fixed salary includes overtime cannot be lower than the statutory premium and cannot replace genuine working time records.

Whether remote employees, field staff, managers or system failures qualify for exceptions should be determined based on actual duties and rules. When a client temporarily requests extended working hours, the contracting employer should first review and approve it and complete schedule or overtime declarations.

8. Public Holidays, Annual Leave and Other Statutory Leave

Employees accrue annual leave pro rata from the start date in the first calendar year based on the full-year benchmark, without needing 12 consecutive months of service. A five-day workweek is usually based on a 20-day first-year annual benchmark; after meeting conditions in the second calendar year it usually increases to 21 days, and from the third year onward it is usually 22 days.

After 10 years with the same employer or 12 years of cumulative service, five-day workweek employees usually enjoy 25 days of annual leave; after 25 years of cumulative service it usually increases to 26 days. Six-day workweeks correspond to more days. On departure, untaken annual leave, holiday allowance and pro-rata Christmas and Easter allowances should be settled separately.

The mandatory or optional nature of different holidays, overlap with Sundays and sector scheduling may vary. Public holiday work should be handled according to the official list and applicable wage premiums, and cannot be directly deducted from annual leave.

Maternity leave is usually 17 weeks; eligible female employees may also apply for 9 months of special maternity protection leave and DYPA benefits. Paternity leave is usually 14 working days. Parental, caregiving, child sickness, school affairs and force majeure situations have separate statutory leave, with different payers and job protection.

Sick leave usually consists of employer salary continuation obligations together with e-EFKA cash benefits, with amounts depending on service length, waiting days and insurance eligibility. Employers should obtain electronic medical certificates and separately record the employer-borne portion, e-EFKA benefits and any salary top-up.

10. Local Employees and Foreign Employees

Local employees, EU or EEA employees and other foreign employees in principle enjoy the same minimum labor law protections, but the right to work, tax residency and social insurance coverage may differ. Companies should verify nationality, actual work location, residence or work permit, tax number (AFM), social insurance number (AMKA) and cross-border social insurance certificates before onboarding.

If an employee holds a valid A1 certificate or is covered by an applicable bilateral social insurance arrangement, they may continue to be covered by another country's social insurance. An EOR arrangement cannot automatically grant the right to work, nor can it eliminate cross-border tax and permanent establishment risks.

Individuals who are actually subject to long-term direction and supervision by an employer or client, have fixed schedules, are integrated into the organization and lack independent business risk cannot be excluded from labor law protection merely by the title "consultant" or "contractor."

11. Remote Work, Data Privacy and Employee Records

Remote work agreements should specify the primary work location, on-site frequency, equipment and expenses, working time records, information security, occupational health and safety and cross-border work approval. Before an employee moves to another country for long-term work, the applicable labor law, tax residency, social insurance, permanent establishment, right to work and data transfer risks should be reassessed.

When processing recruitment, payroll, health, work time card, performance and discipline data, employers should follow lawful basis, data minimization, access control and retention periods. Health, union and complaint data should not be accessible to client personnel without a business need; monitoring must be necessary, proportionate and transparently notified in advance.

Employment files should include contracts, ERGANI records, payslips, APD and tax declarations, digital work time cards, leave, bonuses, performance, warnings, accidents and termination materials. After departure, system access should be closed, assets recovered and records retained for statutory or business-necessary periods.

12. Termination of Employment, Compensation and Final Settlement

Termination of an open-ended contract should be in writing, declared through ERGANI and paid with correct compensation. Employers cannot terminate for discriminatory, retaliatory, abusive or protected-status-violating purposes. Early termination of a fixed-term contract without serious reason may create liability to pay wages for the remaining term.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the minimum wage in Greece in 2026?
From April 1, 2026, the full-time minimum monthly salary for white-collar workers is EUR 920 and the minimum daily wage for blue-collar workers is EUR 41.09. From January 1 to March 31, 2026, EUR 880 and EUR 39.30 still apply respectively.

Are the 14 salaries in Greece required by law or market practice?
For eligible private-sector salaried employees, the Christmas allowance, Easter allowance and holiday allowance are statutory payments. A full year typically totals 2 months of salary, forming the so-called "14 salaries" together with 12 months of normal salary.

What is the ordinary employer social insurance rate in Greece?
For most ordinary private salaried employees, the total social insurance rate is 36.16%, with the employer bearing 22.29% and the employee 13.87%. Heavy, hazardous occupations, supplementary insurance and other special categories may apply different rates.

Are employees entitled to annual leave in their first year?
Yes. Employees accrue annual leave from the start date based on service length. A five-day workweek is usually calculated pro rata based on a 20-day full-year benchmark, and the entitlement does not begin only after 12 months of work.

What is the maximum probation period in Greece?
For open-ended contracts, the probation period agreed in writing is usually up to 6 months. For fixed-term contracts, probation should be proportionate to the contract term and role, usually not exceeding one quarter of the contract term and not exceeding 6 months.

Can a fixed-term contract be ended directly after advance notice?
Not necessarily. If a fixed-term contract is terminated early without serious reason, the employer may need to pay the remaining wages through the original expiry date. Companies should first assess serious reason or a genuine negotiated termination solution.

Can an employee be dismissed unconditionally after paying severance?
No. The employer must also satisfy requirements for written form, ERGANI declaration, notice or pay in lieu arrangements, protected status review and prohibitions on discrimination, retaliation and abuse of rights.

Can employees be hired without a Greek entity?
Companies may assess a compliant Employer of Record (EOR) arrangement, but must confirm case by case the contracting employer or secondment eligibility, ERGANI, social insurance, digital work time card, occupational safety, right to work and client management boundaries.
