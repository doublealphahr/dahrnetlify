Collective Agreements, Social Security, Leave, Termination and EOR Compliance

2026 POLICY UPDATE

Collective Agreement Wage Increases: Sweden has no national statutory minimum wage. From 2026, most industry collective agreements implement new wage levels. Employers must confirm the applicable collective agreement and job category before issuing offers.

Parental Leave and Temporary Parental Benefit Updated: From July 1, 2026, new rules for temporary parental benefit (VAB) and pregnancy benefit take effect. Employers should update payroll and leave systems accordingly.

Social Security Contribution Rates: The 2026 employer social security contribution rate remains generally at 31.42% for ordinary employees, with reduced rates for youth and specific categories. Payroll systems should verify applicable rates by employee age and status.

Hiring employees in Sweden means companies cannot rely on a single national minimum wage or a fixed employer cost percentage. The Swedish labor market is heavily shaped by collective agreements (kollektivavtal), which may set wages, working hours, overtime, pension, insurance, and notice periods. Companies should first confirm the employee's industry, job duties, age, union affiliation, and applicable collective agreement, then configure payroll and compliance accordingly.

This guide is intended for HR, finance, legal and overseas business teams of Chinese companies recruiting, signing contracts, running payroll, calculating employer costs, using Employer of Record (EOR) services or handling employee separations in Sweden. Amounts in this document are expressed in Swedish krona (SEK); specific collective agreement rates, local union requirements, insurance arrangements, and foreign employee right to work still need to be confirmed case by case.

3. Swedish Labor Law and Regulatory Framework

Swedish labor relations are mainly governed by the Employment Protection Act (LAS), Working Hours Act, Annual Leave Act, Parental Leave Act, Discrimination Act, and collective agreements. The Swedish Work Environment Authority is responsible for occupational safety, the Swedish Tax Agency administers tax and social contributions, and the Swedish Pensions Agency manages state pension.

Collective agreements play a central role in Sweden. Even without union membership, an employer may be bound by a collective agreement through membership in an employer organization or through a direct agreement. Collective agreements may set wages, working hours, overtime, pension, insurance, notice periods, and other benefits.

The contracting employer bears responsibility for employment contracts, payroll, tax and social contributions, working environment, leave, and termination. Clients or group managers may provide business input but cannot bypass the Swedish employer to directly discipline or dismiss employees.

4. Recruitment, Job Offer and Onboarding

Recruitment must not discriminate based on gender, age, ethnicity, religion, disability, sexual orientation, or other protected factors. Job advertisements should state the position, work location, wage structure, working hours, and employment type.

6. Wages, Minimum Wage and Gross-to-Net Calculation

Sweden has no national statutory minimum wage. Wages are determined by collective agreements or individual contracts. Companies should confirm the applicable collective agreement and job category before setting wages.

Monthly salary exampleSEK 40,000Gross monthly wage

Employer pension contributionSEK 40,000 × 4.5%SEK 1,800 (example under collective agreement)

Payslips should show gross wage, tax withholding, employee social contribution, and net wage. Employee net pay depends on tax table, individual circumstances, and applicable deductions.

Vacation pay, overtime, and bonuses should be itemized separately. Collective agreement benefits may add further wage components.

10. Local Employees and Foreign Employees

Local and foreign employees in principle enjoy the same labor law protections. Foreign employees must have a work permit consistent with the employer, position, and work location. EOR arrangements do not automatically resolve work permit issues.

Cross-border remote work may trigger tax, social security, permanent establishment, and data protection issues. Employers should assess case by case.

11. Remote Work, Data Privacy and Employee Records

Remote work should be agreed in writing, specifying work location, equipment, expenses, working hours, and data security. GDPR applies to employee data processing.

Employee records should be retained per statutory requirements and include contracts, payslips, working time, leave, and termination documents.

Offboarding settlement should include final wages, unused annual leave, notice pay, and any severance or bonus amounts due.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Does Sweden have a national minimum wage?
No. Wages are determined by collective agreements or individual contracts. Companies must confirm the applicable collective agreement for the industry and job category.

What is the employer social security rate in Sweden?
Generally 31.42% for ordinary employees in 2026, with reduced rates for youth and specific categories.

How many days of annual leave do Swedish employees have?
Statutory minimum 25 working days per year; collective agreements may provide more.

Can I use an EOR to hire in Sweden?
Yes, but you must verify the contracting employer, collective agreement, work permit, and actual management boundaries. EOR does not automatically resolve all compliance issues.
