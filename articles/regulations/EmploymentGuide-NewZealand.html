Minimum Wage, KiwiSaver, Leave, Termination and EOR Compliance

2026 Policy Update

Minimum Wage Increased from April 1 The adult minimum wage rises to NZD 23.95/hour, and the starting-out and training minimum wage rises to NZD 19.16/hour. Enterprises should update hourly rates, annual salary conversions and actual working hours tests.

KiwiSaver Default Rate Enters 3.5% Phase From April 1, 2026, the default employee contribution and matching employer contribution for eligible employees usually rise to 3.5%, with a new arrangement allowing application for a temporary reduction to 3%.

Paid Parental Leave Payment Increased from July 1 The government payment maximum rises to NZD 811.05/week pre-tax, with a maximum continuous payment of 26 weeks. Employers should distinguish government payment, job protection and contractual top-up obligations.

New Zealand's employment compliance is jointly composed of the Employment Relations Act, Minimum Wage Act, Holidays Act, KiwiSaver, PAYE, ACC, privacy and occupational health and safety rules. When Chinese enterprises recruit in New Zealand or use Employer of Record (EOR) services, they cannot only check the annual salary; they must also simultaneously configure written employment agreements, actual working hours, leave, public holidays, KiwiSaver, ACC industry rates and fair termination procedures.

The most important changes in 2026 are: the adult minimum wage rises to NZD 23.95/hour from April 1, and the starting-out and training minimum wage rises to NZD 19.16/hour; KiwiSaver default employee contribution and matching employer contribution usually rise to 3.5%; the government paid parental leave maximum is NZD 811.05/week pre-tax from July 1. Enterprises should simultaneously update offers, contracts, payroll parameters and quotation models.

2. Three Rules Most in Need of Attention in 2026

Minimum Wage Increased from April 1 The adult minimum wage is NZD 23.95/hour, and the starting-out and training minimum wage is NZD 19.16/hour. The lower rate applies only to employees meeting the statutory definition and cannot be automatically used because the employee is new, a student, a graduate or on probation.

The minimum wage test must cover all actual working hours of the employee, including mandatory meetings, training, opening and closing, necessary preparation and other work required by the employer.

KiwiSaver Default Contribution Rises to 3.5% From April 1, 2026, eligible employees contributing at the original default 3% will usually have their employee contribution and matching employer contribution automatically rise to 3.5%.

Employees may apply for a temporary reduction of 3 to 12 months to temporarily maintain their own contribution at 3%. The employer may choose to match 3% during the temporary reduction period, but should update payroll promptly according to IRD notification after the employee restores the higher rate. Enterprises also need to distinguish employer contribution, employee deduction, ESCT and total remuneration clauses.

Paid Parental Leave Government Payment Increased From July 1, 2026, the maximum government paid parental leave payment rises to NZD 811.05/week pre-tax, with a maximum continuous payment of 26 weeks. The employee's actual amount received is the lower of their usual weekly income and the maximum payment.

Government payment, job protection and employer top-up are three different concepts. Job protection period depends on the employee's service eligibility; the employer only needs to top up the difference between the government payment and normal wages when the contract, collective agreement or company policy promises it.

3. New Zealand Labor Law and Regulatory Framework

New Zealand private sector employment relationships are mainly governed by the Employment Relations Act, Minimum Wage Act, Holidays Act, Wages Protection Act, Privacy Act and occupational health and safety rules.

Employment New Zealand provides official guidance on minimum wage, employment agreements, leave, public holidays and termination of employment; Inland Revenue (IRD) is responsible for PAYE, KiwiSaver, ESCT and payday filing; ACC is responsible for work injury cover and employer Work levy; WorkSafe is responsible for occupational health and safety supervision.

The contract employer is responsible for employment contract, wages, PAYE, KiwiSaver, leave, health and safety and termination procedures. Client or group managers cannot bypass the contract employer to directly discipline or dismiss EOR employees.

4. Recruitment, Offer and Onboarding

Recruitment advertisements and interviews must not unlawfully discriminate based on gender, age, race, disability, marital or family status and other protected factors. Health status, criminal record and background check questions must be relevant to genuine position needs.

The offer should clarify whether the wage is gross hourly or annual salary, and state guaranteed hours, working days, overtime or standby arrangements, bonuses, KiwiSaver treatment, annual leave, trial or probation, notice period and work location.

Only when the employee genuinely meets the starting-out or training definition can the lower minimum wage be used. Wages cannot be automatically reduced because the employee is a graduate, student, new hire or on probation.

Before conducting background checks, reference checks or cross-border transfer of candidate data, a privacy notice should be provided and necessary authorization obtained. Individuals who actually continuously accept enterprise direction, use enterprise tools and work at fixed times cannot be excluded from an employment relationship merely by a "consultant" or "contractor" contract.

Contract Type Applicable Scenario Main Risk
Permanent Continuous or permanent positions Employer cannot terminate without reason; fair procedure required
Genuine fixed-term Genuine, reasonable term or project reason exists Must state reason and endpoint in writing; cannot be used to test employees
Casual No continuous work commitment; each shift accepted separately Long-term regular scheduling may convert to continuous employment relationship

Contractor Independent operation and control of work method Court judges by true relationship; misclassification may be retroactive

Fixed-term contracts must have a genuine, reasonable term reason, and state the termination date, event or project completion criteria in the contract. Fixed-term cannot be used only to test whether an employee is suitable for a long-term position.

If the fixed-term reason no longer exists, the contract is repeatedly renewed, or the so-called casual employee has formed fixed and regular scheduling, HR should re-assess the contract nature.

The employment contract should at least state position, work location, wages and pay period, normal working hours, rest arrangements, overtime or standby, leave, KiwiSaver, probation, notice period, policy and dispute resolution method.

90-day Trial and Probation Period A valid 90-day trial must meet the following conditions:

  1. Written into the individual employment agreement;

  2. Agreed and signed by both parties before the employee starts work;

  3. The employee has not previously worked for the employer;

  4. The termination decision is made within the 90-day trial period;

  5. The employer fulfills the notice obligation according to the contract.

During a valid trial period, the employee usually cannot raise a personal grievance for unjustified dismissal, but wages, minimum wage, notice period, anti-discrimination, health and safety and other statutory rights still apply.

Probation is different from a 90-day trial. During ordinary probation, the employee still retains full unjustified dismissal protection, and the employer should clarify performance standards, provide feedback and support, issue warnings and give reasonable opportunity to improve.

Scenario Procedure Main Settlement
Termination during valid 90-day trial Decide within 90 days and give notice per contract Wages, holiday pay and other earned amounts
Termination during probation Feedback, support, warning and response procedure Notice wages and complete final settlement
Employee resignation during probation Notice per contract and complete handover Wages and applicable leave settlement
Invalid trial clause Handle per ordinary dismissal reason and fair procedure Cannot rely on trial to exclude grievance

Minimum wage should be tested against all actual working hours in each pay period. Mandatory meetings, training, opening and closing, necessary preparation and some employer-required travel time may constitute working hours.

Wage sacrifice, KiwiSaver, equipment or other wage deductions must not bring the employee's effective cash wage below the applicable minimum wage.

New Zealand has no national uniform personal income tax "tax-free threshold." PAYE is calculated based on employee tax code, taxable income, ACC earners' levy, student loan and other factors. Formal net salary should use current IRD payroll tax parameters and cannot be calculated simply by multiplying annual salary by a fixed rate.

7. Working Hours, Overtime and Records

The employment agreement should usually set normal weekly working hours at no more than 40 hours, excluding separately agreed overtime. If normal working hours exceed 40 hours, the parties should clearly agree in the contract.

Item Main Rules Employer Action
Normal Working Hours Contract usually stipulates no more than 40 hours/week Clarify guaranteed hours and possible additional hours
Rest and Meal Breaks Arrange statutory paid rest and unpaid meal breaks according to working hours Automatically configure in scheduling system
Ordinary Overtime No national uniform 1.5x statutory standard Implement per contract or collective agreement
Availability Requires genuine reason, reasonable compensation and guaranteed hours Employee may refuse additional shifts without valid clause
Public Holiday Work Usually at least 1.5x First determine whether it is an otherwise working day
Fatigue Management Employer needs to control excessively long hours, night shifts and driving risks Employee consent cannot exclude health and safety responsibility

A fixed annual salary does not automatically cover all additional working hours. Employers should keep records of employee start and end times, rest, training, travel, standby and overtime, and ensure that wages converted by all actual working hours are not below the statutory minimum wage.

An availability clause may only be valid when the employer has a genuine business reason, the contract provides guaranteed hours and reasonable compensation is given. Without a valid clause, the employee can usually refuse to accept additional shifts beyond guaranteed hours.

New Zealand does not have the same uniform "pension plus medical insurance" social security rate as some countries. The employer is mainly responsible for PAYE, payday filing, KiwiSaver, ESCT and ACC; public healthcare is mainly tax-supported.

10. Local Employees and Expatriate or Foreign Employees

Foreign employees must hold work rights consistent with the actual position, statutory employer and work arrangement before starting work. An employment contract or EOR arrangement itself does not generate a visa or work permit, nor can it replace employer accreditation and immigration condition verification.

Foreign employees actually working in New Zealand are usually still protected by minimum wage, leave, public holidays, PAYE, ACC, health and safety and fair termination rules.

KiwiSaver eligibility needs to be judged in conjunction with the employee's residence status, age and scheme rules, and cannot be uniformly enrolled or uniformly excluded for all foreign employees.

The assignment plan should also verify:

  1. Employee tax residency;

  2. Domestic and overseas wages;

  3. Housing, vehicle, tuition and other taxable benefits;

  4. Shadow payroll arrangements;

  5. Double taxation and tax treaties;

  6. Permanent establishment risk;

  7. Cross-border travel and remote work location.

The employment contract, visa materials, actual work location and payroll records should be consistent. Before an employee changes position, employer or long-term work location, work rights and tax impact should be re-verified.

11. Remote Work, Data Privacy and Employee Records

The remote work agreement should clarify work location, normal working hours, contactable time, equipment, network and expenses, safety requirements, accident reporting, health and safety responsibility and return-to-office arrangements.

Working from home does not eliminate the employer's occupational health and safety obligations. Enterprises should assess office equipment, ergonomics, mental health, lone working, overtime, data security and accident reporting risks.

The New Zealand Privacy Act requires employers to explain the collection purpose, use scope, recipients, access and correction rights, security measures and overseas disclosure arrangements for employee personal information. Background checks, employee monitoring and cross-border HR systems should follow necessity, transparency and minimization principles.

Enterprises should keep:

  1. Employment contract and job description;

  2. Wages, actual working hours and wage deduction records;

  3. Annual leave, sick leave, public holiday and alternative holiday records;

  4. KiwiSaver, PAYE, ESCT and payday filing materials;

  5. Health and safety training and accident records;

  6. Performance, discipline, investigation and redundancy consultation materials;

  7. Termination decision and final wage details.

Wage, time and leave records should be kept at least to the statutory period. Performance, investigation and consultation materials should also be able to prove the employer fulfilled good faith and fair procedure.

12. Termination of Employment, Compensation and Final Settlement

New Zealand does not recognize a simple path of "paying notice pay equals dismissal without reason." Except for limited exceptions such as a valid 90-day trial, employer termination needs to satisfy both substantive reason and fair procedure.

Termination Path Reason and Procedure Main Settlement
Valid 90-day trial termination Decide within 90 days and give notice per contract Wages, holiday pay and alternative holidays
Termination during probation Set standards, feedback, support, warning and response Notice wages and complete final pay
Employee resignation Notice per contract and confirm last working day Wages, leave, bonus and expenses
Behavioral dismissal Investigation, inform of allegations, provide materials and hear response Notice and final settlement; except serious misconduct
Performance dismissal Clarify standards, support, warning, improvement period and review Notice and final settlement
Serious misconduct summary dismissal Must have serious and provable reason and fair investigation May not require notice, but earned entitlements must still be paid
Fixed-term natural expiry Valid genuine term and endpoint Usually no uniform redundancy compensation
Fixed-term early termination Requires contract right and fair reason and procedure May generate remaining term loss
Mutual agreement termination Genuine, informed and voluntary consent of both parties Written statement of payment, leave and dispute handling
Redundancy Genuine business reason, provide information, consultation, consider feedback and redeployment Notice; compensation per contract or collective agreement

Redundancy cannot first make a final decision and then supplement consultation through a meeting. The employer should provide relevant information to affected employees before the decision, give reasonable time to comment, and genuinely consider employee feedback, alternatives and redeployment opportunities.

New Zealand has no uniform statutory redundancy compensation applicable to all employees. If the employment contract or collective agreement has no compensation clause, usually no fixed amount of redundancy compensation automatically arises, but notice period, leave and final wages must still be paid.

Final pay usually includes:

  1. Wages up to termination date;

  2. Acquired but unused annual leave;

  3. 8% of gross earnings after the last annual leave anniversary;

  4. Unused alternative holidays;

  5. Earned bonus or commission;

  6. Approved but unreimbursed expenses;

  7. Contractual notice wages or compensation.

If the "notional extension period" formed by acquired annual leave crosses a public holiday, additional public holiday pay may also arise. Unused sick leave and bereavement leave are usually not cashed out.

Assume an employee with a weekly wage of NZD 1,500, final two weeks' wages of NZD 3,000, acquired unused annual leave of 2 weeks, gross earnings of NZD 30,000 after the last annual leave anniversary, and one alternative holiday valued at NZD 300:

Acquired unused annual leave1,500 × 2NZD 3,000
8% after anniversary30,000 × 8%NZD 2,400

Before completing settlement, HR and Payroll should jointly review wage records, leave ledger, public holiday calendar, bonuses, expenses and lawful deductions, and provide the employee with a clear settlement breakdown.

EOR can change the contract employer and service division, but cannot eliminate New Zealand labor law, minimum wage, leave, PAYE, KiwiSaver, ACC, health and safety and employee dispute risks.

Client project completion does not automatically constitute termination of the employment relationship. When performance, discipline, salary adjustment or dismissal is involved, the client should provide true facts and evidence to the statutory employer, and the statutory employer completes the good-faith procedure according to law.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS (FAQ)

What is the minimum wage in New Zealand in 2026?
From April 1, 2026, the adult minimum wage is NZD 23.95/hour, and the starting-out and training minimum wage is NZD 19.16/hour. The lower rate applies only to employees meeting strict statutory conditions.

How much KiwiSaver must New Zealand employers pay?
From April 1, 2026, the default employee contribution and matching employer contribution for eligible employees are usually both 3.5%. Temporary reduction, employee eligibility, total remuneration clauses and ESCT will change the actual payroll and employer cost.

What is the statutory annual leave in New Zealand?
Employees usually enjoy 4 weeks of paid annual leave after 12 months of continuous employment. Departure before 12 months does not mean no leave value; holiday pay is usually calculated at 8% of gross earnings for the relevant period, less amounts already paid or taken in advance.

Can a fixed monthly salary cover all overtime?
This cannot be handled automatically. New Zealand has no national uniform 1.5x ordinary overtime rate, but the contract must clearly state working hours, overtime or on-call arrangements, and conversion by all actual working hours must not be below minimum wage. Availability clauses also require genuine reason, guaranteed hours and reasonable compensation.

How is public holiday work paid?
Employees working on a public holiday are usually paid at least 1.5 times the relevant daily pay; if the day was originally the employee's normal working day, an alternative holiday should also be provided separately.

Can a 90-day trial be signed after onboarding?
No. The 90-day trial must be agreed in writing in the individual employment agreement and signed with consent before the employee starts work; the employee must also not have previously worked for the employer. Ordinary probation is different from trial and cannot exclude unjustified dismissal grievance rights.

Must New Zealand redundancy pay statutory severance?
New Zealand has no uniform statutory redundancy compensation applicable to all employees. Whether to pay depends on the contract or collective agreement, but the employer must still prove genuine business reason, provide information, carry out meaningful consultation, consider employee feedback and redeployment, and pay notice and final settlement amounts.

How much does New Zealand government paid parental leave pay?
From July 1, 2026, the maximum payment is NZD 811.05/week pre-tax, with a maximum continuous payment of 26 weeks. The employee's actual amount received is the lower of their usual weekly income and the maximum payment.

Can employees be hired without a New Zealand entity?
Enterprises can assess a compliant Employer of Record (EOR) arrangement, but must confirm case by case the statutory employer, employee work rights, PAYE, KiwiSaver, ACC, health and safety, client management boundary and termination responsibility.
