Employment Contracts, Minimum Wage, Social Insurance and Termination Compliance

2026 POLICY UPDATE

Minimum wage increased — From January 1, 2026, the German statutory minimum wage rises to EUR 13.90 per hour. Employers should update payroll and working time systems and also verify higher sectoral standards.

Minijob threshold increased — From January 1, 2026, the monthly income limit for Minijobs rises to EUR 603, with an annual limit of EUR 7,236. Employers should re-check actual working hours and one-off payments.

Social insurance contribution ceilings adjusted — In 2026, the monthly contribution ceiling for pension and unemployment insurance rises to EUR 8,450, and the monthly contribution ceiling for health and long-term care insurance rises to EUR 5,812.50. Employers should update payroll parameters and distinguish contribution ceilings from mandatory insurance thresholds.

Germany has a highly regulated labor relationship, social insurance and employee representation system. When Chinese companies recruit employees in Germany, they must not only verify national and sectoral minimum wages, but also handle employment contract form, working time records, social insurance funds, paid leave, employee representation participation and strict dismissal procedures.

In 2026, the German statutory minimum wage rises to EUR 13.90 per hour, and the monthly income limit for Minijobs rises accordingly to EUR 603; the contribution ceilings for pension and unemployment insurance, statutory health and long-term care insurance are also adjusted. Companies cannot simply estimate German employer costs using "gross salary plus 20%."

This guide is intended for HR, legal, finance and overseas business teams of Chinese companies recruiting, contracting, running payroll, calculating employer costs or handling employee departures in Germany. Public holidays use Berlin as an example; other states, sectoral collective agreements and foreign employee work permits should be verified separately. The content was last verified on August 26, 2026.

1. 2026 Germany Employment Compliance at a Glance

One-sentence conclusion: German employment cannot be summarized as "salary plus about 20%." Companies need to first verify sectoral minimum wages and collective agreements, then calculate item by item based on the health insurance fund, employee's child status, social insurance contribution ceilings, work injury insurance and U1/U2 allocation. When handling dismissals, the focus should be on evidence, written form and statutory procedure.

The three things most easily stated incorrectly:

  1. Continuing to use the 2025 minimum wage of EUR 12.82 per hour for 2026 quotes.
  2. Writing the health insurance surcharge or long-term care insurance premium as a uniform rate applicable to all employees.
  3. Understanding probation as allowing oral, immediate and reason-free termination of the employment contract.

German labor relationships are mainly governed by the German Civil Code, the Dismissal Protection Act, the Working Time Act, the Federal Leave Act, the Part-Time and Fixed-Term Employment Contracts Act, the Employee Leasing Act and collective agreements.

The contracting employer is responsible for the employment contract, payroll, social insurance, occupational safety, leave, discipline and departure. Actual instructions from a client or group manager must not bypass the statutory employer and works council procedures.

4.2 Recruitment and Offer Red Lines

Recruitment must not involve unlawful discrimination based on race or ethnicity, gender, religion or belief, disability, age or sexual orientation. Job titles, recruitment copy and images should remain neutral.

Recruitment materials should only collect necessary information relevant to the role. Special caution is required when requesting photos or asking about a candidate's family plans, health status and disability information.

The offer letter should clearly state:

  1. Gross monthly or annual salary;
  2. Weekly working hours;
  3. Work location;
  4. Remote work arrangements;
  5. Bonuses;
  6. Overtime;
  7. Applicable collective agreement;
  8. Probation period;
  9. Notice period;
  10. Statutory and contractual annual leave.

Residence and work permits for foreign employees should be verified through a separate process. The hiring team should ensure that work authorization is consistent with the contracting entity, role and work location.

Fixed-term contracts must satisfy statutory form requirements before the employee starts work. If the employee continues working or renewal is non-compliant, the contract may convert to an open-ended contract.

The end of a client project does not automatically allow the client to directly terminate the employment relationship of an EOR or dispatched employee.

5.2 Probation Period

The probation period should be explicitly agreed in the employment contract, usually up to 6 months. During the agreed probation period and not exceeding 6 months, termination is usually possible with 2 weeks' notice.

Probation does not exclude minimum wage, social insurance, annual leave accrual, sick pay, anti-discrimination and special protection for pregnant, maternity or disabled employees.

6. Wages, Minimum Wage and Gross-to-Net Salary Calculation

6.1 2026 Wage Floor Determination Order

  1. Check the applicable sectoral statutory minimum wage or generally binding collective agreement.
  2. Compare with the national minimum wage of EUR 13.90 per hour and apply the higher applicable standard.
  3. Convert actual working time, on-call or preparation time and piecework pay into hourly wages.
  4. Exclude expense reimbursements, non-work-purpose allowances or specific one-off payments that cannot be used to top up the minimum wage.
  5. Prepare in advance for the parameter switch to the EUR 14.60 per hour minimum wage and Minijob threshold in 2027.

6.2 Berlin Office Role Example

If a full-time employee works 40 hours per week and is paid at the national minimum wage, the average monthly minimum gross salary is approximately:

13.90 × 40 × 52 ÷ 12 = EUR 2,409.33

This amount is only used to demonstrate the national statutory wage floor and does not represent the market wage for professional roles in Berlin, nor a collective agreement wage.

Wage Item What Should Be Clarified Main Risk
Basic salaryGross monthly salary and payment dateMust not be lower than the applicable minimum wage or collective agreement wage
Bonus or commissionTargets, confirmation conditions, departure payment and clawbackMay affect leave, sick leave or departure calculations
OvertimeApproval, time off in lieu or payment method and cap"All-inclusive" clauses must match wage level, transparency and working time requirements
Expense reimbursementTravel, equipment and home office expensesExpense reimbursements cannot be used to top up the minimum wage

Wages are usually paid monthly according to the employment contract, and employees are provided with a verifiable payslip. Any deductions must have a legal or contractual basis and comply with non-attachable wage protection.

The payslip should separately show normal wages, overtime, bonuses, employee benefits, employee social insurance, wage tax and other deductions.

Minimum wage records are especially important for Minijobs and designated sectors. Companies cannot simply keep schedules without recording actual start time, end time and total working time.

6.3 Overtime Pay Example

Assuming an employee has a gross monthly salary of EUR 3,000 and the contract provides for a 40-hour week, the basic hourly wage is approximately:

3,000 ÷ (40 × 52 ÷ 12) = EUR 17.31

If the contract or collective agreement provides for a 25% overtime premium, the example overtime hourly rate is:

17.31 × 125% = EUR 21.64

If there is no contractual or collective agreement basis, companies cannot assume a 25% overtime premium applies, but still need to determine whether the employee is entitled to normal wages, time off in lieu, and whether statutory working time caps are breached.

German public holidays are determined by the federal state where the employee works. When a public holiday falls on a weekend, there is usually no automatic substitute holiday.

Public holiday work is in principle restricted. Where a statutory exception sector applies, companies still need to arrange compensatory rest and handle holiday premiums according to the employment contract or collective agreement.

Employers should consider employees' leave wishes and may only adjust leave arrangements when there is an urgent business need or other employees have more priority social reasons.

9. Employer Social Contributions, Mandatory Benefits and Taxes

Employers are responsible for completing social insurance registration, withholding the employee's share from wages, bearing the employer's share, and remitting to the employee's chosen statutory health insurance fund.

Employees bear personal pension, unemployment, health and long-term care insurance contributions and wage tax. Work injury insurance, U1/U2 allocation and insolvency wage allocation are employer costs and cannot be mixed with the four major social insurance contributions borne by employees.

9.2 Berlin Office Employee Cost Example

Assuming an employee has a gross monthly salary of EUR 5,000, is 30 years old, childless, participates in statutory health insurance, and the 2026 official average surcharge of 2.9% is used for estimation. The employee is not in Saxony, and wages are below the relevant social insurance contribution ceilings.

Employer Cost Item Calculation Monthly Amount
Gross salaryFixedEUR 5,000.00
Pension insurance5,000 × 9.3%EUR 465.00
Unemployment insurance5,000 × 1.3%EUR 65.00
Health insurance5,000 × (7.3% + 1.45%)EUR 437.50
Long-term care insurance5,000 × 1.8%EUR 90.00
Core employer monthly cost subtotalSum of the aboveEUR 6,057.50

Known employee-side social insurance deductions are:

  • Pension insurance: EUR 465.00;
  • Unemployment insurance: EUR 65.00;
  • Health insurance: EUR 437.50;
  • Long-term care insurance: 5,000 × 2.4% = EUR 120.00;
  • Total social insurance: EUR 1,087.50.

Wage tax, etc. still needs to be calculated based on the electronic wage tax characteristics (ELStAM).

The final employer cost also needs to add work injury insurance, U1/U2 allocation, insolvency wage allocation and employee benefits, so EUR 6,057.50 cannot be regarded as the final invoice amount.

9.3 Social Insurance Contribution Ceilings

If an employee has a gross monthly salary of EUR 7,000:

  • Pension and unemployment insurance are calculated on EUR 7,000 because the EUR 8,450 monthly ceiling has not been reached;
  • Health and long-term care insurance are only calculated on EUR 5,812.50.

When an employee's salary exceeds the statutory health insurance mandatory participation threshold of EUR 6,450/month, it is also necessary to determine whether the employee is entitled to and chooses private health insurance. All health insurance obligations cannot be stopped simply because wages exceed the contribution ceiling.

9.4 Payroll Lifecycle Responsibilities

At onboarding, the tax number, social insurance number, health insurance fund and child certificates should be collected.

Each month, companies should complete wage calculation, withholding and remittance using ELStAM and insurance fund parameters. Bonuses should be allocated to the corresponding annual contribution ceiling according to social insurance attribution rules.

Each year, companies need to update social insurance ceilings, health insurance surcharges and various employer allocations. On departure, applicable documents such as social insurance deregistration, wage tax documents, employment certificate and leave certificate should be completed.

11. Remote Work, Data Privacy and Employee Records

Under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the German Federal Data Protection Act (BDSG), companies need a lawful basis when processing employee personal information and must comply with transparency, data minimization, retention period and access control requirements.

Because there is usually a relationship of subordination between employer and employee, employee consent is often not the preferred legal basis for processing employee data.

Monitoring, working time tools, audio and video recording, health information and performance analysis may trigger data protection impact assessments and works council co-determination procedures.

Remote work arrangements should clearly state:

  1. Work location;
  2. Equipment;
  3. Expenses;
  4. Working hours;
  5. Occupational safety;
  6. Accident reporting;
  7. Data security;
  8. Return-to-office arrangements.

Cross-border remote work by employees may change tax, social insurance, permanent establishment and work permit obligations. Companies cannot understand a German employment contract as permission for employees to work remotely from any location worldwide.

12. Termination of Employment, Compensation and Final Settlement

12.1 Pre-Dismissal Checklist

Before dismissal, confirm the contract type, probation period or length of service, company headcount, genuine reason, evidence, works council, special protection, notice period, untaken annual leave, bonuses and foreign employee status.

Termination of the employment contract must satisfy the written form requirement. Electronic signatures or emails usually cannot replace a signed original. The date of service determines when the notice period starts.

Employees usually have only 3 weeks to challenge a dismissal before the labor court, so employers should ensure that procedure and service records are complete.

When the Dismissal Protection Act applies, ordinary dismissal outside probation requires a socially justified reason related to the employee's person, conduct or business operation.

Even if the Dismissal Protection Act does not apply, the employer must still comply with special protection, anti-discrimination, public order and good morals, written form and works council procedures.

A valid fixed-term contract can end naturally when the agreed term expires or the contract purpose is achieved. Early ordinary termination can only be used when the employment contract or collective agreement expressly permits it.

12.2 Employer Action Steps

  1. Confirm contract, probation, length of service, Dismissal Protection Act applicability and genuine reason.
  2. Verify special protection status such as pregnancy, maternity, childcare, severe disability and employee representation.
  3. Confirm works council, collective agreement, trade union and authority approval or procedures.
  4. Complete performance, warning, investigation, role elimination, social selection or alternative position evidence.
  5. Determine separately the signed original service date, notice period start and end, last working day and legal termination date.
  6. Pre-calculate wages, annual leave, bonuses, notice period wages, severance and tax and social insurance.
  7. Complete final settlement, employment certificate, social insurance and tax documents, asset access closure and record archiving.

12.4 Severance and Departure Settlement Example

There is no automatic statutory severance applicable to all employees for ordinary dismissal in Germany.

Severance may come from:

  • Business dismissal offer under Section 1a of the Dismissal Protection Act;
  • Labor dispute settlement;
  • Social plan;
  • Employment contract;
  • Collective agreement.

The typical formula under Section 1a is 0.5 months' salary per year of service, but it applies only when the statutory notice expressly provides for it and the employee does not file a dismissal protection action.

Assuming a Berlin employee has a gross monthly salary of EUR 5,000 and has served 8 years. The employer makes a business dismissal offer meeting Section 1a conditions, the employee does not file an action, and untaken annual leave and bonuses are calculated separately.

Settlement Item Calculation Amount
Example Section 1a severance0.5 × 5,000 × 8EUR 20,000
Notice period3 months to end of month for 8 years of serviceNormal wages and social insurance continue, calculated separately
Untaken annual leaveBalance as of termination date × annual leave wageCalculated separately
Current determinable totalIncluding severance onlyEUR 20,000 + notice period wages + annual leave + other earned amounts

Final documents usually include the termination original, proof of service, payslip, leave certificate, employment certificate, social insurance and tax documents, equipment and access rights list.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

What is the 2026 Germany statutory minimum wage?
From January 1, 2026, the German statutory minimum wage is EUR 13.90 per hour. From January 1, 2027, it will rise to EUR 14.60 per hour. Employers should also verify whether a higher sectoral minimum wage or generally binding collective agreement applies.

How much is the 2026 Minijob income threshold?
From January 1, 2026, the monthly income limit for Minijobs rises to EUR 603, with an annual limit of EUR 7,236. Employers should re-check actual working hours and one-off payments.

What are the 2026 social insurance contribution ceilings?
In 2026, the monthly contribution ceiling for pension and unemployment insurance is EUR 8,450, and the monthly contribution ceiling for statutory health and long-term care insurance is EUR 5,812.50. The mandatory health insurance threshold is EUR 6,450/month.

What is the maximum probation period in Germany?
The probation period should be explicitly agreed in the employment contract, usually up to 6 months. During probation, termination is usually possible with 2 weeks' notice. Probation does not exclude minimum wage, social insurance, annual leave, sick pay and special protection requirements.

How many annual leave days do German employees have per year?
Statutory minimum is at least 24 working days for a six-day week, usually at least 20 working days for a five-day week. Contract or collective agreement often provides 25 to 30 days; more favorable rules take precedence.

Does ordinary dismissal in Germany require proving employee fault?
Ordinary dismissal outside probation requires a socially justified reason related to the employee's person, conduct or business operation when the Dismissal Protection Act applies. Payment of severance cannot replace the reason and procedure requirements.

Is there automatic statutory severance in Germany?
There is no automatic statutory severance applicable to all employees for ordinary dismissal. Severance may come from Section 1a business dismissal offer, labor dispute settlement, social plan, employment contract or collective agreement.

Can a company hire employees through EOR without a German entity?
Feasibility assessment is possible, but it must be confirmed case by case whether the arrangement constitutes temporary agency work under the Employee Leasing Act, and whether licensing, equal treatment, management boundary, and foreign employee status requirements are met.
