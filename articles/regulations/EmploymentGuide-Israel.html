Minimum Wage, NII, Pension, Leave and Termination Compliance

2026 POLICY UPDATE

Minimum Wage Adjustment
From April 1, 2026, the general adult minimum wage increases to NIS 6,443.85/month and NIS 34.64/hour. Employers should update wages, overtime base, minimum NII contribution income and quotation models.

NII Annual Parameters Updated
From January 1, 2026, the low-tier wage threshold for general resident employees is NIS 7,703, and the contribution income ceiling is NIS 51,910. Payroll systems should calculate by employee status and wage segments.

Annual Leave Choice Day Amendment
From July 20, 2026, employees may give 30 days' notice to use a first-degree relative memorial day as a choice day, but this day is deducted from the existing annual leave balance and is not an additional paid holiday.

Specific Maternity Benefit Extension
From April 1, 2026, when eligible and the newborn is recognized as having complex disabilities, the benefit may be extended by 5 weeks under statutory conditions. Employers should separately manage labor law leave length and NII paid benefit period.

As more Chinese enterprises enter Israel's technology, life sciences, trade, engineering and professional services markets, local employment management is no longer just signing employment contracts and paying monthly wages. Enterprises also need to simultaneously handle minimum wage, 42-hour workweek, overtime and weekly rest, annual leave, sick leave, National Insurance and health insurance, mandatory pension, severance pay contributions and dismissal hearings.

Israel's employment obligations come from statute, collective agreements, extension orders, court precedents, individual employment contracts and administrative rules. Resident employees and foreign employees are subject to different National Insurance rates, health insurance, housing and guarantee obligations; pension contributions and the Severance Pay Law Section 14 arrangement cannot be merged into a single general "social security ratio."

This guide is intended for Chinese enterprise HR, legal, finance, payroll and overseas business teams planning to recruit employees in Israel, systematically organizing the recruitment and onboarding, employment contracts, probation period, minimum wage, working hours and leave, National Insurance, pension, foreign employees, departure settlement and Employer of Record (EOR) rules that need to be mastered in 2026, helping enterprises complete a round of compliance judgment before recruitment, quotation, payroll and termination of employment.

2. Important Changes in 2026 and Employer Impact

Minimum Wage Adjusted from April 1, 2026
The general adult full-time minimum wage increases to NIS 6,443.85/month, and the hourly minimum wage increases to NIS 34.64. Enterprises should update employment contracts, hourly wages, overtime base, minimum National Insurance contribution income and quotation models, and retroactively check payslips after April.

2026 NII Thresholds and Contribution Ceiling Updated
From January 1, 2026, the low-tier wage threshold for general resident employees' National Insurance and health insurance is NIS 7,703, and the ordinary contribution income ceiling is NIS 51,910. Employers should calculate employee and employer shares by wage segments, and configure correct reporting fields based on age, resident status and controlling shareholder categories.

Annual Leave Choice Day Rule Adjusted on July 20, 2026
The 2026 amendment adds first-degree relative memorial day as a choice day that employees may use with 30 days' notice. This day is deducted from the annual leave entitlement the employee already has, and is not an additional paid holiday. Enterprises should update leave types, approval rules and annual leave balance descriptions.

Maternity Benefit Extension for Specific Newborn Circumstances
From April 1, 2026, when eligible and the newborn is recognized as having complex disabilities, the maternity benefit may be extended by 5 weeks under statutory conditions. Employers should separately manage the labor law leave length and the National Insurance Institute (NII) paid benefit period.

3. Labor Law Framework and Regulatory System

Israel's labor relations are mainly based on the Minimum Wage Law, Working Hours and Rest Law, Annual Leave Law, Sick Pay Law, Women's Employment Law, Severance Pay Law, pension extension orders, industry collective agreements or extension orders, employment contracts and labor court precedents.

Israel does not have a national uniform statutory 13th or 14th month salary. Annual bonus, meal allowance, stock and additional insurance usually depend on contract, collective agreement, extension order or company policy. Convalescence allowance (Dmei havra'a) may be a mandatory obligation arising from extension orders and should be separately verified in annual payroll parameters.

Recruitment advertisements and candidate screening must not unlawfully discriminate based on gender, pregnancy, fertility treatment, age, religion, nationality, reserve duty or other protected factors. Background checks should be proportionate to job necessity and comply with notice, consent and privacy requirements.

Project or temporary contractIdentifiable deliverable or short-term needRepeated renewal may prove long-term employment relationship

The employer should provide the employee with written employment conditions within the statutory time limit, stating employer, position, reporting relationship, start date, contract term, wage composition, payment date, normal working hours, weekly rest, social contributions and termination rules. Foreign employees should receive a written contract in a language they can understand.

Fixed-term contract natural expiry and early termination must be handled separately. Non-renewal on expiry may be regarded as dismissal under severance pay rules, especially if the employer did not propose renewal at the statutory time. Before wage reduction, lowering pension insured wage, canceling fixed allowances, significant position change or work location change, written consultation and notice procedures should be followed.

Israel's general private labor law does not have a unified statutory maximum probation months applicable to all positions. The probation period should be reasonably agreed in the contract and verified against applicable collective agreements. Probationary employees still enjoy minimum wage, leave accrual, NII, pension waiting period, anti-discrimination, hearing and statutory notice protection. A contract clause stating "immediate dismissal without cause during probation" cannot exclude these rights.

6. Wages, Minimum Wage and Pre-tax Take-home Pay Calculation

From April 1, 2026, the general adult full-time minimum wage is NIS 6,443.85/month, and the hourly minimum wage is NIS 34.64. Youth, disability-adjusted wages, specific industries, foreign expert wage thresholds or collective agreements may apply different or higher standards.

Scenario Calculation or Judgment Employer Action

For example, an hourly worker working 170 normal hours in May 2026 has a minimum normal wage of 170 × NIS 34.64 = NIS 5,888.80. Overtime, weekly rest or holiday work, travel and other entitlements should be calculated separately, and annual bonus or reimbursement cannot be used to fill the normal hourly wage.

Payslips should separately list basic wage, normal hours, overtime, holiday, annual leave, sick leave, bonus, travel, pension, severance, NII, health insurance, income tax and lawful deductions. Employers must not pass on their own NII, pension employer share, severance, recruitment fees or general operating expenses to employees.

Annual Leave
The Annual Leave Law uses consecutive calendar vacation days to express the statutory entitlement. Weekly rest days are not counted as actual vacation working days, so the net working day conversion differs between the 5-day and 6-day systems.

When less than a full year or actual working days are below the statutory threshold, it should be calculated pro-rata according to the statutory formula. From July 20, 2026, employees may give 30 days' notice to use a first-degree relative memorial day as a choice day, but this day is deducted from the existing annual leave balance.

Sick Leave
Employees usually accrue 1.5 days of sick leave per working month, with a cumulative cap of 90 days; employment contracts or collective agreements may provide more favorable treatment.

Sick Leave Stage Statutory Payment Ratio Payer
Day 10%Generally no sick pay, contract may be more favorable
Days 2-350%Employer pays
Day 4 onwards100%Employer pays within available balance

When an employee is on continuous sick leave for 6 days and the balance is sufficient, the statutory wage totals the equivalent of 4 days' wages: Day 1 0, Days 2-3 each 50%, Days 4-6 each 100%. Sick leave is not routinely taken over by NII from Day 3, and sick leave balance is usually not cashed out on departure unless the contract, collective agreement or company policy provides otherwise.

Maternity and Parental Leave
Employees with 12 months of continuous service at the same employer or workplace usually have 26 weeks for maternity and parental leave; less than 12 months usually 15 weeks. Leave length does not equal full pay. NII provides 15 or 8 weeks of maternity benefit based on contribution period. From April 1, 2026, when eligible and the newborn is recognized as having complex disabilities, the benefit may be extended by 5 weeks under statutory conditions.

10. Local Employees and Foreign Employees

The core processes for local resident employees include employment contract, wage tax, NII, health insurance, pension, attendance, leave and termination. In addition to labor law obligations, foreign employees also require separate verification of work permit, permitted employer, industry restrictions, wage threshold, private medical, housing and industry guarantee.

The common 2026 two tiers for ordinary foreign residents NII are: up to NIS 7,703, employee 0.1%, employer 0.75%; above that up to NIS 51,910, employee 0.87%, employer 2.65%. However, international agreement countries, Palestinian employees, nursing, agriculture, construction and other industries may apply different rules.

Enterprises cannot presume that all foreign work permits can be handled by EOR just because "Israel can assess EOR." Foreign personnel should be verified case by case according to status, industry and permit path.

11. Remote Work, Data Privacy and Record Keeping

When an employee works remotely from Israel for an overseas company on a long-term basis, it may trigger local minimum wage, working hours, NII, tax, permanent establishment and data protection obligations. If the overseas employer does not withhold, resident employees may need to self-report to NII, but this should not be regarded as the recommended default compliance model.

Employee files should keep employment contracts and changes, Form 101, identity and permits, NII and fund information, wage and working hours records, payslips, leave, performance, discipline, hearing and departure documents. 12. Termination of Employment, Dismissal Hearing, Severance Pay and Final Settlement

Israel does not have a closed statutory list of reasons applicable to all ordinary dismissal situations, but the employer should have a genuine, good faith, non-discriminatory reason and give the employee a genuine hearing opportunity before the final decision.

Recommended order of operation:

  1. Distinguish probation, performance, discipline, redundancy, fixed-term expiry, early termination or mutual agreement.
  2. Check protected situations such as pregnancy, fertility treatment, return from maternity leave, sick leave, reserve duty, work injury, whistleblowing and trade union.
  3. Form the proposed reason, facts and evidence, but do not make an irreversible decision yet.
  4. Send the hearing invitation a reasonable time in advance, stating the main reasons and materials, allowing the employee to prepare and be accompanied by a representative.
  5. Genuinely hear the employee's statement, record new facts and alternatives.
  6. After the hearing, independently deliberate, then make the decision and notice in writing.
  7. Complete wages, notice, annual leave, convalescence allowance, pension, severance, Form 161 and equipment handover.

The statutory notice period for monthly salaried employees is usually: 1 day per month of service for the first 6 months; 6 days plus 2.5 days per month of service for the second half after months 7-12; 1 month after 1 year. Daily or hourly employees apply a different incremental table, usually 1 month after 3 years.

Employees with usually 1 year of continuous service who are dismissed are entitled to severance pay, with the basic formula being last qualifying monthly wage × years of service. Employee resignation generally does not automatically generate statutory severance pay, but statutory deemed dismissal situations such as health, childcare, material adverse change and specific relocation may be exceptions.

Early termination of a fixed-term contract may also generate remaining term income loss, and unlawful dismissal or hearing defects may generate additional compensation.

13. Entity, Employer of Record and Payroll Outsourcing Selection

Enterprises with long-term operations, larger headcount and the need to directly control employment brand, compensation and benefits usually assess establishing an Israeli entity. During market validation, with few hires or when the entity is not yet established, Employer of Record services can be assessed; when there is already a local employer and only payroll calculation and filing support is needed, payroll outsourcing can be considered.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS (FAQ)

What is the minimum wage in Israel in 2026?
From April 1, 2026, the general adult full-time minimum wage is NIS 6,443.85/month, and the hourly minimum wage is NIS 34.64.

How many hours per week does an ordinary full-time employee in Israel work?
The ordinary full-time workweek is usually 42 hours. Overtime should first be judged by daily working hours, then verified against weekly cumulative and applicable permits.

Can resident employee NII be calculated with a single fixed ratio?
No. In 2026, the two-tier threshold for general resident employees is NIS 7,703, and the ordinary contribution income ceiling is NIS 51,910. Both employee and employer rates are calculated by wage segments.

Is sick leave paid by NII from Day 3?
No. The general rule is Day 1 0%, Days 2-3 employer pays 50%, Day 4 onwards employer pays 100%, up to available sick leave balance.

Is there a unified statutory maximum probation months in Israel?
General private labor law does not have a unified maximum probation months applicable to all positions. The probation period should be reasonably agreed, and cannot exclude hearing, notice, minimum wage, pension waiting period and anti-discrimination protection.

Does paying 6% under Section 14 necessarily mean no severance top-up?
Not necessarily. The Section 14 written text, contribution ratio, wage base and service period should be verified. When only 6% is contributed, employees meeting full severance eligibility may still have a top-up to 8.33%.

Can foreign employees directly apply the resident NII and pension tables?
No. Foreign employees' NII, private medical, housing, industry guarantee and pension or severance arrangements should be judged separately according to status, industry and permit path.

Can the decision be issued first and the hearing held afterward when dismissing an employee?
This should not be done. The hearing must occur before the final decision, and the employer should inform the proposed reasons and materials in advance and maintain a genuinely open decision-making attitude.

Can EOR be used in Israel?
It can be an assessment option, but the local entity, employee status, foreign permit, wage and fund handling, management boundary, hearing and termination responsibility must be confirmed case by case.
