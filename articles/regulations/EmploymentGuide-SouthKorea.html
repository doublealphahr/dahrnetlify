Minimum Wage, Four Major Insurances, Leave and Termination Compliance

2026 POLICY UPDATE

Minimum Wage Increased: From January 1, 2026, South Korea's minimum wage rises to KRW 10,320 per hour; converted at 209 hours per month, this equals KRW 2,156,880. Employers should update offers, contracts and payroll parameters accordingly.

Social Insurance Parameters Adjusted: In 2026, the National Pension contribution is 4.75% each for employer and employee, and the total National Health Insurance rate rises to 7.19%; from July 1, the National Pension standard monthly income floor and ceiling are adjusted to KRW 410,000 and KRW 6,590,000.

Family Leave System Updated in Phases: From August 20, 2026, short-term parental leave is implemented; from September 18, new and adjusted spouse support measures take effect; from November 27, the paid period of infertility treatment leave is expanded. Employers should update leave policies and payroll processes according to effectivity dates.

Hiring and managing employees in South Korea in 2026 requires companies to simultaneously handle minimum wage, employment contracts, probationary periods, working time and overtime, public holidays, the four major insurances, personal income tax, severance pay and termination procedures. South Korean labor compliance places particular emphasis on just cause and written procedure for dismissal; paying 30 days' notice pay cannot replace the dismissal reason, evidence and statutory procedure.

This guide is intended for Chinese companies planning to hire directly in South Korea, assess Employer of Record (EOR) or Professional Employer Organization (PEO) services, establish local payroll processes or recruit foreign employees. Amounts in this document are expressed in Korean won (KRW); specific applicability still needs to be determined based on workplace headcount, position, contract type, employee nationality, work location and actual management approach.

2. Three Rules Most in Need of Update in 2026

2.1 Minimum Wage Increased to KRW 10,320 per Hour

According to the Ministry of Employment and Labor's 2026 minimum wage announcement, from January 1, 2026, the minimum wage is KRW 10,320 per hour, an increase of 2.9% over 2025; converted at 40 hours per week and 209 hours per month, the monthly minimum wage is KRW 2,156,880. This standard does not distinguish by industry and in principle applies to all workplaces.

Companies cannot compare only the total contract monthly salary. Fixed overtime pay, night shift pay or holiday pay should be split from ordinary working time wages, and the wage items that can be included in the minimum wage comparison should be confirmed. Part-time, short-hour employees and probationary employees should also complete the minimum wage check according to applicable rules.

2.2 National Pension and Health Insurance Parameters Adjusted

In 2026, South Korea's National Pension reform first raises the total rate for workplace participants to 9.5%, with employers and employees each bearing 4.75%. The National Pension Service also announced that from July 2026 to June 2027, the standard monthly income floor is KRW 410,000 and the ceiling is KRW 6,590,000.

The National Health Insurance Service announced a 2026 total health insurance rate of 7.19% for workplace participants, with employers and employees each bearing 50%, i.e., 3.595% each. The long-term care insurance rate is 0.9448%, and its premium is calculated as "health insurance premium × 0.9448% ÷ 7.19%," approximately equivalent to 13.14% of the health insurance premium. Companies should set parameter review nodes at the beginning of the year and in July respectively.

2.3 Family Leave System Updated in Phases in the Second Half

The Ministry of Employment and Labor announced in July 2026 the work-family balance system arrangements for the second half of the year. From August 20, employees may use 1 week or 2 weeks of short-term parental leave once per year under statutory conditions for short-term care situations such as school closure, illness or hospitalization, or infectious disease school suspension, and the period used counts toward their total parental leave entitlement.

From September 18, spouse miscarriage or stillbirth leave is newly added, in principle usable within 5 days, of which the first 3 days are paid; spouse maternity leave may be used from 50 days before the expected delivery date, and qualifying male employees may also apply for parental leave before the child's birth to care for a spouse at risk of miscarriage or premature birth. From November 27, the paid period within the 6 days of annual infertility treatment leave increases from 2 days to 4 days. Employers should update employee handbooks, application forms, payroll codes, supporting documents and government subsidy processes according to the different effectivity dates.

4. Recruitment, Job Offer and Onboarding

Common recruitment channels in South Korea include the official employment platform WorkNet, as well as JobKorea, Saramin, Incruit, Wanted and LinkedIn Jobs. Recruitment advertisements should state the employing entity, work location, job duties, wage composition, working hours, probationary period and right to work requirements, and avoid setting age, gender, marital or parental status, school or family background restrictions unrelated to the position.

South Korean law does not uniformly stipulate the probationary period length for all positions, and market practice is commonly 3 months. Probation, wages, evaluation criteria and failed probation handling should be written into the contract. Probationary wages must still meet minimum wage rules; the probationary minimum wage reduction applicable to limited circumstances should not be directly used without verifying the position and conditions.

Probation is not a "period for dismissal without reason." Employers should still establish objective position standards, evaluation records, communication or coaching materials, and state in writing the reason for failure and the effective date. After the employee continues working, confirmation of regularization, permissions and benefit updates should be completed promptly.

6. Wages, Minimum Wage and Gross-to-Net Calculation

South Korean wages should in principle be paid directly and in full in currency to the employee, and at least once a month on a fixed date. Deductions permitted by law, collective agreement or employee authorization should have a clear basis; companies cannot withhold earned wages because of equipment, handover or client non-payment.

11. Remote Work, Data Privacy and Record Keeping

Remote or hybrid work policies should specify the primary work location, normal working hours, overtime approval, equipment, expense reimbursement, information security, monitoring boundaries, accident reporting and return-to-office arrangements. Remote employees within South Korea still need to comply with working time, minimum wage, overtime and work injury requirements.

Cross-border remote work by employees may change the labor law of the work location, personal income tax, social insurance, visa and corporate permanent establishment determinations. Companies should require employees to apply for approval before cross-border work and record the country, dates, duties, paying entity and cost-bearing method.

Employment contracts, payroll ledgers, attendance and overtime records, leave materials, insurance filings, performance or disciplinary evidence, written dismissal notices and separation settlement working papers should be retained according to applicable statutory periods. When processing resident registration, health, family or visa information, purpose limitation, least privilege and secure transmission should be implemented.

12. Termination of Employment, Severance Pay and Offboarding Settlement

South Korean employers cannot lawfully dismiss an employee merely by giving 30 days' notice or paying one month's wage. Ordinary dismissal generally requires just cause and written notice; economic redundancy also requires review of urgent business necessity, efforts to avoid dismissal, fair selection and advance consultation conditions.

Severance Pay and Final Settlement

According to official South Korean legal materials, employees with continuous service of at least 1 year and meeting weekly working hour conditions are generally entitled to severance pay of not less than 30 days' average wage per year of service, with fractions of less than a full year generally calculated pro-rata. Wages, severance pay and other monetary obligations generally should be paid within 14 days after termination of the employment relationship, unless the parties agree on a deferral.

Simplified formula:

Severance Pay = Total wages for the 3 calendar months before separation ÷ Total days in that period × 30 × Years of continuous service

The 2026 total health insurance rate for workplace participants is 7.19%, generally 3.595% each for employer and employee. Long-term care insurance is calculated separately as a health insurance premium add-on and must not be omitted.

Do South Korean employees have paid annual leave in the first year?
Yes. Employees with less than 1 year of service generally accrue 1 day of paid annual leave per full attendance month, up to 11 days in the first year; it cannot be stipulated that employees only begin to enjoy any annual leave after one year.

Is 13th month pay mandatory in South Korea?
There is no universal statutory 13th month pay applicable to all enterprises in South Korea. Whether bonuses or annual wages must be paid depends on the employment contract, employment rules, collective agreement or established enterprise practice.

Can a South Korean employee be dismissed after 30 days' notice?
No. 30 days' notice or notice pay is only part of the dismissal requirement. The employer generally also needs just cause, written notice, corresponding evidence and lawful procedure, and should confirm whether the employee is in a statutory protection period.

How is South Korean severance pay calculated?
Employees with continuous service of at least 1 year and meeting working hour conditions are generally entitled to severance pay of not less than 30 days' average wage per year of service, with fractions of less than a full year generally calculated pro-rata. The actual amount is affected by the last 3 months' wages, bonuses, allowances and days.

Must foreign employees participate in South Korea's four major insurances?
It cannot be generalized. The applicability conditions for health insurance, work injury insurance, National Pension and employment insurance differ, and National Pension and employment insurance may also be affected by employee nationality, visa, social security agreements or reciprocity arrangements, and should be verified per person.

Can using an EOR guarantee a South Korean E-7 visa?
No. The E-7 visa depends on the specific category, position, education and experience, salary, employing entity and Korean immigration requirements. EOR can participate in feasibility assessment but cannot replace competent authority approval.
