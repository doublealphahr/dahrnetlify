Industry Minimum Wage, Social Security, Leave and Termination Compliance

2026 Policy Update

Automotive Industry Wage Update From June 15, 2026, the automotive industry implements new minimum hourly wages by skill and experience. Employers must update wage parameters according to employees' actual qualifications, experience and work content.

Employer National Insurance Continues Zonal Application In 2026, the general industry regional rate continues to apply in zones from 0% to 14.1%. Enterprises must determine the actual rate based on entity location, industry and social insurance coverage.

Employee National Insurance Reduced to 7.6% In 2026, National Insurance on wage income for employees aged 17 to 69 is usually 7.6%. Employers should update withholding settings according to employee tax cards and cross-border coverage status.

Norway does not have a national uniform minimum wage applicable to all private sector employees, but statutory wage floors exist in generalised industries such as construction, electrical trades, cleaning, hotel and catering, road freight, agriculture and horticulture, fish processing, shipyards, tourist buses and automotive. Before quoting or issuing an offer, Chinese enterprises should first confirm the employee's industry, actual duties, work location, experience, working hours and applicable collective agreement, and cannot treat market wages or a single industry example as a national uniform statutory standard.

This guide is intended for Chinese enterprise HR, finance, legal and overseas business teams that recruit, sign contracts, run payroll, calculate employer costs, use Employer of Record (EOR) services or handle performance and departure in Norway. The Chinese data is organized according to 2026 rules; specific industry wages, collective agreements, regional employer National Insurance, individual employee tax, cross-border social insurance, work rights and high-risk dismissals still need case-by-case confirmation.

Automotive Industry Introduces New Wage Floor From June 15, 2026, the automotive industry minimum for newly qualified skilled workers is NOK 223.50/hour, at least 1 year experienced skilled workers NOK 237.00; non-skilled workers aged 18 and above NOK 208.00, those with at least 1 year experience NOK 212.00. Employers should update contracts and payroll parameters according to actual work content and experience.

Regional Employer National Insurance Continues Zonal Application In 2026, general industry employer National Insurance is divided by region into different tiers of 0%, 5.1%, 6.4%, 7.9%, 10.6% and 14.1%. Oslo Zone I usually applies 14.1%. Enterprises should confirm based on entity location, business type and employee social insurance coverage, and cannot uniformly apply one rate nationwide.

Employee National Insurance Adjusted to 7.6% In 2026, National Insurance contribution on wage income for employees aged 17 to 69 is usually 7.6%. Employers should withhold according to employee tax cards and social insurance status, and re-determine when a valid A1 or other cross-border coverage certificate exists.

3. Norway Labor Law and Regulatory Framework

Norwegian labor relations are mainly governed by the Working Environment Act, Holidays Act, generalised collective agreements, tax and National Insurance rules, occupational pensions, work injury insurance, equal treatment and data protection provisions. The Labour Inspection Authority, Tax Administration and NAV are respectively responsible for working conditions, payroll tax and social security filing and statutory benefits.

The contract employer bears employment contract, registration, payroll, withholding, employer contributions, leave, occupational health and safety, discipline and termination responsibilities. Client business managers may set work objectives but cannot bypass the contract employer to directly adjust wages, discipline, refuse statutory leave or dismiss EOR employees.

4. Recruitment, Offer and Onboarding

Recruitment conditions should be relevant to the actual requirements of the position and must not improperly screen based on age, gender, nationality, family status or other protected characteristics. The job description should clarify actual duties, work location, reporting line, working hours, shifts, travel, language and objective qualifications.

The offer should separately list basic gross wage, fixed allowances, bonus or commission, overtime arrangements, work location, remote proportion, probation period, contract term and proposed start date. Recruitment platform market wages are not statutory wages, and foreign employee work rights should be verified separately before onboarding.

5. Employment Contracts, Contract Types and Probation Period

Indefinite-term contracts are the default form. Fixed-term contracts may only be used for legally permitted temporary reasons, and should state the endpoint or project, renewal rules and early termination method. Long-term continuous positions should in principle use indefinite-term contracts; repeated renewal may trigger conversion to indefinite-term and compensation risk.

Part-time employees enjoy equal protection, and the contract should state weekly working hours, scheduling and the method of changing working hours. Temporary dispatch is subject to agency qualifications and equal treatment rules. Independent contractor status depends on actual control, organizational integration, economic dependence, substitution rights and risk bearing, not the contract title.

The probation period must be agreed in writing, usually maximum 6 months. Employer termination during probation must still be based on adaptability, competence or reliability, and must first be discussed with the employee; the notice period is usually 14 days, counted from the day after delivery. Employee resignation during probation is usually also 14 days, unless the contract or collective agreement has another lawful provision.

6. Wages, Minimum Wage and Pre-tax Take-home Pay Calculation

Norway does not have a national uniform minimum wage applicable to all positions. Before quotation, it must first be determined whether the position belongs to a generalised industry such as automotive, construction, electrical trades, fish processing, road freight, agriculture and horticulture, hotel and catering, tourist buses, cleaning or shipyards; if not, wages are mainly determined by the employment contract and applicable collective agreement.

7. Working Hours, Overtime and Records

The usual working hours limit is 9 hours/24 hours, 40 hours/7 days; employees usually enjoy 11 consecutive hours of rest per day and 35 consecutive hours of rest per week. Collective agreements often adopt a 37.5-hour workweek.

Overtime exceeding ordinary working hours is paid at least 40% supplement. Even if the parties agree to convert overtime hours into compensatory rest, the 40% cash supplement usually must still be paid. Employers must record actual start and end times, rest, overtime approval and compensation.

Whether remote work, business travel, training, standby and cross-time-zone meetings constitute working hours should be determined by whether the employee is subject to employer direction. A manager position or fixed salary does not automatically exempt maximum working hours, rest and minimum overtime supplement requirements.

8. Statutory Holidays, Annual Leave and Other Statutory Leave

Employees enjoy 25 working days of statutory annual leave per calendar year, with Saturdays counted as working days, usually equivalent to 4 weeks plus 1 day. Employees joining before September 30 can usually request the full 25 days of annual leave for the year, and those joining later can usually request at least 1 week; whether corresponding holiday pay applies depends on the previous year's income.

Holiday pay is usually at least 10.2% of the previous year's qualifying wages; 12% is common when the fifth week of annual leave applies, and the statutory rate for employees over 60 is usually 12.5%. Holiday pay and paid leave are not the same concept, and earned holiday pay should be settled on departure to avoid double-counting with unused annual leave.

Public holidays falling on weekends usually do not automatically receive substitute rest. May 1 and May 17 have special wage protection; other public holiday wages should be handled in conjunction with monthly salary, hourly contracts and collective agreements.

Ordinary sick leave is usually borne by the employer for the first 16 calendar days, after which NAV pays within eligibility and caps. Parental leave benefits are mainly borne by NAV; whether the employer tops up contractual wages depends on the collective agreement, contract or company policy.

The 2026 general industry regional rates include Zone I 14.1%, Zone II 10.6%, Zone III 6.4%, Zone IV 5.1%, Zone IVa 7.9% and Zone V 0%. Entity location, industry and cross-border A1 coverage may change the actual rate.

Employers should complete employee registration, monthly wage filing (a-melding), payroll tax and employer contribution reconciliation. Occupational pension, work injury insurance and holiday pay are not included in the 14.1% employer National Insurance and cannot be omitted.

10. Local Employees and Foreign Employees

Local employees, EEA citizens and other foreign employees in principle enjoy the same minimum labor law protection, but work rights, tax residency and social insurance coverage differ. Non-EEA employees usually need applicable work and residence permits.

Cross-border employees with a valid A1 or other social insurance coverage certificate may be exempt from Norwegian National Insurance contributions. Enterprises should verify nationality, actual work location, permit validity, social insurance coverage and expected start date before hiring. EOR arrangements cannot automatically grant work rights or eliminate cross-border tax risks.

11. Remote Work, Data Privacy and Employee Records

The remote work agreement should state the main work location, office attendance frequency, equipment and expenses, working hours records, occupational safety, information security and cross-border approval. Before an employee works long-term from another country, tax residency, permanent establishment, social insurance, data transfer and work rights should be assessed.

Employee files should follow lawful basis, necessity, access control and retention periods. Health, discipline, payroll and identity documents should have restricted access; monitoring measures must be necessary, proportionate and transparently notified. System access should be closed promptly after departure and equipment and data handover completed.

12. Termination of Employment, Compensation and Final Settlement

Ordinary employer dismissal requires objectively just cause related to the enterprise, employer or employee, and a discussion meeting must be held before the decision. Summary dismissal applies only to serious breach. Fixed-term expiry and early termination must be judged separately; client project completion does not automatically constitute lawful dismissal reason.

Ordinary notice period is at least 1 month; 5 years of service usually 2 months, 10 years usually 3 months. For employees with 10 years of service, employer termination at age 50 usually 4 months, 55 years 5 months, 60 years 6 months. The ordinary notice period usually starts from the first day of the month after the employee receives notice.

Norway does not have a uniform statutory severance payment applicable to all ordinary employees. Pregnancy, family leave, sick leave, trade union or employee representative, whistleblowing, discrimination complaints, work injury and disability may strengthen dismissal protection.

Taking an employee with a monthly salary of NOK 60,000, 6 years of service, dismissed due to genuine restructuring as an example, 2 months' notice period wages are NOK 120,000; if the holiday pay base generated in the year is NOK 300,000, 10.2% holiday pay is NOK 30,600, and the identifiable amount example is NOK 150,600, plus unpaid wages, expenses and earned bonus.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS (FAQ)

Does Norway have a national uniform minimum wage?
No. Norway only stipulates statutory minimum wages for 10 generalised industries; other positions are mainly paid according to employment contracts and applicable collective agreements.

What is the Norwegian employer National Insurance rate in 2026?
The rate is zonal from 0% to 14.1%. Oslo Zone I general industry is usually 14.1%, but entity location, industry and cross-border A1 certificate may change the result.

How many days of annual leave do Norwegian employees have per year?
Statutory annual leave is 25 working days, with Saturdays also counted as working days, usually equivalent to 4 weeks plus 1 day. Collective agreements often provide a fifth week of annual leave.

What is holiday pay?
Usually at least 10.2% of the previous year's qualifying wages; 12% is common when the fifth week of annual leave applies, and the statutory rate for employees over 60 is usually 12.5%.

Can employees be dismissed without reason during probation?
No. Probation termination must usually be related to adaptability, competence or reliability, and must fulfill discussion and written notice procedures, with notice usually 14 days.

Does Norway have uniform statutory severance pay?
There is no uniform statutory severance payment applicable to all ordinary employees, but lawful reason, discussion meeting, notice period and written procedure must still be satisfied, and the contract or collective agreement may provide other compensation.

Can employees be hired without a Norwegian entity?
Enterprises can assess a compliant Employer of Record (EOR) arrangement, but need to confirm case by case the contract employer, dispatch qualification, industry wages, insurance, work permit and actual management boundary.
