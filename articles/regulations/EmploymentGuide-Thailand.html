Minimum Wage, Social Security, Leave, Termination and EOR Compliance

2026 POLICY UPDATE

Minimum Wage Increased: From January 1, 2026, Thailand's daily minimum wage is adjusted by province. Bangkok and surrounding provinces apply THB 372–400/day; other provinces apply THB 337–380/day. Employers should update payroll and contracts accordingly.

Social Security Contribution Ceiling Updated: The 2026 social security contribution wage ceiling remains THB 15,000/month; employer and employee each contribute 5%, with the government contributing 2.75%. Contributions should be calculated on actual wages up to the ceiling.

Personal Income Tax Withholding Tables Updated: The Revenue Department has issued updated 2026 personal income tax withholding tables for employment income. Employers should update payroll systems and recalculate employee net pay.

Hiring employees in Thailand means companies cannot rely on a single national minimum wage or a fixed employer cost percentage. Thailand applies provincial minimum wages, and social security, personal income tax, severance pay, and termination procedures all require separate calculation. Companies should first confirm the employee's actual work province, contract type, wage structure, and employee nationality, then configure payroll and compliance accordingly.

This guide is intended for HR, finance, legal and overseas business teams of Chinese companies recruiting, signing contracts, running payroll, calculating employer costs, using Employer of Record (EOR) services or handling employee separations in Thailand. Amounts in this document are expressed in Thai baht (THB); specific provincial minimum wages, collective agreements, work permits, and foreign employee right to work still need to be confirmed case by case.

3. Thai Labor Law and Regulatory Framework

Thai labor relations are mainly governed by the Labor Protection Act B.E. 2541 (1998) and its amendments, the Social Security Act B.E. 2533 (1990), the Revenue Code, the Civil and Commercial Code, and the Workmen's Compensation Act. The Ministry of Labour and the Department of Labour Protection and Welfare are responsible for labor protection, the Social Security Office administers social security, and the Revenue Department administers tax.

Collective agreements and company work rules may set higher standards for wages, working hours, overtime, leave, and termination. Companies should confirm the applicable rules based on industry, company size, and work location.

The contracting employer bears responsibility for employment contracts, payroll, social security, tax, working environment, leave, and termination. Clients or group managers may provide business input but cannot bypass the Thai employer to directly discipline or dismiss employees.

4. Recruitment, Job Offer and Onboarding

Recruitment must not discriminate based on gender, age, ethnicity, religion, disability, or other protected factors. Job advertisements should state the position, work location, wage structure, working hours, and employment type.

6. Wages, Minimum Wage and Gross-to-Net Calculation

Thailand applies provincial daily minimum wages. Companies should verify the applicable rate for the actual work province before setting wages. Monthly salaried employees must receive at least the equivalent of the daily minimum wage multiplied by 30 days.

Wage ScenarioCalculationResult or Note
Monthly salary exampleTHB 50,000Gross monthly wage
Employer social securityTHB 15,000 × 5%THB 750 (capped at THB 15,000 base)
Employee social securityTHB 15,000 × 5%THB 750 (capped at THB 15,000 base)
Annual gross wageTHB 50,000 × 12THB 600,000

Payslips should show gross wage, social security, tax withholding, provident fund, and net wage. Employee net pay depends on tax table, individual circumstances, and applicable deductions.

Overtime, holiday pay, and bonuses should be itemized separately. Collective agreement benefits may add further wage components.

10. Local Employees and Foreign Employees

Local and foreign employees in principle enjoy the same labor law protections. Foreign employees must have a work permit consistent with the employer, position, and work location. EOR arrangements do not automatically resolve work permit issues.

Cross-border remote work may trigger tax, social security, permanent establishment, and data protection issues. Employers should assess case by case.

11. Remote Work, Data Privacy and Employee Records

Remote work should be agreed in writing, specifying work location, equipment, expenses, working hours, and data security. The Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) applies to employee data processing.

Employee records should be retained per statutory requirements and include contracts, payslips, working time, leave, and termination documents.

Statutory severance pay in Thailand is generally based on service period: 30 days' wages for 120 days to less than 1 year; 90 days for 1–3 years; 180 days for 3–6 years; 240 days for 6–10 years; 300 days for 10–20 years; 400 days for 20 years or more. Employers should pay within 30 days of termination.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

What is the minimum wage in Thailand in 2026?
Thailand applies provincial daily minimum wages. Bangkok and surrounding provinces apply THB 372–400/day; other provinces apply THB 337–380/day. Companies must verify the rate for the employee's actual work province.

What is the employer social security rate in Thailand?
Employer and employee each contribute 5% of wages up to a ceiling of THB 15,000/month. The government contributes 2.75%.

How many days of annual leave do Thai employees have?
At least 6 working days per year after 1 year of service. Collective agreements may provide more.

How is severance pay calculated in Thailand?
Severance pay is based on service period, ranging from 30 days' wages for 120 days to less than 1 year, up to 400 days' wages for 20 years or more.

Can I use an EOR to hire in Thailand?
Yes, but you must verify the contracting employer, work permit, social security registration, and actual management boundaries. EOR does not automatically resolve all compliance issues.
