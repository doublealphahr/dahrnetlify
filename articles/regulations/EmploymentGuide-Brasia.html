Minimum Wage, INSS, FGTS, Leave, Termination, and EOR Compliance

2026 POLICY UPDATE

National minimum wage raised to BRL 1,621
From January 1, 2026, the national minimum wage is adjusted from BRL 1,518 to BRL 1,621. Employers should update employment contracts, Payroll, and eSocial, and continue to verify higher standards set by state or CBA.

INSS and personal income tax parameters updated
From January 2026, the employee INSS contribution base ceiling is raised to BRL 8,475.55, and a new monthly tax reduction mechanism applies to personal income tax. Employers should update the payroll calculation engine and re-measure employee take-home pay.

eSocial and FGTS Digital technical rules upgraded
From May to July 2026, labor dispute FGTS handling, eSocial S-1.3, and alphanumeric CNPJ preparation enter new implementation stages. Employers and service providers should complete system compatibility, interface, and filing continuity checks.

Brazilian labor relations are centered on the Consolidation of Labor Laws (Consolidação das Leis do Trabalho, CLT), overlaid with the Federal Constitution, social security, tax, Employment Guarantee Fund (FGTS), occupational health and safety, General Data Protection Law (LGPD), and collective agreement requirements. Companies cannot estimate costs only with "monthly salary plus one fixed ratio"; the employee's state and city, industry and position collective bargaining agreement (CBA), company tax regime, occupational risk level, and benefit policy all change actual results.

This guide is intended for Chinese company HR, finance, legal, and overseas business teams planning to hire directly in Brazil, use Employer of Record (EOR) services, manage remote employees, run payroll, or handle departure. The content is organized based on materials verified as of July 20, 2026; before formal quotation and execution, the position, CBA, tax regime, and system basis should still be reviewed by a Brazilian payroll service provider, lawyer, or tax advisor.

2. Three Rules to Watch Most in 2026

2.1 National minimum wage adjusted to BRL 1,621

Per Decreto No. 12.797/2025, from January 1, 2026, the national minimum wage is adjusted to BRL 1,621/month. Employers need to update contracts, payroll systems, and eSocial wage information, but must not therefore ignore higher wage and benefit standards agreed in state minimum wage or CBA.

2.2 INSS and personal income tax parameters updated

In 2026, employee INSS continues to be calculated at progressive rates of 7.5%–14%, with the contribution base ceiling adjusted to BRL 8,475.55.

Personal income tax withholding, in addition to the progressive tax table, also applies the 2026 monthly reduction mechanism: monthly income up to BRL 5,000 may be reduced to zero, and BRL 5,000.01–7,350 applies decreasing reduction. Payroll systems cannot estimate take-home pay only by applying the 27.5% maximum rate.

2.3 eSocial and FGTS Digital enter new technical implementation stage

From July 2026, eSocial S-1.3 and related technical notes enter production application, in coordination with alphanumeric CNPJ implementation preparation; from May 1, 2026, some labor litigation or labor claim related FGTS collection is also handled through FGTS Digital per official arrangement.

Companies should confirm payroll and HR system compatibility to avoid filing interruption or departure FGTS calculation errors.

3. Brazil Labor Law and Regulatory Framework

Brazilian labor relations are mainly governed by CLT, the Federal Constitution, social security and tax law, FGTS system, occupational health and safety norms, and collective agreements.

An employment relationship is usually judged based on remuneration, personality, continuity, and subordination. Even if the contract is written as an independent contractor (PJ), if an individual works long-term according to company scheduling, instructions, and management, it may still be reclassified as a CLT employee.

Main authorities and systems include:

  1. Brazilian Ministry of Labor and Employment;
  2. Brazilian Federal Revenue;
  3. National Social Security Institute (INSS);
  4. Caixa Econômica Federal and FGTS management system;
  5. eSocial;
  6. National Data Protection Authority (ANPD).

Labor law is mainly federal, but state minimum wage, city and state holidays, industry CBA, occupational risk rate, and company tax regime significantly affect actual compliance results.

Probation is not a "zero-cost dismissal period."

When a probation contract naturally expires without renewal, wage balance, proportional 13th month, proportional annual leave and 1/3, and FGTS deposit generally still need to be settled.

If the employer terminates the probation contract early, it may need to pay compensation equal to half of the remaining term wage; if the contract has a mutual early termination clause, it may instead be handled per indefinite-term dismissal rules.

6. Wages, Minimum Wage, and Gross-to-Net Calculation

6.1 Minimum Wage Judgment Order

Companies should verify in sequence:

  1. Federal national minimum wage;
  2. State minimum wage of employee work location;
  3. Applicable CBA or industry position wage floor;
  4. Special requirements such as professional qualification, commission, field work, hazardous or unhealthy work.

The final contract wage should adopt the standard more favorable to the employee and actually applicable. Companies cannot use bonus, reimbursement, meal allowance, or transport allowance to fill the basic wage gap.

Calculation also needs to overlay the 2026 monthly tax reduction mechanism:

8.2 Annual Leave

After completing 12-month acquisition period, employees generally enjoy 30 calendar days annual leave, arranged by employer within the following 12 months.

Absence without cause reduces annual leave days per law:

Absence Without Cause in Acquisition Period General Annual Leave Days
Up to 5 days30 days
6–14 days24 days
15–23 days18 days
24–32 days12 days
Over 32 daysMay lose the annual leave for that period

Annual leave pay should include normal wage and at least 1/3 holiday bonus, generally paid before leave.

Annual leave may be split into maximum three segments with employee consent, one segment at least 14 days and the rest at least 5 days each. Employer delay in arranging annual leave may generate double payment risk.

8.3 13th Month Wage

13th month wage is generally accrued at 1/12 for each qualifying month, with 15 days of work in a month generally counted as one month.

The first installment may generally be paid between February and November, and the second installment generally no later than December 20. At departure, proportional 13th month for qualifying months in the year should also be settled.

8.4 Sick Leave, Maternity Leave, and Paternity Leave

Ordinary sick leave first 15 days are generally borne by employer, and after 15 days handled per INSS benefit rules, but employee needs to meet participation and medical certificate conditions.

Maternity leave is generally 120 days, paternity leave generally 5 days. Companies participating in Empresa Cidadã or applicable CBA may provide longer period.

Employees who are pregnant, postpartum, work-injured, or have occupational disease may enjoy stability protection, and termination must be individually checked before hiring.

10. Local Employees and Foreign Employees

Local employee onboarding generally involves:

  1. CPF;
  2. Identity and address proof;
  3. Bank account;
  4. Digital work card (CTPS Digital);
  5. PIS, PASEP, or NIT;
  6. Dependent information;
  7. Occupational health certificate.

Foreign employees must complete the same CLT and payroll registration, and must also have residence and work authorization matching the actual work activity.

Business visit cannot replace local work permit. Companies should verify identity and authorization pathway before issuing Offer, and assess the impact of foreign employee departure, transfer, or employer change on residence status.

Wages paid by overseas headquarters or employees working remotely do not automatically exclude Brazilian labor, payroll, tax, and social security obligations.

11. Remote Work, Data Privacy, and Employee Records

Remote or hybrid work should clarify through contract or supplementary agreement:

  1. Work location;
  2. Equipment provision and ownership;
  3. Network and office expenses;
  4. Information security;
  5. Working hours management;
  6. Remote arrangement change mechanism;
  7. Equipment return and departure clearance.

Employees working long-term in Brazil, even if client or headquarters is overseas, generally still need to assess Brazilian labor, payroll, tax, and data compliance responsibility.

Under LGPD, companies processing employee data must have lawful basis and comply with purpose limitation, minimum necessity, access control, and security requirements.

Health, work injury, background check, and biometric information need stricter protection. When transferring employee data to China headquarters, EOR, payroll provider, or HRIS, the transfer purpose, sharing recipient, security measures, and retention period should be recorded.

Termination amounts generally should be paid within maximum 10 days after contract termination, and complete:

  1. eSocial termination event;
  2. FGTS Digital calculation and release;
  3. Termination documents;
  4. Wage and entitlement settlement;
  5. Asset return;
  6. System access closure;
  7. Confidentiality and IP reminder.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

What is the 2026 Brazil national minimum wage?
From January 1, 2026, the national minimum wage is BRL 1,621/month. However, state minimum wage, industry or position wage floor, and CBA may stipulate higher standards, and companies must continue to verify based on employee location and position.

Can Brazil employer cost be calculated with a fixed ratio?
No. Employer INSS, RAT/FAP, third-party contribution, FGTS, 13th month, annual leave 1/3, CBA benefits, and company tax regime all change the result. A fixed ratio can only be used as a scenario example with all assumptions stated.

Is 13th month pay mandatory in Brazil?
Yes. 13th month wage is generally accrued at 1/12 for each qualifying month and paid in two installments; proportional amount should also be calculated at departure.

How many annual leave days do Brazilian employees have?
After completing 12-month acquisition period, employees generally enjoy 30 calendar days annual leave, and receive additional bonus of at least 1/3 of annual leave pay. Absence without cause may reduce annual leave days.

What is the maximum probation period in Brazil?
Probation contract generally total term not exceeding 90 days. Employees still enjoy basic labor rights during probation, and employer early termination may also generate compensation.

What is the FGTS rate for ordinary employees in Brazil?
FGTS for ordinary CLT employees is generally 8% of wage remuneration, borne by employer. Apprentices generally apply 2%. Employer without-cause dismissal may also bear 40% penalty of relevant deposit base.

What must be paid when dismissing an employee without cause in Brazil?
Generally need to handle notice period or payment in lieu, wage balance, proportional 13th month, accrued and proportional annual leave and 1/3, FGTS release, and 40% penalty, and check whether employee is in pregnancy, work injury, union representative, or other protected period.

Can a company hire employees without a Brazilian entity?
Companies may evaluate a compliant Employer of Record (EOR), but must confirm position, work city, CBA, Payroll, client management boundary, data responsibility, and foreign employee work rights. EOR cannot eliminate all labor relationship risk.
