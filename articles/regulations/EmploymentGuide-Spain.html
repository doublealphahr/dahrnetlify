Minimum Wage, Social Security, Working Hours, Leave and Termination Compliance

2026 POLICY UPDATE

Minimum Wage Rises to EUR 1,221: From January 1, 2026, the minimum wage rises to EUR 1,221/month and EUR 17,094/year. Companies should update 12/14-month pay, part-time ratios and collective agreement wage comparisons accordingly.

MEI Contribution Increased to 0.90%: In 2026, the Intergenerational Equity Mechanism contribution rises to a total of 0.90%, with employers bearing 0.75% and employees 0.15%. Payroll systems and cost budgets must both be adjusted.

Maximum Contribution Base Updated: The 2026 ordinary regime monthly maximum contribution base is EUR 5,101.20. Wages above the ceiling may still be subject to the high-earner solidarity contribution, and companies must not stop all related contributions after reaching the ceiling.

Hiring employees in Spain means companies cannot simply look at the national minimum wage or use a single unified "employer social security percentage." Companies must first confirm the employee's province and city, applicable collective agreement (Convenio Colectivo) and job group, then complete budgeting and payroll calculation in conjunction with annual wages, 12- or 14-month structure, contract type, working hours, occupational risk and social security parameters.

This guide is intended for HR, finance, legal and overseas business teams of Chinese companies recruiting, signing contracts, running payroll, calculating employer costs, using Employer of Record (EOR) services or handling performance, redundancy and separation in Spain. The content uses 2026 national rules and the Madrid holiday calendar as examples; collective agreements, occupational risk rates, autonomous community and municipal holidays, personal income tax and individual employee circumstances still need to be confirmed case by case.

3. Spanish Labor Law and Regulatory Framework

Spanish labor relations are mainly governed by the Workers' Statute (Estatuto de los Trabajadores), social security, occupational risk prevention, equal employment, collective agreements and data protection regulations. The Official State Gazette (BOE) publishes laws and the annual minimum wage, the Ministry of Labor and Social Economy is responsible for labor policy, the General Treasury of Social Security (TGSS) is responsible for registration and contributions, the State Public Employment Service (SEPE) is responsible for contract and unemployment-related procedures, and the Tax Agency is responsible for personal income tax (IRPF).

Collective agreements may determine job groups, wage tables, annual working hours, overtime, holidays, probationary periods and notice periods. Companies should confirm the applicable agreement based on industry, company activity, work location and position, and cannot judge only by contract name or headquarters location.

The contracting employer is responsible for employment contracts, TGSS registration, payslips, tax and social security, working time records, occupational health and safety, leave and termination. Clients or group managers may provide business facts, but cannot bypass the Spanish employer to directly discipline or dismiss employees.

Fixed-term contracts exceeding one year generally require at least 15 days' notice upon expiry; whether expiry compensation is 12 days' wages per year and specific exceptions depend on the contract type. Unlawful use of temporary contracts may result in the employment relationship being deemed open-ended.

The probationary period must be agreed in writing and primarily follow the collective agreement. Where no collective agreement rule exists, it is generally up to 6 months for technical qualification positions and 2 months for other employees; non-technical positions in enterprises with fewer than 25 employees may be up to 3 months; fixed-term contracts of no more than 6 months are generally up to 1 month, unless the collective agreement provides otherwise.

During probation, ordinary dismissal grounds or notice are generally not required, but discrimination, retaliation or violation of special protection is prohibited. Prior performance of the same duties, absence of written agreement or exceeding the statutory cap may all render the probation clause invalid.

6. Wages, Minimum Wage and Gross-to-Net Calculation

The 2026 Spanish minimum wage is EUR 40.70/day or EUR 1,221/month, and the full-time annual gross wage must not be less than EUR 17,094. Companies should first check the applicable collective agreement and job group wage table, then compare with the national annual minimum wage and apply the higher.

Wage ScenarioCalculationResult or Note
14-month minimum annual wageEUR 1,221 × 14EUR 17,094
Spread over 12 monthsEUR 17,094 ÷ 12EUR 1,424.50/month
50% part-timeEUR 17,094 × 50%EUR 8,547/year; agreement hourly wage must still be checked
Annual salary EUR 36,000 on 14-month payEUR 36,000 ÷ 14Approx. EUR 2,571.43 gross per payment

Payments in kind cannot reduce the minimum cash wage payable. The payslip should separately list basic wage, wage supplements, extra payment allocation, overtime, taxable benefits, employee social security and personal income tax. Reimbursements must not be used to fill the minimum wage, and whether collective agreement wage items can offset or absorb must also be determined item by item.

After minimum wage or collective agreement wage table adjustments, companies should screen employees by effectivity date, recalculate basic wage, fixed supplements, two extra payments, part-time ratios and social security bases, and simultaneously update contract notices, payslips, budgets and client quotations.

Spain generally has up to 14 paid labor holidays per location that in principle cannot be substituted by compensatory rest, composed of national, autonomous community and municipal holidays. The above Madrid calendar cannot be directly used for Barcelona or other cities; holiday work, compensatory rest and premiums must also be checked against the collective agreement.

Sick leave, birth and care leave generally involve contract suspension, social security benefits and collective agreement top-ups. Companies should distinguish annual leave, public holidays, temporary illness, birth or care leave and other paid permits in the system, and cannot uniformly deduct them from annual leave balances.

10. Local Employees and Foreign Employees

Local and foreign employees in principle both enjoy Spanish minimum wage, collective agreement, working hours, annual leave, occupational safety, anti-discrimination and dismissal rules. Non-EU employees must also have a work permit consistent with the employer, position, wage and work location.

Employers should verify the right to work before onboarding and contract changes. Cross-border remote work by employees may also trigger social security affiliation, A1 certificate, personal income tax, permanent establishment and cross-border data issues; using an Employer of Record service does not automatically resolve these obligations.

11. Remote Work, Data Privacy and Employee Records

When regular remote work reaches the statutory scope of application, a written remote work agreement is generally required, specifying equipment, costs, work location, working hours, control method and return-to-office mechanism.

Employee data is governed by the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), Spanish data protection law and Spanish Data Protection Agency (AEPD) rules. Digital monitoring, geolocation, screen recording and AI performance tools must have a lawful purpose, necessity and proportionality, and complete transparent notice and applicable employee representative procedures.

Companies should retain employment contracts, collective agreement assessments, payslips, social security, working time, leave, occupational safety, performance, discipline, employee representative procedures and termination delivery evidence. Cross-border remote work must be reviewed in advance for tax, social security, permanent establishment, right to work and data issues.

12. Termination of Employment, Compensation and Offboarding Settlement

Companies should first distinguish probation termination, disciplinary dismissal, objective dismissal, collective redundancy, fixed-term expiry, employee resignation and mutual agreement termination. Paying compensation or notice pay cannot replace lawful reason and statutory procedure.

ScenarioProcedure and NoticeMain Settlement
Employer termination during probationGenerally no ordinary notice during valid probation, but must be in writing, non-discriminatory and review protection statusWages, unused annual leave, pro-rata extra payments and social security exit
Employee resignationNotice period mainly based on collective agreement or contract, commonly 15 days but not uniform nationwideWages, annual leave, extra payments and other earned amounts
Disciplinary dismissalSerious and culpable, written statement of facts and effective date, and compliance with agreement or employee representative procedureGenerally no ordinary notice and 20-day compensation, but final settlement still required
Objective dismissalWritten reason, generally 15 days' notice and 20 days' wages per year of service simultaneously, capped at 12 monthsCompensation, notice period, wages, annual leave and extra payments
Fixed-term expiryContracts exceeding 1 year generally 15 days' notice; 12 days' wages per year of service where applicableWages, annual leave, extra payments and expiry compensation
Collective redundancyWhen statutory headcount and period thresholds are reached, consultation and employee representative procedures are requiredSelection criteria, compensation, notice and administrative documents

When a court finds unfair dismissal, the employer generally chooses between reinstatement or payment of 33 days' wages per year of service, capped at 24 months; historical service periods may apply transitional rules. If the dismissal is void, reinstatement and back pay are generally required. The 33 days/year is not a fixed price for companies to purchase "dismissal without reason" in advance.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

What is the minimum wage in Spain in 2026?
EUR 1,221 per month in 14 payments, or EUR 17,094 per year; EUR 40.70 per day.

Can the 14 payments be spread over 12 months?
Yes, the two extra payments may be prorated monthly, but the annual gross wage must still meet the minimum threshold.

What is the maximum contribution base in 2026?
EUR 5,101.20 per month for the ordinary regime. High earners may also be subject to a solidarity contribution above this ceiling.

How is severance calculated for objective dismissal?
20 days' wages per year of service, capped at 12 monthly payments.

Can I use an EOR to hire in Spain?
Yes, but you must verify the EOR's legal employer status, collective agreement applicability, work permits, and actual management boundaries.
