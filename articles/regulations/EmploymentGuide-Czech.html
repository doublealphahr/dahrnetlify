Minimum Wage, Social Security, Statutory Leave, and Termination Compliance

2026 POLICY UPDATE

Minimum hourly wage raised
From January 1, 2026, the minimum hourly wage for employees aged 20 and over is raised; companies should update youth proportional brackets, industry wage comparison, and actual working hours testing.

MyFutureFund automatic pension launched
From 2026, eligible employees and employers each contribute 1.5% of gross wage, with the state subsidizing 0.5%; companies should complete pension eligibility identification and avoid duplicate participation.

PRSI and retirement age rules updated
From June 29, 2026, new contractual retirement age rights take effect; from October 1, Class A PRSI rate increases; employers should update retirement policy, payroll system, and annual budget separately.

Czechia has a relatively complete labor law, social security, and payroll filing system with clear procedural requirements. For Chinese companies planning to recruit or manage teams in Czechia, compliance involves not only whether wages reach the statutory floor, but also contract type, probation, working hours and overtime records, employee registration, tax and contribution withholding, annual leave accrual, and termination reason and delivery procedure.

This guide is intended for Chinese company HR, finance, legal, and overseas business teams that recruit, sign contracts, configure payroll, calculate employer costs, use Employer of Record (EOR) services, or handle performance and departure in Czechia. The data in this document is organized per 2026 rules; industry collective agreements, risk positions, temporary work agreements, foreign employee status, and individual approval still need to be reviewed before execution.

3. Czech Labor Law and Regulatory Framework

Czech local labor relations are simultaneously governed by the Labor Code, collective agreements or industry rules, tax and social security, public health insurance, occupational safety, equal employment, and personal data rules.

The Ministry of Labour and Social Affairs (MPSV) is responsible for labor policy and regulatory information, the Czech Social Security Administration (ČSSZ) is responsible for social insurance and related employee registration, the tax authority is responsible for wage income tax, and the State Labour Inspection Office is responsible for labor conditions and occupational safety supervision.

The contract employer is responsible for signing, registration, payroll, employee withholding, employer contributions, leave, occupational health and safety, discipline, and termination. Business managers may set work goals and manage daily performance, but must not bypass the contract employer to directly change wages, discipline, or dismiss EOR employees.

Companies should judge compliance requirements in the following order: determine contract employer and actual work location; identify employee and contract type; verify minimum wage or collective agreement; clarify gross wage and each component; configure tax, social security, and health insurance; establish working hours and leave records; re-verify all parameters at personnel change or departure.

4. Recruitment, Offer, and Onboarding

Job descriptions should truthfully state position duties, work location, reporting line, working hours or shifts, travel, language, and objective qualifications, and should not use age, gender, nationality, or family status requirements unrelated to the position. Market wages displayed on recruitment platforms cannot replace statutory minimum wage or applicable collective agreement standard.

The Offer should separately state gross wage, basic wage, allowances, bonus or commission, working hours, work location, probation, and expected onboarding date.

Background checks should be limited to necessity and lawful basis, with restricted access and retention period for health, criminal, credit, and reference information.

From July 1, 2026, registration must be completed no later than before the employee actually starts work. Non-foreign employees may first complete partial registration, with remaining information generally completed within 8 days after employee starts work; if the employee ultimately does not report, the employer should update ČSSZ within the prescribed period.

5. Employment Contracts, Contract Types, and Probation

Employment contracts should be in written form and at least state parties, start date, work location, and position duties. For ease of execution, wage and payment cycle, working hours, annual leave, probation, notice period, applicable collective agreement or policy, bonus or commission, confidentiality and IP, personal data, and equipment arrangement should also be stated.

Contract Type Applicable Scenario Main Requirements and Risks
Indefinite-term contractLong-term continuous positionCommon form; termination must follow statutory path
Fixed-term contractClear temporary task or projectGenerally cumulative not exceeding 3 years and maximum 2 renewals; statutory exceptions verified separately
Part-time contractBelow full-time hoursAgree weekly working hours in writing; annual leave calculated by hourly proportion; minimum wage still applies
Work performance agreement (DPP) or work activity agreement (DPČ)Short-term or limited working hours workSubject to working hours, registration, annual leave, and participation threshold; cannot be used to circumvent labor law
Independent contractingGenuinely independent operationActual control, organizational integration, and economic dependence may lead to reclassification as employee

Fixed-term contracts should state termination date or project at signing, and clarify whether early termination is permitted. Repeated renewal to circumvent indefinite-term protection may lead to disputes.

When position, wage, work location, working hours, or remote work changes materially, written agreement or statutory procedure should be obtained.

Ordinary employee probation may be agreed in writing for maximum 4 months, management maximum 8 months; fixed-term contract probation must not exceed half of contract term.

Both parties may terminate in writing during probation generally without stating reason, but must not be based on discrimination or retaliatory reason, and employer must not terminate during the first 14 calendar days of employee temporary sick leave.

6. Wages, Minimum Wage, and Gross-to-Net Calculation

The 2026 Czech minimum wage is CZK 22,400/month or CZK 134.40/hour for a 40-hour workweek.

Overtime, public holiday, night shift, difficult environment, and Saturday/Sunday work statutory surcharges are not included in minimum wage comparison. The private sector's original graded guaranteed wage has been cancelled, but the public pay system still has guaranteed salary groups, and industry collective agreements may stipulate higher standards.

Payroll calculation should first determine contract weekly working hours and current period working hours, then use applicable minimum hourly wage or collective agreement monthly salary to test normal working hours wage, and finally add overtime, night shift, weekend, and public holiday compensation separately.

Onboarding or departure month should use locally recognized working day or hourly base, and should not be uniformly averaged by calendar day.

Payslips should separately state normal wage, overtime and night shift, public holiday, fixed allowances, expense reimbursement, bonus or commission, annual leave pay, employee deductions, and employer items.

Bonus clauses should clarify trigger conditions, assessment period, approval, acquisition date, payment date, departure handling, and recovery rules.

7. Working Hours, Overtime, and Records

Standard weekly working hours are generally 40 hours; two-shift system generally 38.75 hours, three-shift and continuous operation generally 37.5 hours. Specific contract working hours, scheduling, and rest arrangements may also be affected by collective agreements and industry rules.

Item Main Rule Employer Action
Employer-directed overtimeGenerally no more than 150 hours/yearPre-approve and record actual working hours and business reason
Additional agreed overtimeWith employee consent, may exceed 150 hoursOverall average generally must not exceed 8 hours/week, and comply with reference period limit
Overtime compensationGenerally pay normal wage plus at least 25% average wage surcharge, or arrange compensatory restPayslip separately shows wage and surcharge or compensatory rest balance
Night shift, weekend, and public holidayApply separate statutory compensationMust not use overtime surcharge to replace other mandatory surcharges
Working hours recordsRecord scheduling, actual start and end, rest, overtime, and compensationRemote, travel, training, on-call, and cross-time-zone meetings also judged by actual situation

Manager title does not automatically exempt working hours rules. Fixed salary or all-inclusive arrangement also cannot cancel mandatory rest, maximum working hours, and minimum statutory compensation.

Companies should keep auditable scheduling, working hours, overtime approval, and compensation records.

8. Public Holidays, Annual Leave, and Other Statutory Leave

Private sector employees generally enjoy at least 4 weeks annual leave per year, public sector generally 5 weeks, teachers may enjoy longer leave.

Annual leave is calculated by hours: employees generally acquire the right after continuous at least 4 weeks of employment relationship and working 4 times the weekly working hours; those not completing a full year gradually accrue per statutory rules.

Part-time employees should not be simply halved by days. For example, an employee working 20 hours/week with 4 weeks annual leave per year, full-year basic entitlement is generally 80 hours.

Unused statutory annual leave at departure may be cashed out at average wage; in-service cash substitution for statutory annual leave is generally restricted.

When a public holiday falls on a normal working day and the employee does not work, monthly salary generally should not be reduced.

Public holiday work generally prioritizes paid compensatory rest and normal wage, or per agreement pays surcharge equal to 100% of average wage. Holiday overlapping with weekend generally does not automatically generate additional substitute rest day.

The first 14 calendar days of sick leave are paid by employer at statutory reduced average income for qualifying working days, and thereafter generally paid by ČSSZ as sickness benefit.

Maternity leave is generally 28 weeks, multiple births generally 37 weeks; fathers may receive paternity benefit per law. Parental leave and government parental allowance are different systems and should be managed separately.

9. Employer Social Security, Mandatory Benefits, and Tax

Employer is responsible for employee registration, calculating and withholding employee share, paying employer share, filing, reconciliation, and departure deregistration.

Employer-borne items must not be transferred as employee deductions, and social security, health insurance, and work injury insurance must not be merged into one opaque total ratio.

10. Local Employees and Foreign Employees

Local employees and foreign employees are in principle both protected by Czech labor law, minimum wage, working hours, annual leave, occupational safety, and termination rules.

Foreign employees must also hold work and residence authorization matching the contract entity, position, and work location; an employment contract or EOR arrangement itself cannot automatically generate work rights.

Employers should verify permit status before onboarding, and re-assess when permit, position, work location, contract entity, or remote work country changes.

From April 1, 2026, foreign employees already need to complete relevant registration before actually starting work; from July 1, the pre-onboarding registration requirement extends to all employees.

Cross-border work may also involve A1 or other social security coverage certificate, tax residence status, permanent establishment, and data transfer. Companies cannot assume that all cross-border work continues to apply Czech tax and social security just because the employee is contracted by a Czech entity.

11. Remote Work, Data Privacy, and Employee Records

Remote work agreements should state main work location, on-site frequency, equipment and expenses, working hours records, occupational health and safety, information security, and cross-border work approval.

Before an employee works long-term from another country, tax residence status, permanent establishment, social security or A1, data transfer, and work authorization should be reviewed.

Employee files should be managed per necessity, lawful basis, and retention period. Health, discipline, wage, tax, and identity data should have restricted access; background check data and information no longer needed after departure should be promptly deleted or archived.

Companies should keep employment contracts, employee registration receipt, payslips, tax and social security filing, working hours, annual leave, sick leave, performance, discipline, termination, asset, and access records.

At departure, final settlement, authority deregistration, equipment recovery, and system access closure should be completed in the same checklist.

12. Termination, Compensation, and Final Settlement

Outside probation, employer termination of employment contract must comply with statutory reasons listed in the Labor Code, be in writing, and specifically state the reason. Termination reason generally cannot be arbitrarily replaced after a dispute arises.

Employee resignation generally does not require stating reason, but should be in writing and comply with notice period.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

What is the 2026 minimum wage in Czechia?
From January 1, 2026, the minimum hourly wage for employees aged 20 and over is CZK 134.40/hour, and the monthly wage for a 40-hour workweek is CZK 22,400.

Do Czech annual salaried employees still need minimum hourly wage testing?
Yes. Employer should test within a wage reference period of maximum one month by dividing countable wage by actual working hours, and cannot look only at contract annual salary.

What is the 2026 employer social security rate in Czechia?
For ordinary employees, employer social security is generally 24.8%, plus 9% employer health insurance. Risk positions increase to 27.8%, medical rescue personnel and enterprise firefighters increase to 29.8%.

Which employees need to join MyFutureFund?
Generally employees aged 23–60, with annual income of at least CZK 20,000, and no other payroll pension. In 2026–2028, employee and employer each contribute 1.5%, with state subsidy 0.5%.

How many annual leave days do Czech employees have per year?
Most employees enjoy at least 4 normal working weeks. Annual leave accrues from onboarding, calculated by hours; part-time employees should not be simply halved by days.

Is Good Friday a statutory public holiday in Czechia?
Yes. Czechia has 13 statutory public holidays in 2026, including Good Friday and Easter Monday.

How is statutory redundancy pay calculated in Czechia?
When eligible, generally 1 month average wage for less than 1 year service, 2 months for 1 to less than 2 years, and 3 months for 2 years or more. Work injury or occupational disease may apply higher special compensation.

Can a company hire employees through EOR without a Czech entity?
Feasibility assessment is possible, but contract employer, work rights, Payroll, social security, health insurance, work injury, CBA, client management boundary, and termination responsibility must be verified.
