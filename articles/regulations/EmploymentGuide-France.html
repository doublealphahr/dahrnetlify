Minimum Wage, Social Insurance, Paid Leave and Termination Compliance

2026 POLICY UPDATE

Minimum wage increased mid-year — From June 1, 2026, the SMIC rises to EUR 12.31 per hour and EUR 1,867.02 per month for a 35-hour schedule. Employers must update wages, overtime bases and relief calculations.

Social security ceiling updated — From January 1, 2026, the PASS rises to EUR 48,060 and the PMSS to EUR 4,005. Employers must update capped items such as basic and supplementary pensions accordingly.

Contribution and relief parameters adjusted — In 2026, the employer rate for uncapped basic pension rises to 2.11%, and rules such as the general unified degressive relief (RGDU) are updated. Companies should recalculate through the current payroll system rather than using an old cost rate.

The French labor system is shaped by the Labor Code, national statutory standards, sector collective agreements (Convention Collective), company agreements and employment contracts. Companies cannot design an employment solution based only on the statutory minimum wage or a fixed employer cost ratio. Job classification, company headcount, work location and employee category all change wage, insurance, working time and termination obligations.

This guide is intended for HR, legal, finance and overseas business teams of Chinese companies planning to recruit or manage employees in France. It covers key issues such as recruitment and onboarding, employment contracts, payroll calculation, leave, social insurance, remote work, termination of employment and Employer of Record (EOR) services.

3. French Labor Law and Regulatory Framework

Employment relationships in France are mainly governed by the Labor Code, the social security system, sector collective agreements, company agreements and employment contracts.

The Ministry of Labor and labor inspection authorities are responsible for labor policy and enforcement; URSSAF administers most social contributions; Agirc-Arrco administers supplementary pensions; France Travail handles unemployment and departure certificates; and the French National Commission for Information Technology and Liberties (CNIL) regulates personal data.

The applicable collective agreement may affect job minimum wages, probation periods, overtime, bonuses, leave, supplementary insurance and notice periods. An English job title cannot replace French job classification. Companies should complete a written assessment based on main business, APE code, job duties and the scope of the agreement.

4. Recruitment, Offer Letters and Onboarding

Recruitment methods must be directly related to the role and explained to candidates in advance. Companies must not collect candidates' family plans, health status, religion, political views or other sensitive information unrelated to the role, nor discriminate based on protected characteristics.

Stage Employer Action Evidence to Retain
Before offer Confirm work location, job classification, collective agreement, salary, working hours, contract type and budget Job and collective agreement assessment records
Contract State duties, location, salary, working hours, probation, leave, notice, bonuses, remote work, confidentiality and intellectual property Signed contract and policy acknowledgements by both parties
Pre-employment declaration Complete the pre-employment declaration (DPAE) with URSSAF before the employee starts work Declaration receipt and submission time
Payroll and insurance Collect identity, social security number, bank and tax information, and set up supplementary health and provident insurance Payroll master data and insurance certificates
Health, safety and data Complete risk assessment, occupational health, equipment, access rights and privacy notice DUERP, training and equipment records

The offer letter should state the contract type, work location, weekly working hours, gross salary, collective agreement and job classification, remote work percentage and overtime arrangements.

Wages must be compared with both the SMIC and the applicable collective agreement minimum wage, and the higher standard applies. Meal reimbursements, transport allowances or uncertain bonuses cannot be used to fill the gap between basic pay and the statutory or collective agreement minimum wage.

For foreign candidates, companies should verify before onboarding whether the right to work and the contracting entity are consistent. Specific visa, residence and work permit routes should be assessed separately.

5. Employment Contracts, Contract Types and Probation Periods

Temporary agency work Genuine temporary need The agency is the employer, but the user company still bears safety and equal treatment responsibilities
Apprenticeship and vocational training contract Combines training and work Special rules apply to pay, age, training provider and funding
Independent contracting Genuinely independent business If the client controls time, place and working methods, there is a risk of reclassification as employment

When a CDD expires, wages, untaken leave and any applicable precarious employment allowance should normally be settled, commonly 10% of gross pay. A collective agreement may reduce this to 6% when accompanied by training conditions, and certain statutory exceptions do not require payment.

After probation, a CDD may be terminated early only through statutory routes such as mutual agreement, the employee moving to a CDI, serious misconduct, force majeure or medical unfitness. Unlawful early termination may trigger a risk of paying wages for the remaining contract term.

6. Wages, Minimum Wage and Gross-to-Net Salary Calculation

From June 1, 2026, the SMIC applicable in mainland France, Guadeloupe, French Guiana, Martinique, Reunion and elsewhere is EUR 12.31 per hour and EUR 1,867.02 gross per month for a 35-hour schedule.

Mayotte applies EUR 9.56 per hour and EUR 1,449.93 per month. Paris does not have a separate city minimum wage, but collective agreement job minimum wages may exceed the SMIC.

The wage floor should be determined in the following order:

  1. Identify the applicable collective agreement and job classification;
  2. Obtain the current collective agreement minimum wage;
  3. Compare with the SMIC applicable to the work location and apply the higher standard;
  4. Verify hourly pay by converting actual working time, preparation time, on-call time or commissions;
  5. Separate basic pay, bonuses, overtime, reimbursements, meal vouchers, transport costs and benefits in kind;
  6. Update payroll parameters when SMIC or collective agreement increases take effect.
Scenario Calculation Reference Result
35-hour full time Official monthly standard EUR 1,867.02/month
20-hour part time per week 12.31 × 20 × 52 ÷ 12 Approx. EUR 1,066.87/month
40 hours per week First 35 hours are normal hours, the other 5 hours are overtime Cannot simply calculate 12.31 × 40 while ignoring overtime

Wages are usually paid at least monthly with a payslip. Basic pay, fixed bonuses, overtime pay, expense reimbursements, meal vouchers, transport allowances and benefits in kind should be listed separately.

Employers usually bear at least 50% of the employee's public transport subscription cost, and this obligation cannot be deducted from the SMIC.

The employee's gross-to-net salary must be calculated monthly by the payroll system based on basic pension, supplementary pension, CSG/CRDS, the employee's supplementary health contribution and income tax withholding. Companies should not promise a fixed net salary.

7. Working Hours, Overtime and Recordkeeping

Item 2026 General Rule Employer Action
Statutory full-time hours 35 hours per week, 151.67 hours per month, 1,607 hours per year Above 35 hours requires overtime or RTT arrangements
Daily working time cap Usually 10 hours Exceptions require a legal, agreement or authority basis
Weekly working time cap 48 hours in a single week, average 44 hours over 12 consecutive weeks Establish rolling monitoring
Daily rest Usually 11 consecutive hours Assess cross-time-zone meetings and on-call together
Weekly rest Usually at least 24 hours plus the daily 11 hours Sunday work requires a sector exception or statutory basis
Overtime rates Without an agreement, hours 36 to 43 are +25%, and from hour 44 +50% Agreements may set different rates, but usually not below 10%

For example, for a 35-hour employee with a monthly salary of EUR 3,200, the reference hourly wage is:

3,200 ÷ 151.67 = EUR 21.10

If the employee works 5 hours of overtime in a week and there is no more specific agreement, the example overtime pay is:

21.10 × 5 × 125% = EUR 131.88

The employer must also check the annual overtime quota, compensatory rest and the applicable collective agreement.

Cross-time-zone meetings, work from home, on-call time and travel time may also affect working time and rest compliance. Companies should keep verifiable scheduling, overtime and rest records.

8. Public Holidays, Annual Leave and Other Statutory Leave

Employees generally accrue 2.5 working days (jours ouvrables) of annual leave per effective month of work, totaling 30 working days, or 5 weeks, for a full year.

Part-time employees in principle accrue at the same 2.5 days per month and cannot have this directly halved because they work fewer weekly hours.

Scenario Statutory Basis Employer Action
Normal accrual 2.5 working days per effective month of work Explain whether the company manages by working days or actual days worked
Less than one year Accrues monthly from the start date Cannot require 12 months of service before annual leave is available
Ordinary sick leave Usually 2 working days per month, maximum 24 days per year Keep separate records from work injury and occupational disease
Work injury or occupational disease Usually still accrues at 2.5 days per month Maintain based on certificates and statutory scope
Sick leave preventing leave taking Usually has a 15-month carryover window After return to work, inform the employee in writing of the balance and deadline
Cash payment while employed Statutory annual leave is in principle for actual rest Cannot routinely substitute cash for leave
Departure Untaken annual leave is compensated List separately on the payslip and final settlement receipt

Holiday pay should compare the following two methods and pay the result more favorable to the employee:

  • The one-tenth rule: holiday pay equals one-tenth of the gross salary in the reference period;
  • The maintained-salary rule: calculated according to the salary the employee would have received if continuing to work normally.

9. Employer Social Contributions, Mandatory Benefits and Taxes

Employers are responsible for the DPAE, social security registration, monthly nominal social declaration (DSN), withholding employee contributions and income tax, paying employer contributions, and implementing supplementary health insurance, occupational health and work injury insurance.

The 2026 annual social security ceiling (PASS) is EUR 48,060 and the monthly social security ceiling (PMSS) is EUR 4,005.

Basic pension Employer 2.11% on full salary + 8.55% within PMSS 0.40% on full salary + 6.90% within PMSS PMSS is EUR 4,005
Family allowances Employer full rate 5.25%; relief must be calculated under current 2026 rules None Pay level and rules such as RGDU

French relief mechanisms in 2026 involve annual and mid-year parameter changes. Health and family allowance items cannot be quoted manually using only one simplified rate. Formal calculations should use current URSSAF rules and payroll systems.

10. Local Employees and Foreign Employees

Local and foreign employees in principle apply the same French labor law, collective agreements, wages, working hours, leave and social insurance standards, but foreign employees also involve work permits, residence status, social insurance coverage, tax residency and post-departure immigration implications.

Employers should verify before the employee starts work whether the right to work and the contracting entity are consistent. Employer of Record (EOR) services do not automatically have sponsorship capacity and cannot replace lawful work authorization.

Verification Item Local Employee Foreign Employee
Labor law and collective agreement Applies Also applies
Minimum wage and job wage Applies Also applies
Social insurance Insured under French rules Need to verify French coverage, international agreements or posting arrangements
Right to work Usually no additional work permit required Verify residence and work authorization before onboarding
Tax residency Determined by individual circumstances Needs assessment based on residence and cross-border work arrangements
Impact of departure Mainly labor and unemployment issues May also affect residence and work permit

Cross-border remote work may also trigger tax, social insurance, permanent establishment, work authorization and data transfer risks in the place where work is actually performed, and cannot be carried out under a French employment contract alone.

11. Remote Work, Data Privacy and Employee Records

Remote work arrangements should state the work location, equipment, expenses, working hours, accident reporting, information security, data processing and return-to-office mechanism. A French employment contract does not mean the employee can work remotely long term from any country.

The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and CNIL rules require employers to have a lawful basis for processing and to observe transparency, minimization, retention periods and information security principles.

Because the employment relationship is one of subordination, employee consent usually cannot serve as the sole basis for data processing.

Monitoring, screen recording, email review, location tracking and AI performance tools must be proportionate to the purpose, and the information procedure for employees and the Social and Economic Committee (CSE) must be followed.

Record Category Main Content
Recruitment and onboarding Recruitment notice, candidate information, DPAE and right-to-work verification
Employment contract Contract, renewal, role, salary, working hours and remote work changes
Payroll and tax Payslips, DSN, income tax withholding and social contributions
Working time and leave Scheduling, overtime, RTT, annual leave, sick leave and family leave
Health and safety DUERP, occupational health, training and accident records
Employee relations Performance, warnings, discipline, investigations and employee statements
Termination documents Reasons for termination, procedure, settlement, certificates and asset recovery

Companies should set reasonable retention periods and access rights for different records, and must not retain employee personal data indefinitely.

12. Termination of Employment, Compensation and Final Settlement

Probation termination, ordinary dismissal, economic dismissal, serious misconduct, fixed-term expiry, early termination of a fixed-term contract and mutually agreed termination are different routes and cannot substitute for one another.

Outside probation, ordinary dismissal must have a real and serious reason (cause réelle et sérieuse).

Scenario Procedure and Notice Settlement Focus
Employer termination during probation 24 hours, 48 hours, 2 weeks or 1 month notice depending on length of service Wages, annual leave and compensation for insufficient notice
Employee termination during probation Usually 24 or 48 hours notice Wages, annual leave, assets and departure documents
Ordinary individual dismissal Written invitation, interview after at least 5 working days, dismissal letter usually at least 2 working days later Notice period, annual leave, statutory or collective agreement compensation and bonuses
Employee resignation Intent must be clear and unambiguous; notice mainly based on collective agreement, contract or custom Wages, annual leave, notice period and certificates
CDD expiry Expires according to contract; where applicable, 10% or collective agreement 6% precarious employment allowance Wages, annual leave, expiry compensation and certificates
Early CDD termination Only statutory routes; unlawful termination may require compensation through the end of the contract Risk of remaining wages and final settlement
Economic dismissal Economic reason, adaptation and redeployment, selection order, CSE, CSP or PSE depending on scale Special procedure, priority rehiring, notice and compensation
Serious or gross misconduct Disciplinary procedure must still be followed; notice and statutory dismissal compensation may not be paid Wages for work performed and annual leave usually still must be settled
Mutually agreed termination Voluntarily signed by both parties, with withdrawal period and authority approval Specific compensation, wages, annual leave, bonuses and documents

Statutory dismissal compensation generally requires at least 8 months of continuous service, except in cases such as serious or gross misconduct.

The minimum standard is generally:

Length of Service Statutory Minimum Dismissal Compensation
First 10 years One quarter of a month's reference salary per year of service
Portion over 10 years One third of a month's reference salary per year of service

Where the collective agreement provides a more favorable standard, the more favorable one applies.

Taking a CDI employee with a monthly reference salary of EUR 4,000, 6 years of service and ordinary individual dismissal, assuming a 2-month notice period paid by the employer but not worked, EUR 1,200 untaken leave compensation, EUR 500 earned bonus, and no more favorable collective agreement compensation:

Item Calculation Amount
Wages for the current month worked Assume full month EUR 4,000
Payment in lieu of notice 4,000 × 2 EUR 8,000
Statutory dismissal compensation 4,000 × 1/4 × 6 EUR 6,000
Untaken leave compensation Calculated on balance EUR 1,200
Earned bonus Per plan EUR 500
Example gross settlement total Sum of the above EUR 19,700

This example does not include tax treatment, more favorable collective agreement compensation, dispute settlement or protected employee risk.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Does France only have the SMIC as the wage floor?
No. Employers should compare the SMIC with the applicable collective agreement job minimum wage and apply the higher standard. Some collective agreements also provide for job classification, seniority increases, bonuses and allowances.

Can French employer cost be calculated at a fixed 45%?
It cannot be used as a final quote. Actual cost depends on salary, social security ceiling, relief, work injury rate, supplementary insurance, company headcount, location and collective agreement.

Can an employee be dismissed immediately without reason during probation?
It cannot be summarized that way. Probation termination must still comply with advance notice, anti-discrimination and special protection requirements, and genuine records related to job performance should be retained.

Is 10% precarious employment allowance always payable when a CDD expires?
Not necessarily. The common standard is 10%, but a collective agreement may reduce it to 6% when accompanied by training conditions, and certain statutory exceptions do not require payment.

Can a CDD be terminated immediately because the client project ends early?
It cannot terminate automatically. After probation, early termination of a CDD is limited to statutory routes such as mutual agreement, the employee moving to a CDI, serious misconduct, force majeure or medical unfitness. Unlawful early termination may result in compensation for wages through the end of the contract.

Can the reason be omitted after paying dismissal compensation?
No. Outside probation, ordinary dismissal must have a real and serious reason and follow the interview, written notice and applicable collective agreement procedures.

Is all work on French public holidays paid double?
No. May 1 in principle applies special paid rest and double pay for necessary work; whether other public holidays are days off or paid at a premium usually depends on statutory exceptions, collective agreements and company custom.

Can using an EOR automatically resolve French work permits?
No. The EOR solution and immigration work authorization must be verified separately. An EOR does not automatically have sponsorship capacity.
