Beware of Recruitment Scams

Last updated: 12 October 2026

At Double Alpha HR, the security of your job search is extremely important to us. With an increase in reported job scams and instances of employment fraud across the UK and Europe, we have set out the best ways to stay secure.

Job and employment scams trick you into handing over money or sensitive personal information by offering you jobs that do not exist, often claiming to be from a reputable organisation — including Double Alpha HR.

An employment scam can include being contacted by somebody claiming to be a Double Alpha HR consultant via email, social media, SMS, WhatsApp, or on a job board, who may ask you to pass over personal details or financial information.

Job and employment scams have become increasingly sophisticated, but knowing the tell-tale signs could prevent you from becoming a victim.

Warning Signs: How to Spot a Job or Employment Scam

If you receive communications regarding a job opportunity, the following warning signs could indicate a potential scam:

  • You come across an advertisement, or receive an email, message, letter, or phone call, offering you a guaranteed income or job.
  • There is a suspicious profile image, or a lack of genuine profile information, activity, or contact details.
  • The message claims that large sums of money can be made with little effort using your personal computer, or guarantees large returns.
  • The message asks you to provide personal details or pay a fee for more information about the job or for start-up materials.
  • You're asked to transfer money on behalf of someone else, which may be money laundering.
  • You receive an unsolicited message from a "recruiter" using a generic email address (such as @yahoo or @hotmail) as the point of contact.
  • You're asked to provide personal details or money, possibly in the form of an "administrative fee" or "accreditation costs".
  • You're immediately offered a high-paying job without an interview — if an offer seems too good to be true, it likely is.
  • The job advert is poorly written and contains spelling or grammatical errors.

What to Do If You Suspect a Job Scam

If you suspect you have been targeted by a recruitment scam, please report it to Action Fraud, the UK's national reporting centre for fraud and cybercrime. If you are based in the EU, you can report it to your local police or consumer protection authority.

Please also let us know by emailing Business@doublealphahr.com, so we can warn other candidates about the scam.

If You Have Been the Victim of a Job Scam

If you have already exchanged personal information or money with a suspected scammer:

Act immediately:
  • Report it to Action Fraud (UK) or your local authority (EU).
  • Contact your bank immediately if you have transferred any money, and ask them to freeze or reverse the transaction.
  • Change any passwords you may have shared, especially if you used them on other websites.
  • Be alert to follow-up scams — scammers often target victims a second time.
  • If you've shared your National Insurance number, passport details, or other identity documents, consider reporting the identity theft to the relevant authorities.

How to Protect Yourself

  • Never pay money to apply for a job or to be registered with a recruitment agency.
  • Verify the identity of anyone who contacts you about a role — call the company back on a number you find independently.
  • Check that the email domain matches the company's official website.
  • Be cautious about sharing personal documents (passport, bank details, NI number) before you've met the recruiter and confirmed the role is real.
  • Trust your instincts — if something feels wrong, it probably is.
